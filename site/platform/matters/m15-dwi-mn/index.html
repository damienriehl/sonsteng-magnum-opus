01

Introduction

You are an associate at Ellingboe & Ravndal LLP, and your client is Travis Wenzloff, a forty-three-year-old commercial HVAC technician charged in the Minnesota District Court for Sherburne County with Third-Degree Driving While Impaired. Travis holds a Class A commercial driver's license and drives a service truck to job sites every day, so his livelihood rides entirely on that credential. On a subzero January night, after a fourteen-hour double shift and a stop for beers on the way home, he was pulled over on Highway 10 following a citizen's report of a weaving vehicle. A deputy ran field sobriety tests on an ice-packed shoulder in extreme cold, and an evidentiary breath test at the jail reported an alcohol concentration of 0.09 — just over the per-se line. Travis tells you he was exhausted and distracted, not drunk, and that a prescription cough syrup and a mouthwash he used before driving may have skewed the reading. A prior impaired-driving incident several years back has pushed the stakes higher and threatens his commercial license for good. This exercise puts you in the first client interview and the strategic work that follows. Your job is to gather the facts — including the ones Travis is ashamed to volunteer — spot the issues in the stop, the field tests, and the breath sample, counsel a frightened client whose career is on the line, and build a plan that treats the license as the real prize. Everything you need is in the case file; the law is yours to research.

02

Learning Objectives

By completing this exercise you will practice and be assessed on a defined set of lawyering skills and tasks. First, fact gathering (SK-LP-07 / TSK-022, TSK-023, TSK-025): conduct a client interview that builds a reliable timeline of the shift, the drinking, the stop, the field tests, and the breath test, and that earns the sensitive facts — the fatigue-and-phone distraction behind the weaving, the mouthwash used to mask the smell of beer, an uncounted third drink, and a prior offense the client cannot bring himself to raise — which surface only when the client trusts you. Second, diagnosis and issue-spotting (SK-LP-01 / TSK-001, TSK-004): identify the legal vulnerabilities in the reasonable-suspicion basis for the stop, in field sobriety tests run in extreme cold on ice, and in a 0.09 breath reading exposed to mouth-alcohol and margin-of-error challenges. Third, procedural framework (SK-LP-04): track the two parallel proceedings — the criminal charge and the civil implied-consent license revocation — and their separate clocks. Fourth, counseling a distressed client (SK-LP-10 / TSK-034, TSK-037): address Travis's fear for his CDL and his family's income, explain what happens next, and set expectations without over-promising. Fifth, negotiation strategy (SK-LP-13 / TSK-044, TSK-045): prepare a plea-and-negotiation plan that protects the commercial license. Sixth, professional responsibility (SK-LP-16 / TSK-060): observe the no-contact rule with the represented arresting deputy and stay within the scope of the flat-fee engagement. These objectives map directly to the grading rubric for the matter.

Rehearsal runs are unlimited; the graded events are the interview, the written work, and the negotiation.

Procedural and Factual History

The stop and arrest occurred on the night of Friday, January 17, 2025, on eastbound Highway 10 in Sherburne County during a subzero cold snap. Deputy Reid Kallestad of the Sherburne County Sheriff's Office responded to a citizen's 911 report of a dark pickup weaving, located a matching vehicle, followed it for roughly a mile and a half, observed it touch the fog line once, and stopped it at 10:58 p.m. He conducted standardized field sobriety tests on an ice-packed shoulder in extreme cold, administered a preliminary roadside breath test, and arrested Travis Wenzloff. At the Sherburne County Jail a certified operator administered an evidentiary breath test on a DataMaster DMT, which reported an alcohol concentration of 0.09. Travis was read the Implied Consent Advisory and served with a notice of license revocation. Because his driving record reflects a prior impaired-driving incident, the State charged the current offense as Third-Degree Driving While Impaired. Travis was released the following morning; his vehicle had been towed. He retained Ellingboe & Ravndal LLP on January 22, 2025. A not-guilty plea was entered, the defense served a demand for discovery, filed a timely petition for judicial review of the implied-consent revocation, and requested a contested omnibus hearing on probable cause and suppression. As of the simulation date, the omnibus hearing is pending, the implied-consent review is pending, and no plea has been reached. The commercial-license consequence remains the client's overriding concern.

07

Considerations

Several threads deserve early attention. The stop rests on a citizen's weaving report plus limited independent observation — a single touch of the fog line over a mile and a half of following. Consider how reliable the tip was, whether the reporting motorist could even confirm the stopped truck was the one she watched, and how much the deputy corroborated on his own; the dashcam is the record that tests all of this. The field sobriety tests were performed on an ice-packed shoulder at roughly four below zero, in wind, by a man who had just worked a fourteen-hour day and who lives with a reflux condition — each of which bears on how much weight balance-based testing can carry. The breath sample reads 0.09, only just over the per-se line, so the instrument's stated measurement uncertainty and a possible mouth-alcohol event — an alcohol-based cough syrup, an alcohol-containing mouthwash used minutes before driving, reflux during the observation period, and a question whether that period was truly continuous — are not academic; they may be the difference between a per-se case and no per-se case. Weigh suppression against negotiation, but never lose sight of the two clocks: the criminal charge and the civil implied-consent revocation move separately, and the revocation reaches the client's license first. Above all, keep the commercial-license consequence at the center: for a CDL holder a second qualifying incident is a career-ending, lifetime disqualification, and that — not jail — is what your client actually needs you to fight. Mind the no-contact rule as you gather the other side's account.

08

Substantive Information

This is an impaired-driving prosecution in Minnesota, elevated above a first-offense level by a prior incident within the look-back period, in which the State also pursues an administrative revocation of the client's license through the implied-consent process. You will need to research, in the assigned jurisdiction, the standards that govern each pressure point in this file: the level of suspicion required for a lawful traffic stop, and how a citizen tip together with an officer's own observations does or does not supply it; the evidentiary treatment and known limitations of standardized field sobriety tests, particularly when administered off level ground, in extreme cold, and on a fatigued subject; the foundational requirements and the margin-of-error and mouth-alcohol challenges applicable to evidentiary breath testing, including the significance of a pre-test observation period and of alcohol-containing oral products and reflux; and the implied-consent framework that governs both the admissibility of the breath sample and the administrative revocation, along with the separate and heightened consequences that attach to a commercial driver's license. You should also research the mechanics of charge reduction and plea negotiation where a prior offense drives the degree of the charge, and the professional-conduct rule governing communication with a person represented by counsel, which controls how you may approach the State's law-enforcement witness. The case file gives you the facts; the controlling law is the object of your research and is not supplied here.

Dear Mr. Wenzloff:

Thank you for retaining Ellingboe & Ravndal LLP. This letter sets out the terms of our representation so that we both understand what we will do and what it will cost.

Scope of representation. We will represent you in the pending Third-Degree Driving While Impaired charge in the Minnesota District Court for Sherburne County, and in the related civil proceeding to revoke your driver's license through the implied-consent process. Our work includes advising you, reviewing the State's evidence, filing and litigating a timely petition for judicial review of the license revocation, appearing at your criminal hearings, requesting and litigating a contested omnibus hearing on probable cause and suppression if warranted, and negotiating with the prosecution toward the best available resolution. Because you hold a commercial driver's license, we will also advise you on the commercial-license consequences and keep them at the center of our strategy. A full jury trial, a trial of the implied-consent petition to conclusion, or an appeal would be a separate engagement we would discuss with you in advance.

Fee. Your fee for the representation described above is a flat fee of $3,000. A flat fee means the price is fixed regardless of the number of hours the matter requires; you will not receive an hourly bill. You have deposited $3,000 into our client trust account as an advance against this fee. We will hold those funds in trust and draw against them only as the fee is earned through the stages of your case; any portion not earned if the matter concludes early will be refunded to you. Ordinary costs such as copying and postage are included; unusual out-of-pocket expenses, such as an independent breath-test expert, would be discussed and approved by you before we incur them.

What we ask of you. Please be candid with us — everything you tell us is confidential and protected — respond promptly when we need information or a decision, and keep every court date. Some of the deadlines in your case, especially on the license side, are short and cannot be missed. The more complete your account, the better we can defend you.

Your goals. We understand your priority is protecting your commercial driver's license and your ability to keep working, protecting your family's income, and avoiding a jail sentence. We will keep those goals at the center of our strategy while giving you honest advice about what the evidence allows.

If these terms are acceptable, please sign below and return one copy. We are glad to be your lawyers.

Sincerely,
