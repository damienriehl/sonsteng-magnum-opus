01

Introduction

You are an associate at Ellingboe & Ravndal LLP, and your client is Devon Halvard, a twenty-nine-year-old line cook charged in the Meridian District Court, Halden County, with a first-offense Driving While Impaired after a taillight stop in Norsholm. Devon left a coworker's after-hours birthday dinner, was stopped for an inoperative rear taillight, performed field sobriety tests on an icy, sloped shoulder, and gave an evidentiary breath sample that came back exactly at the per-se limit of 0.08. He tells you he had two beers over about three hours and felt fine to drive. He is not worried about the theory of the case; he is worried about the one thing that keeps him employed — his driver's license — because there is no bus running when his kitchen closes. This exercise puts you in the first client interview and then in the strategic and negotiation work that follows. Your job is to gather the facts, including the ones Devon is embarrassed to volunteer, spot the issues that give the defense leverage, counsel a frightened first-time client honestly, and build a negotiation plan. Everything you need is in the case file; the law is yours to research.

02

Learning Objectives

By completing this exercise you will practice and be assessed on a defined set of lawyering skills and tasks. First, fact gathering (SK-LP-07 / TSK-022, TSK-023, TSK-025): conduct a client interview that builds a reliable timeline of the evening and the arrest and that earns the sensitive facts — a diagnosed vestibular condition, a fourteen-hour double shift, acid reflux immediately before the breath test, and an omitted birthday toast — which surface only when the client trusts you. Second, diagnosis and issue-spotting (SK-LP-01 / TSK-001, TSK-004): identify the legal vulnerabilities in the stop, the field sobriety tests, and the breath sample. Third, counseling a distressed client (SK-LP-10 / TSK-037): address Devon's fear about his license and record, explain what happens next, and set expectations without over-promising. Fourth, negotiation strategy (SK-LP-13 / TSK-044, TSK-045): prepare a plea and charge-negotiation plan, including a realistic path to a reduced charge. Fifth, professional responsibility (SK-LP-16 / TSK-060): observe the no-contact rule with the represented arresting officer and stay within the scope of the flat-fee engagement. These objectives map directly to the grading rubric for the matter.

03

Activities

This matter unfolds across several graded and rehearsal activities that mirror the Practicum's DWI arc. (1) Preliminary case analysis. Read the full case file and produce a short case-analysis memorandum identifying the charge, the elements the State must prove, and the defense's best factual and legal openings. (2) Client interview. Conduct a recorded interview with Devon Halvard through the simulated-client engine. Open with rapport and confidentiality, funnel from open-ended narrative to targeted probing, and reach the emotional and practical concerns driving him. Some facts will not come out unless you earn them. (3) Strategy and negotiation plan. Draft a strategic settlement-and-negotiation plan (SSNP) that weighs suppression against a negotiated resolution and identifies your leverage and your exposure. (4) Plea negotiation. Conduct a simulated negotiation with the prosecution toward a resolution such as a reduction to careless driving. (5) Contested omnibus preparation. Outline the suppression arguments you would raise at the omnibus hearing. Rehearsal runs are unlimited; the graded events are the interview, the written work, and the negotiation.

07

Considerations

Several threads deserve early attention. The stop rests on a single equipment violation; consider whether the record supports the asserted taillight failure and what the dashcam actually shows. The field sobriety tests were performed on a shoulder later measured at roughly a seven-percent grade, packed with ice and snow, in eighteen-degree weather, after your client had worked a fourteen-hour shift and while managing a diagnosed inner-ear condition — each of which bears on how much weight the tests can carry. The breath sample reads exactly at 0.08, the per-se line, so the instrument's stated measurement uncertainty and a possible mouth-alcohol event immediately before the test are not academic; they may be the difference between a per-se case and no per-se case. Weigh suppression against negotiation: a motion that knocks out the stop or the sample may end the case, but a negotiated reduction to careless driving may better protect the license your client actually cares about. Consider the collateral license consequence as its own problem with its own clock. Finally, keep the client's non-legal goals — his job, his dignity, his fear of the record — at the center of your counseling, and mind the no-contact rule as you gather the other side's account.

08

Substantive Information

This is a first-offense impaired-driving prosecution in Meridian, a state whose impaired-driving scheme sets a per-se threshold at an alcohol concentration of 0.08 within two hours of driving and grades the offense by aggravating factors, with a first offense and no aggravators sitting at the lowest (fourth) degree. You will need to research, in the jurisdiction assigned, the standards that govern each pressure point in this file: the level of suspicion required for a lawful traffic stop and how an equipment violation figures in it; the evidentiary treatment and known limitations of standardized field sobriety tests, particularly when administered off level ground and in winter conditions; the foundational requirements and margin-of-error/mouth-alcohol challenges applicable to evidentiary breath testing; and the implied-consent framework that governs both the admissibility of the breath sample and the administrative revocation of the license. You should also research the mechanics of charge reduction and plea negotiation in a first-offense impaired-driving case, and the professional-conduct rule governing communication with a person represented by counsel, which controls how you may approach the State's law-enforcement witness. The case file gives you the facts; the controlling law is the object of your research and is not supplied here.
