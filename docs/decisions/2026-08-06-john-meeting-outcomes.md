## 4. Midstate and Rogers — DECIDED, with a naming correction

**"Midstate and Rogers." Not "Midstate v. Rogers."** John corrected this twice, and then
corrected Damien's follow-up assumption that it might be two cases:

> "No, it's one case. It's Midstate and Rogers. It's an arbitration… arbitrations are not
> versus."

**Converted to court it becomes "Rogers v. Midstate"** — Rogers is the plaintiff there —
**and the remedy changes from reinstatement to money damages.** That is not bookkeeping.
The same facts entered through a different door produce a different caption, a different
moving party, and a different thing worth asking for. It is the entire argument for one
model file over five exercises.

**The settlement layer is the negotiation and mediation material.** If Rogers wins, it
resolves in a large negotiated settlement — *"he gets so many years pay, the kids get to go
to college."* John: *"that's all in there, it's all about negotiations and mediation."* It
lives in the case file and in the video on the CD. This is teaching material, not backstory.

Build order: arbitration first to full depth, then trial, negotiation, mediation, and
contract interpretation onto the same facts.

## 9. Credit and assessment — DECIDED, with the numbers corrected

**Two tracks.** Log the hours, and separately propose the reform.

- **The log** — weekly hours spent on projects (including class time) and hours the student
  could bill a client. Schools set whatever credits they want in the meantime; John's read
  of the status quo is that *"they make assumptions which are way off, and we're teaching
  inefficiency."*
- **The proposal** — competency-based credit, pitched to schools and the ABA, evidenced by
  the list of what students demonstrably learn *quickly* rather than inefficiently. The log
  is what makes that argument with data instead of assertion.

**The assessment scale, in John's own numbers.** He took it from the University of
Minnesota, and the earlier note recording a floor of 3 was wrong:

| Score (of 7) | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 7 | Rare — *"you're never going to get many sevens"* |
| 6 | The redo target |
| **4** | **Average, and therefore competent — the credit floor** |
| 3 | Failure |
| 1–2 | Effectively never given |

- **Under 6, a student may redo that section** to bring it to a 6.
- **The credit floor is an average of 4**, not 3.
- **Instructors and schools may set any thresholds they want**, so both numbers ship as
  per-school, per-instructor settings, never constants.
- **Watch the presentation.** John: students read a 4 as terrible *"because that's a
  midline, you know, like a C."* Competent needs to look like competent.
