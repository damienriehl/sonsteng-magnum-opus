04

Model case file — Midstate and Rogers

Expanded
Adopted as the model file. Build the arbitration first, then the other four postures onto the same facts. And the name is not what the sheet said.
  • No “versus” — it is “Midstate and Rogers,” because it is an arbitration and an arbitration has no v. Every artifact, filename, page title, and packet header uses the conjunctive form.
  • Court flips both — converted to a court posture the matter becomes “Rogers v. Midstate” (Rogers is the plaintiff there), and the remedy changes from reinstatement to money damages.
  • That is the pedagogy — this is not a labeling detail. The same facts entered through a different door produce a different caption, a different moving party, and a different thing worth asking for. It is the reason one file beats five exercises.
  • Incorporated — John's originals come into Legal Practicum under the item 3 license, in a separately-licensed directory marked © John O. Sonsteng.

AI briefings and recorded speakers

Decided
AI briefings are the default layer; a curated set of recorded human speakers is the marquee layer.

09

Credit hours and assessment

Expanded
Both tracks. Log the hours and write the competency-based credit proposal — the log is the proposal's evidence.
  • Track one — the weekly log — students submit hours spent on projects, including class time, and hours they could bill a client. Course credits stay conventional for the registrar and the ABA. The gap between the two numbers is the lesson.
  • Track two — the proposal — a competency-based credit proposal schools can put to their own accreditors. The list of what students demonstrably learn quickly rather than inefficiently is the exhibit that supports the claim, which is what turns a fifty-year argument into one made with data.
  • Thresholds are settings — not constants. Per school, per instructor.
