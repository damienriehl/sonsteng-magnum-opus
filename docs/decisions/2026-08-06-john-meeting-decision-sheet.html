Catalog and distribution

Revised
Everything free. The practicum is a public repo; the catalog shows each case's procedural and factual history, and anyone downloads what they want.
  • There is no paywall — “ordering” in the Aug 5 email meant downloading. John: I'm ready to give it. I have no idea — he had no position on money and no interest in taking one.

09

Credit and assessment

Numbers corrected
Log the hours and write the competency-credit proposal. The scale is 7 points, and the floor is 4 — not 3.
  • Two tracks — weekly hours worked and hours billable, while schools keep setting credits however they like; plus a competency-based proposal to schools and the ABA, evidenced by what students demonstrably learn quickly.
  • The earlier floor of 3 was wrong — John's scale comes from the University of Minnesota.
  • Presentation matters — students read a 4 as terrible because that's a midline, you know, like a C. Competent has to look competent.
Score of 7Meaning
7Rare — “you're never going to get many sevens”
6The redo target: anything under 6 may be redone to reach it
4Average, therefore competent — the credit floor
3Failure
1–2Effectively never given
  • Both are settings — instructors and schools set whatever thresholds they want, so neither number ships as a constant.
10

Alumni and the school

Reversed
Alumni do not assess — the AI does. And the school gets approached after the build, not alongside it.
  • Both halves flipped — John, unprompted: they wouldn't be giving feedback because you can't trust them. The AI is going to do it. Alumni involvement survives only as a recommendation to schools for engagement.
  • Sequence is deliberate — finish the practicum, get Midstate in and right, then go to Greg Buck, maybe Frank Harris, maybe the dean: here is what we built, get on board or we roll out with Stanford and others.
  • John named why it works — that's a good way to negotiate without negotiating. I didn't want to go behind their back. This is up front… a pleasant way rather than confrontational. Don't front-run it; the leverage is the finished thing.
