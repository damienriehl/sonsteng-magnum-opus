03

Copyright and license

4 min
  • John's position — I would like to maintain the copyright and I already own all the needed materials. Both are compatible with everything the project needs.
  • Ownership is not the question — John stays the sole copyright owner. The only question is what license the Practicum software gets, and CC-BY answers it without transferring anything.
  • Attribution is the teeth — CC-BY requires the Sonsteng credit to travel with every copy, every adaptation, every school's derivative, permanently. That is a stronger name-recognition instrument than a locked copyright nobody can use.
    • Two layers, two licenses — John's content under CC-BY 4.0; the software the project wrote stays MIT. They sit side by side without conflict, exactly as the repo already anticipates.
    • Unblocks the originals — the July 18 record deferred John's Midstate/Trialbook materials purely because the license was unsettled. This settles it, and item 4 becomes buildable.
Confirm before relying on it: get John's I already own all the needed materials in writing, covering (a) anything produced under a school appointment or with school resources, and (b) the other lawyers who appear on the CD in item 5 — their briefings are their copyrights unless cleared.
Say it out loud once: CC-BY lets anyone redistribute free of charge. The website ordering in item 8 therefore sells assembly, currency, and support — not exclusivity. That is a workable business; it just isn't a copyright moat.

Model case file — Midstate University v. Rogers

3 min
  • John's call — The model case file should be Midstate University and Rogers. It is an arbitration that can also be used for a court trial, negotiation, mediation, and contract interpretation.
  • One file, five modes — that reuse is the whole argument. A single fact pattern a student re-enters five ways teaches that posture changes strategy, which no five separate exercises can show.
  • Now buildable — the platform currently ships 20 original matters and deliberately excluded the Sonsteng originals pending the license. Item 3 removes that block; Midstate lands in its own separately-licensed directory with John's copyright marked on every artifact.
  • Scope is the real question — five full postures is five times the packet work. Building arbitration first and proving the reuse before committing to all five is the cheaper path to the same claim.

06

AI briefings and recorded speakers

5 min
  • John's assumption is correct — I hope I am correct in assuming that the briefings can be conducted using artificial intelligence. If this is true, the game changes. It is true. It works today, and the platform already runs AI-delivered client interviews with debrief and critique.
  • What it already gives him — every requirement in his second email falls out of it:
    • Consistent format — The briefings must be consistent in format. A generated briefing is identical in shape every time; a room full of professors is not.
    • Printable and rewatchable — A student can print out the written portions and watch any part more than once. Script and transcript ship with every briefing by construction.
    • Presenter quality — One cannot assume that all professors are good at making presentations. This is the strongest argument for AI delivery, not against it: it removes the variance.
    • Revisable — a rule changes, the briefing updates. No re-recording, no rescheduling a speaker.
  • Where humans still win — John's extraordinary speakers recorded on a studio background carry authority and recruit schools. That is a marquee layer, not the baseline.

Vocabulary lock

1 min
  • Two terms, hard-won — John has already run the experiments on students and reported the results:
    • Assessment and feedback — I do not want to use the term grading.
    • Planning Guide and Checklist — Planning guide alone went unread; checklist alone made students think they had to do every item and tick it off. The combined term works.
  • Lock it, then stop revisiting — this is field-tested and costs one minute. Once locked it propagates through the site, the syllabi, and every rubric automatically.

Exercise catalog and how schools order

3 min
  • John's design — Rather than list all the exercises completely… we can provide a procedural and factual history for each one, allowing schools to choose the exercises they find helpful. The schools can then order the materials through our website.
  • The catalog is the marketing — a procedural and factual history is enough for a professor to know whether an exercise fits their course, and not enough to run it. That is the right line.
  • First two packages — John named them: the General Practice Practicum and the Small Business Practicum, syllabus and materials. Those are the reference implementations to ship first.
  • What ordering actually sells — under CC-BY (item 3) anyone may redistribute free. So the order button delivers assembly, print-ready packages, LMS-ready files, updates, and support — decide which of those before pricing anything.
09

Credit hours and the billable-hours log

3 min
  • John's diagnosis — The slower the learners are, the more time they spend. The system is geared to reward inefficiencies. Against practice, where if the lawyer does good work fast, the lawyer must charge the client a higher rate or make less per hour.
  • What he wants logged — Each week the student must submit the number of hours they spent working on projects. This includes class time. The students also submit how many hours they can bill a client. The gap between those two numbers is the lesson.
  • Log it without fighting the ABA — the weekly log delivers the entire pedagogy on its own. Making it the basis for credit turns a build into an accreditation campaign, and the two are separable.
  • What the log produces — hours per project, effective hourly rate at a stated fee, a flat-fee comparison, and cohort benchmarks. That is the instrument that answers John's real question: whether they can make a living being a lawyer.

Alumni as national faculty, and the board meeting

3 min
  • John's alumni idea — every school works with its alumni advisor to recruit alumni for assessment and feedback. He gives three reasons:
    • Recognition, not fundraising — involves the alumni in a way that recognizes their accomplishments and does not seek funding.
    • Giving follows — when alumni is involved this way, we can expect an increase in contributions.
    • National faculty — it also allows the school to say they have a national faculty in the practicum courses.
  • The institutional ask — John wants to meet Greg Buck, the new board chairman, plus Frank Harris and perhaps the dean, so that MHSL have the opportunity to be a national leader.
  • Don't make them choose sight-unseen — a pilot with real alumni assessors turns the board meeting from a proposal into a demonstration. But scheduling takes weeks, so start it now rather than after.
