# Ground-Truth Facts — Sofia Reyes v. Bradley Hutchins (m13)

*Master narrative for the auto-negligence capstone. Every material fact carries a sequential anchor. Student-facing; facts only, no law.*

## The parties and the setting

Sofia Reyes is a thirty-four-year-old rideshare driver who lives in the City of Belmar Springs, in Sunhaven County, Florida [m13.fact.001]. She has driven for a rideshare platform full-time for about three years, treating it as her sole source of income and averaging roughly fifty to sixty passenger trips a week [m13.fact.002]. She drives a 2019 four-door sedan that she owns outright and keeps in good repair [m13.fact.003].

Bradley Hutchins is a forty-one-year-old regional sales representative who lives two towns over and commutes daily through Belmar Springs [m13.fact.004]. He drives a large late-model SUV [m13.fact.005]. On the day of the collision he was driving home from a client visit at the end of a long workday [m13.fact.006].

The collision happened at the intersection of Camino Real Boulevard and 22nd Avenue, a four-lane signalized intersection with dedicated left-turn lanes and standard three-phase traffic signals [m13.fact.007]. The intersection sits in a mixed commercial-residential corridor with a posted speed limit of 35 miles per hour on Camino Real Boulevard [m13.fact.008].

## The collision

The crash occurred on the evening of Thursday, February 6, 2025, at approximately 5:50 p.m. [m13.fact.009]. The weather was clear and dry, but the sun was low on the western horizon, producing significant glare for westbound drivers on Camino Real Boulevard [m13.fact.010]. Reyes was traveling westbound on Camino Real Boulevard, entering the intersection to continue straight through [m13.fact.011]. Hutchins was traveling eastbound on Camino Real Boulevard and attempting to complete a left turn onto northbound 22nd Avenue [m13.fact.012].

Reyes says she entered the intersection on a solid green light, traveling at approximately 25 miles per hour, below the posted limit because she was carrying a passenger and driving cautiously into the sun glare [m13.fact.013]. Hutchins says he entered the intersection on a green light to set up his left turn, waited for a gap, and began his turn as the light changed to yellow, believing Reyes had time to stop [m13.fact.014]. Hutchins contends that Reyes accelerated through a stale, late-yellow-turning-red light rather than stopping, and therefore shares fault for the impact [m13.fact.015].

The front of Hutchins's SUV struck the passenger side (the right front and right door) of Reyes's sedan as she crossed the intersection — a classic "T-bone" or broadside impact [m13.fact.016]. The force spun Reyes's sedan roughly ninety degrees and pushed it into the far crosswalk [m13.fact.017]. Both airbags in Reyes's sedan deployed [m13.fact.018].

## The disputed traffic signal

The central factual dispute is which driver had the right of way when the two cars entered the intersection [m13.fact.019]. Reyes maintains she had a green through-light and that Hutchins turned left across her path without yielding [m13.fact.020]. Hutchins maintains he had begun a lawful turn and that Reyes ran a light that had already turned yellow, then red, against her [m13.fact.021]. Neither driver can state with precision how many seconds the light had been yellow at the moment of entry [m13.fact.022]. The intersection is not equipped with a red-light traffic camera, so no automated footage exists [m13.fact.023].

An independent eyewitness, Gerald Fontaine, was stopped in the northbound left-turn lane on 22nd Avenue facing the intersection and had a clear view of the signal facing Camino Real traffic [m13.fact.024]. Fontaine states that the Camino Real signal was green for through traffic and that the eastbound SUV turned left into the path of the westbound sedan, which he believes had the right of way [m13.fact.025]. Fontaine did not know either driver and gave his contact information to the investigating officer at the scene [m13.fact.026].

## What Reyes has not volunteered

In the two or three seconds before she entered the intersection, Reyes glanced down at her phone, mounted on the dash, because a new ride-request notification had just chimed [m13.fact.027]. She has been reluctant to mention this because she fears it makes the crash look like her fault, even though she believes she still had a green light and was watching the road [m13.fact.028]. Reyes also has a history of left-knee soreness from a recreational-league soccer injury around 2019, which she treated with rest and never had surgically evaluated [m13.fact.029].

## What Hutchins has not volunteered

Hutchins was reaching for a travel mug of coffee in the center console as he began his turn and momentarily took his eyes off the oncoming lane [m13.fact.030]. He has downplayed this because he believes the signal timing, not his attention, caused the crash [m13.fact.031].

## The investigation and citation

Officer Elaine Sturdivant of the Belmar Springs Police Department responded to the scene, interviewed both drivers and the eyewitness, and completed a Florida Traffic Crash Report [m13.fact.032]. Officer Sturdivant issued Hutchins a traffic citation for failure to yield the right of way while turning left, based on the physical evidence and the independent witness account [m13.fact.033]. The crash report notes clear weather, dry pavement, and sun glare as a contributing environmental condition [m13.fact.034]. No citation was issued to Reyes [m13.fact.035].

## Reyes's injuries and treatment

Reyes was transported by ambulance to the emergency department, where she complained of severe right-knee pain and could not bear weight [m13.fact.036]. Initial X-rays showed no fracture, and she was discharged with a knee immobilizer and a referral to an orthopedic specialist [m13.fact.037]. An MRI on February 20, 2025, revealed a torn medial meniscus in her right knee [m13.fact.038]. Dr. Nadia Emerson, an orthopedic surgeon, performed arthroscopic surgery to repair the meniscus on March 18, 2025 [m13.fact.039]. Reyes completed roughly ten weeks of physical therapy from late March through early June 2025 [m13.fact.040].

Dr. Emerson's records state that the meniscus tear is consistent with the mechanism of the collision, while acknowledging that Reyes reported prior intermittent knee soreness [m13.fact.041]. Dr. Emerson's report assesses that Reyes has reached maximum medical improvement with a permanent partial impairment of the right knee and an increased risk of future arthritis [m13.fact.042].

## Economic harm

Because of the surgery and recovery, Reyes was unable to drive for the rideshare platform for approximately ten weeks [m13.fact.043]. Her rideshare earnings statements show average weekly net earnings of about $850 in the months before the crash, supporting a lost-income claim of roughly $8,500 for the time she was off the road [m13.fact.044]. Her total medical bills for the emergency visit, imaging, surgery, and physical therapy came to approximately $42,000 [m13.fact.045]. Her sedan sustained roughly $9,800 in damage and was declared a total loss by her insurer [m13.fact.046].

## The no-fault / PIP insurance layer

Under Florida's no-fault motor-vehicle insurance framework, Reyes first submitted her medical bills and lost-wage claim to her own automobile insurer under her Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage [m13.fact.047]. Her PIP coverage carried the standard $10,000 limit, which was exhausted by a fraction of her medical bills, leaving the great majority of her medical expenses and all of her pain-and-suffering damages outside PIP [m13.fact.048]. Whether Reyes may step outside the no-fault system and pursue Hutchins directly for her remaining damages turns on whether her knee injury meets the statutory injury threshold — a question the firm must research and Reyes does not understand [m13.fact.049]. Reyes's own insurer initially delayed part of her PIP payout, requesting additional documentation, which added to her financial stress [m13.fact.050].

## Reyes's circumstances and the engagement

Reyes came to Ellingboe & Ravndal LLP on the referral of a former client [m13.fact.051]. At intake she was anxious and tearful, describing mounting bills, a totaled car she still owed nothing on but could not replace, and rent she had fallen behind on during the weeks she could not drive [m13.fact.052]. She is three weeks behind on her rent and is under real financial pressure to resolve the case quickly, which the firm must weigh against the value of a fuller recovery [m13.fact.053]. The firm agreed to represent Reyes on a contingency-fee basis, advancing litigation costs to be reimbursed from any recovery [m13.fact.054].

## Procedural posture

The firm filed a civil negligence complaint against Hutchins in the Circuit Court for Sunhaven County, Florida, seeking damages for Reyes's injuries, medical expenses, lost income, and property damage [m13.fact.055]. Hutchins, defended by counsel retained through his liability insurer, answered the complaint and raised comparative fault as an affirmative defense, alleging that Reyes ran the light and was distracted [m13.fact.056]. The parties are in written discovery, exchanging interrogatories and requests for production, and depositions of the two drivers and the eyewitness are being scheduled [m13.fact.057]. The firm has sent a settlement demand to Hutchins's liability insurer, which has responded with a low offer that assigns Reyes a substantial share of comparative fault [m13.fact.058]. The matter is being built toward a civil jury trial in the Circuit Court [m13.fact.059].
