## 1. Model Case Analysis (keyed to fact anchors)

### The plaintiff's theory of the case (our client, plaintiff-counsel)

This is a **right-of-way swearing contest won on independent evidence**. The intersection has no red-light camera [m13.fact.023], so liability turns on witness credibility and physical evidence — and here the plaintiff holds the stronger corroboration:

- **The independent eyewitness is the load-bearing asset.** Gerald Fontaine, stopped in the northbound left-turn lane with a clear view of the Camino Real signal, states the through-signal was **green** and that the eastbound SUV turned left into the westbound sedan's path [m13.fact.024], [m13.fact.025]. He knew neither driver and gave contact info at the scene [m13.fact.026] — a disinterested witness, the single most valuable liability fact.
- **The citation.** Officer Sturdivant cited **Hutchins** for failure to yield the right of way while turning left, based on physical evidence and the independent witness [m13.fact.033]; **no citation issued to Reyes** [m13.fact.035]. (Note for the student: a traffic citation's admissibility/weight is itself a research question, but as investigative fact it corroborates the theory.)
- **The physics corroborate.** The front of Hutchins's SUV struck the passenger side of Reyes's sedan — a classic broadside/T-bone [m13.fact.016] — spinning it ~90° into the far crosswalk [m13.fact.017], with both airbags deployed [m13.fact.018]. A strong answer ties point-of-impact + rotation + rest position to the competing accounts and shows the physical evidence fits a left-turning SUV striking a through-driver, not a speeder running a red.

**Negligence elements (the student must find and frame the law):** duty (ordinary care of a driver, heightened for a left-turning driver who must yield), breach (failure to yield [m13.fact.033]; the concealed coffee-mug reach [m13.fact.030]), causation (the broadside impact), and damages (below). The best answers organize the whole liability case in elements against the disputed signal facts [m13.fact.019].

### The comparative-fault problem (the heart of the exposure)

Florida moved to a **modified comparative-negligence** system in 2023 — a plaintiff more than 50% at fault is generally barred from recovery (Fla. Stat. § 768.81, as amended by Ch. 2023-15, Laws of Fla.) `[instructor: verify before teaching]`. This transforms two concealed/gated facts into case-threatening exposure:

- **Reyes glanced at her phone** in the two or three seconds before entering the intersection, for a ride-request chime [m13.fact.027] — concealed, and she fears it makes the crash look like her fault [m13.fact.028]. Distraction evidence lets the defense argue she failed to keep a proper lookout and pushes her fault share up.
- **The stale-yellow theory.** Hutchins claims Reyes accelerated through a late-yellow-turning-red light [m13.fact.015], [m13.fact.021]; neither driver can pin the seconds of yellow [m13.fact.022].

The A-grade student sees the **strategic stakes of the phone glance**: under modified comparative negligence, if a jury put Reyes over 50%, she recovers **nothing**. So the analysis must (a) counsel the client honestly that the fact is harmful and cannot be hidden, (b) develop it as a *momentary* glance while she still had a green and was watching the road, and (c) marshal Fontaine and the citation to keep her share well under the bar. Contrast the defense's mirror-image exposure: Hutchins concealed reaching for his coffee mug [m13.fact.030] and privately doubts he looked long enough before committing to the turn [m13.fact.031] — the defense's own distraction problem.

### The no-fault / PIP layer and the injury threshold

Florida's no-fault framework required Reyes to submit medicals and lost wages to her own PIP coverage first (Fla. Stat. §§ 627.730–627.7405) `[instructor: verify before teaching]`. Her $10,000 PIP limit was exhausted by a fraction of her ~$42,000 in medical bills [m13.fact.045], [m13.fact.048], leaving the bulk of medicals and **all** pain-and-suffering outside PIP. **Whether Reyes may step outside no-fault and sue Hutchins for non-economic damages turns on the statutory permanent-injury threshold** (Fla. Stat. § 627.737(2)) `[instructor: verify before teaching]`, which Reyes does not understand [m13.fact.049]. The gateway fact: Dr. Emerson's opinion that Reyes reached maximum medical improvement with a **permanent partial impairment** of the right knee and increased future-arthritis risk [m13.fact.042]. The A-answer ties that permanency opinion to *both* the threshold (unlocking the tort claim for pain and suffering) and the future-damages claim, and it addresses the causation soft spot: the tear is "consistent with the mechanism" but Reyes reported prior intermittent soreness [m13.fact.029], [m13.fact.041] — a prior-condition/eggshell issue the defense will exploit.

### Damages

Economic: ~$42,000 medical [m13.fact.045], ~$8,500 lost income (average net ~$850/wk × ~10 weeks) [m13.fact.044], ~$9,800 property (total-loss sedan) [m13.fact.046]. Non-economic: available **only if the threshold is met** — the surgery, the permanent impairment, and the arthritis risk drive the pain-and-suffering and future-medicals claim. A top answer flags PIP set-off/collateral-source issues at a high level and does not double-count the PIP-paid amounts.

### The A-grade conclusion

Liability strongly favors Reyes on the independent evidence (Fontaine + citation + physics); the live risk is **comparative-fault reduction**, not defeat, provided the phone-glance is managed candidly and her share kept under 50%. The permanency opinion opens the tort door past PIP for the substantial non-economic claim. The realistic recommendation weighs the client's genuine **financial pressure to resolve fast** [m13.fact.053] against the value of a fuller recovery: counsel honestly on the settlement trade-off, use the low insurer offer [m13.fact.058] as a floor to negotiate up from, and avoid letting the rent emergency stampede the client into an under-value settlement without informed consent.

- **c02.s02 Comparative-fault apportionment (40):** Assesses how the alleged late entry [m13.fact.015] and the phone glance [m13.fact.027] shift fault and reduce (or, past 50%, bar) recovery under § 768.81's modified system `[instructor: verify before teaching]`. ### c03 — Damages & no-fault / PIP analysis (55 pts)

- **Recognizes Hutchins is represented and does NOT interview him directly** [m13.fact.056]; routes contact through Petrakis/discovery under Rule 4-4.2 `[instructor: verify before teaching]`. Binary-ish: direct contact is a serious hit.

## 4. Grading Guidance

### A / B / C discriminators
- **A (309–325):** Builds liability on the independent eyewitness + citation + physics; runs a rigorous **modified-comparative-negligence** analysis that treats the phone glance as case-threatening and manages it candidly; ties the permanency opinion to *both* the § 627.737 threshold and future damages; flawless Rule 4.2 handling; counsels the financially pressured client toward an informed settlement decision rather than a panic settlement.
- **B (276–293):** Solid liability and damages; spots comparative fault and Rule 4.2; but underweights the phone glance's 50%-bar significance, or treats the PIP/threshold layer superficially, or coaches around the harmful fact instead of counseling on it.
- **C (~228):** Assumes a clean win; ignores or hides the comparative-fault exposure; muddles PIP with the tort claim; contacts Hutchins directly; or lets the rent emergency drive an uncounseled settlement.

### Common errors (watch for these)
1. **Contacting Hutchins directly** — the signature ethics failure (c06). Route through Petrakis.
2. **Missing the 50% cliff** — treating comparative fault as mere percentage reduction rather than a potential total bar under the 2023 amendment; failing to see the phone glance could zero out the recovery.
3. **Coaching the client to deny the phone glance** — an ethics and strategy failure; the fact will surface in discovery. Counsel candidly instead.
4. **Confusing PIP with the tort claim** — treating the $10k PIP as the recovery ceiling, or double-counting PIP-paid medicals; missing that the threshold is the gate to non-economic damages.
5. **Ignoring the prior-knee causation attack** — not planning for the "consistent with, but prior soreness" record [m13.fact.041].
6. **Letting the rent crisis stampede the settlement** — recommending the insurer's low offer without informed-consent counseling on the trade-off.

### Re-write demands (trigger a mandatory revision if)
- The student **interviewed Hutchins directly** — require a written Rule 4.2 analysis and a corrected plan routed through defense counsel.
- The analysis **omits the modified-comparative-negligence 50% bar** or misstates it as ordinary reduction — require a corrected comparative-fault analysis.
- The damages section **conflates PIP with the tort recovery** or ignores the § 627.737 threshold — require a corrected no-fault/threshold analysis.
- The client counseling **advises hiding or denying the phone glance** — require an ethically corrected counseling memo.
