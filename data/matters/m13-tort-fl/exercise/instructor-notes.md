# Instructor Notes — Reyes v. Hutchins (m13)

*For instructors only. Not part of the student packet. Citations below are instructor-verified at publish; verify against current Florida law before teaching.*

## Teaching arc
This is the tort/personal-injury capstone. It runs the full litigation arc — intake, investigation, liability and damages analysis, discovery, settlement, and trial preparation — around an ordinary but richly disputed intersection collision. The pedagogy mirrors the Sonsteng auto-negligence jury-trial exercise: a swearing contest over right-of-way, an independent eyewitness as the load-bearing evidence, and a diagram/scene reconstruction as the anchor exhibit.

## Load-bearing teaching points
- **The signal dispute.** There is no red-light camera, so liability turns on witness credibility and physical evidence. Push students to connect the point of impact, the ~90-degree rotation, and rest positions to the competing accounts, and to see the independent eyewitness (Fontaine) and the failure-to-yield citation as their strongest liability assets.
- **Comparative fault.** Florida moved to a modified comparative-negligence system in 2023 (see Fla. Stat. § 768.81, as amended by Ch. 2023-15, Laws of Fla.), generally barring recovery for a plaintiff more than 50% at fault. Students should analyze how the alleged late entry and the concealed phone-glance (m13.fact.027) could push Ms. Reyes's share upward, and how to counsel a client about a harmful fact.
- **No-fault / PIP and the injury threshold.** Florida's no-fault framework (Fla. Stat. §§ 627.730–627.7405) requires PIP coverage and limits tort suits for non-economic damages unless the injury meets the permanency threshold in Fla. Stat. § 627.737(2). Dr. Emerson's permanency and MMI opinion (m13.fact.042) is the gateway; tie it to both the threshold and the future-damages claim.
- **Damages.** Economic (~$42k medical, ~$8.5k lost income, ~$9.8k property) plus non-economic if the threshold is met; discuss future medicals and arthritis risk. Note the collateral-source and PIP set-off issues at a high level.
- **Contingency fee.** Florida contingency-fee tiers and the statement of client rights are governed by R. Regulating Fla. Bar 4-1.5(f). The engagement letter deliberately keeps specific percentages in the (unincluded) statement of client rights.
- **Professional responsibility — Rule 4.2.** Hutchins is represented (R. Regulating Fla. Bar 4-4.2). The persona is configured to fire a teaching moment, not a silent block, if a student tries to interview him directly.

## Interview/debrief focus
Ms. Reyes is a **distressed** client under financial pressure. The rapport-gated facts (prior knee soreness; rent arrears) and the concealed fact (phone glance) unlock only under the specified relational triggers — students who lead, pressure, or flatter should not earn them. Debrief on both fact coverage (Axis A) and the client's felt experience of being heard (Axis B).

## Timing (rough clock)
- Client interview + debrief: ~90 min.
- Investigation / chronology workshop (eyewitness, crash report, physics): ~90 min.
- Liability + comparative-fault analysis: ~75 min.
- Damages / PIP-threshold workshop: ~60 min.
- Settlement plan + negotiation simulation: ~90 min.
- (Optional) trial-prep / deposition outlines: out-of-class.

## Discussion questions
1. With no red-light camera, what evidence actually decides the signal dispute — and why is the independent eyewitness worth more than either driver's word?
2. Under Florida's post-2023 modified comparative negligence, what happens if a jury puts Ms. Reyes at 51%? How does that reframe the phone-glance fact?
3. How do you counsel a client about a harmful fact (the phone glance) you have an ethical duty not to help her conceal?
4. What exactly does the permanency/MMI opinion unlock — the tort threshold, the future-damages claim, or both?
5. The client is three weeks behind on rent and wants to settle fast. How do you honor that pressure without letting it drive an under-value settlement?
6. Hutchins has his own concealed distraction (the coffee mug). How would you develop it in his deposition — and why can't you just call him up?
