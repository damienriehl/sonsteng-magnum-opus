# Instructor Notes — Reyes v. Hutchins (m13) {#b:b8857f77}

*For instructors only. Not part of the student packet. Citations below are instructor-verified at publish; verify against current Florida law before teaching.* {#b:3408a0cd}

## Teaching arc {#b:e6314e47}
This is the tort/personal-injury capstone. It runs the full litigation arc — intake, investigation, liability and damages analysis, discovery, settlement, and trial preparation — around an ordinary but richly disputed intersection collision. The pedagogy mirrors the Sonsteng auto-negligence jury-trial exercise: a swearing contest over right-of-way, an independent eyewitness as the load-bearing evidence, and a diagram/scene reconstruction as the anchor exhibit. {#b:c6add3b6}

## Load-bearing teaching points {#b:6e73a073}
- **The signal dispute.** There is no red-light camera, so liability turns on witness credibility and physical evidence. Push students to connect the point of impact, the ~90-degree rotation, and rest positions to the competing accounts, and to see the independent eyewitness (Fontaine) and the failure-to-yield citation as their strongest liability assets. {#b:15e50fbe}
- **Comparative fault.** Florida moved to a modified comparative-negligence system in 2023 (see Fla. Stat. § 768.81, as amended by Ch. 2023-15, Laws of Fla.), generally barring recovery for a plaintiff more than 50% at fault. Students should analyze how the alleged late entry and the concealed phone-glance (m13.fact.027) could push Ms. Reyes's share upward, and how to counsel a client about a harmful fact. {#b:714ebe4a}
- **No-fault / PIP and the injury threshold.** Florida's no-fault framework (Fla. Stat. §§ 627.730–627.7405) requires PIP coverage and limits tort suits for non-economic damages unless the injury meets the permanency threshold in Fla. Stat. § 627.737(2). Dr. Emerson's permanency and MMI opinion (m13.fact.042) is the gateway; tie it to both the threshold and the future-damages claim. {#b:39f7eed7}
- **Damages.** Economic (~$42k medical, ~$8.5k lost income, ~$9.8k property) plus non-economic if the threshold is met; discuss future medicals and arthritis risk. Note the collateral-source and PIP set-off issues at a high level. {#b:d9412d83}
- **Contingency fee.** Florida contingency-fee tiers and the statement of client rights are governed by R. Regulating Fla. Bar 4-1.5(f). The engagement letter deliberately keeps specific percentages in the (unincluded) statement of client rights. {#b:6d7d86c2}
- **Professional responsibility — Rule 4.2.** Hutchins is represented (R. Regulating Fla. Bar 4-4.2). The persona is configured to fire a teaching moment, not a silent block, if a student tries to interview him directly. {#b:c751817d}

## Interview/debrief focus {#b:db185ffe}
Ms. Reyes is a **distressed** client under financial pressure. The rapport-gated facts (prior knee soreness; rent arrears) and the concealed fact (phone glance) unlock only under the specified relational triggers — students who lead, pressure, or flatter should not earn them. Debrief on both fact coverage (Axis A) and the client's felt experience of being heard (Axis B). {#b:cc1709e0}

## Timing (rough clock) {#b:024d7a28}
- Client interview + debrief: ~90 min. {#b:f182bb68}
- Investigation / chronology workshop (eyewitness, crash report, physics): ~90 min. {#b:cac75d37}
- Liability + comparative-fault analysis: ~75 min. {#b:4544d92a}
- Damages / PIP-threshold workshop: ~60 min. {#b:7e9dc378}
- Settlement plan + negotiation simulation: ~90 min. {#b:b6b9410f}
- (Optional) trial-prep / deposition outlines: out-of-class. {#b:56a15cd1}

## Discussion questions {#b:8f2bf42f}
1. With no red-light camera, what evidence actually decides the signal dispute — and why is the independent eyewitness worth more than either driver's word? {#b:0cdd6403}
2. Under Florida's post-2023 modified comparative negligence, what happens if a jury puts Ms. Reyes at 51%? How does that reframe the phone-glance fact? {#b:f071a098}
3. How do you counsel a client about a harmful fact (the phone glance) you have an ethical duty not to help her conceal? {#b:85ba3475}
4. What exactly does the permanency/MMI opinion unlock — the tort threshold, the future-damages claim, or both? {#b:143e14d4}
5. The client is three weeks behind on rent and wants to settle fast. How do you honor that pressure without letting it drive an under-value settlement? {#b:8fbd9c42}
6. Hutchins has his own concealed distraction (the coffee mug). How would you develop it in his deposition — and why can't you just call him up? {#b:24e29617}
