# Instructor Notes — m09 (Marriage Dissolution: Sitko / Aldergrove)

*Teaching notes only. Do not distribute to students. Student packets are facts-only; the citations below are for the instructor and are marked instructor-verified-at-publish. No answer key or intentional-error layer is provided at this stage.*

## What this matter is really teaching

This is primarily an **interview-and-counsel** exercise wearing family-law clothes. The pedagogical center of gravity is the client interview: two of the most consequential facts (the February 2025 wall-punch/doorway incident and the two hours Renata left the children alone) are **rapport-gated**, and one (the ongoing relationship with a coworker that predated the separation announcement) is **concealed**. A student who charges at the property spreadsheet will build a competent-looking petition on an incomplete record. Reward interviewers who open with an open-ended invitation, ask about wellbeing, do not interrupt, reassure on confidentiality, and follow hints gently — and penalize any attempt to extract admissions through pressure or flattery (which, by design, does not work on the persona).

## Rapport-trigger key (for debrief)

- Renata's wall-incident disclosure unlocks at **min_turns 5** with `acknowledged_emotion` + `confidentiality_reassurance`.
- Renata's "left the kids home" disclosure unlocks at **min_turns 6** with `nonjudgmental_response` + `no_interruption_streak`.
- The concealed relationship is not rapport-gated; it should generally stay concealed unless the student builds substantial trust and asks with real care — treat surfacing it as a stretch achievement, and discuss the strategic question of whether counsel even needs it disclosed.

## Professional-responsibility moments

- **Rule 4.2 (communication with a represented person):** Harlan is represented by opposing counsel. Any student attempt to interview him directly should trigger the teaching moment, not a silent block. Meridian Rule of Professional Conduct 4.2 (mirrors ABA Model Rule 4.2).
- **Confidentiality:** Meridian Rule of Professional Conduct 1.6. Discuss whether Renata's sensitive facts belong in any served document and how to protect them.
- **Trust accounting:** the evergreen retainer implicates Meridian Rule of Professional Conduct 1.15 (safekeeping property); note that fees are applied only after a statement issues.

## Substantive research targets (Meridian canon)

Students research these themselves; instructor reference only:

- **No-fault ground / procedure:** Meridian dissolutions proceed on irretrievable breakdown in the Family Division of the District Court.
- **Custody / best interests:** research the Meridian best-interests factors; emphasize the primary-caretaker history versus each parent's availability, and how a single family-conflict incident is weighed.
- **Property division:** Meridian is an equitable-distribution jurisdiction — marital property divided justly and equitably, not necessarily equally. Sweat-equity in the home and valuation of a closely held one-person business (tangible assets plus disputed goodwill) are the hard questions.
- **Spousal maintenance:** research the maintenance standards; the key teaching point is that Renata's higher, steadier income cuts both ways — it is her strength on stability and her exposure on maintenance.
- **Statute of limitations is not in play** (dissolution), but note the trust-accounting and confidentiality rules above.

## Discussion prompts

- Should counsel fight over the "sole vs. joint" label or invest in a workable, predictable parenting schedule? Tie to the children's demonstrated anxiety about "whose night."
- What discovery and which expert do you need to value the business, and how do you handle the accountant's inability to confirm or rule out unreported cash?
- How do you counsel an angry, guilt-ridden client toward a settlement that serves the children without abandoning her legitimate interests?
