# Instructor Notes — m09 (Marriage Dissolution: Sitko / Aldergrove)

*Teaching notes only. Do not distribute to students. Student packets are facts-only; the citations below are for the instructor and are marked instructor-verified-at-publish. No answer key or intentional-error layer is provided at this stage.*

## What this matter is really teaching

This is primarily an **interview-and-counsel** exercise wearing family-law clothes. The pedagogical center of gravity is the client interview: two of the most consequential facts (the February 2025 wall-punch/doorway incident and the two hours Renata left the children alone) are **rapport-gated**, and one (the ongoing relationship with a coworker that predated the separation announcement) is **concealed**. A student who charges at the property spreadsheet will build a competent-looking petition on an incomplete record. Reward interviewers who open with an open-ended invitation, ask about wellbeing, do not interrupt, reassure on confidentiality, and follow hints gently — and penalize any attempt to extract admissions through pressure or flattery (which, by design, does not work on the persona).

## Rapport-trigger key (for debrief)

- Renata's wall-incident disclosure unlocks at **min_turns 5** with `acknowledged_emotion` + `confidentiality_reassurance`.
- Renata's "left the kids home" disclosure unlocks at **min_turns 6** with `nonjudgmental_response` + `no_interruption_streak`.
- The concealed relationship is not rapport-gated; it should generally stay concealed unless the student builds substantial trust and asks with real care — treat surfacing it as a stretch achievement, and discuss the strategic question of whether counsel even needs it disclosed.

## Professional-responsibility moments

- **Rule 4.2 (communication with a represented person):** Harlan is represented by opposing counsel. Any student attempt to interview him directly should trigger the teaching moment, not a silent block. Meridian Rule of Professional Conduct 4.2 (mirrors ABA Model Rule 4.2).
- **Confidentiality:** Meridian Rule of Professional Conduct 1.6. Discuss whether Renata's sensitive facts belong in any served document and how to protect them.
- **Trust accounting:** the evergreen retainer implicates Meridian Rule of Professional Conduct 1.15 (safekeeping property); note that fees are applied only after a statement issues.

## Substantive research targets (Meridian canon)

Students research these themselves; instructor reference only:

- **No-fault ground / procedure:** Meridian dissolutions proceed on irretrievable breakdown in the Family Division of the District Court.
- **Custody / best interests:** research the Meridian best-interests factors; emphasize the primary-caretaker history versus each parent's availability, and how a single family-conflict incident is weighed.
- **Property division:** Meridian is an equitable-distribution jurisdiction — marital property divided justly and equitably, not necessarily equally. Sweat-equity in the home and valuation of a closely held one-person business (tangible assets plus disputed goodwill) are the hard questions.
- **Spousal maintenance:** research the maintenance standards; the key teaching point is that Renata's higher, steadier income cuts both ways — it is her strength on stability and her exposure on maintenance.
- **Statute of limitations is not in play** (dissolution), but note the trust-accounting and confidentiality rules above.

## Teaching arc (how to run the session)

*The model answers, disclosure tables, rubric mapping, and grading bands now live in `exercise/answer-key.md` (instructor-only). These notes are the facilitation layer — the arc, the clock, and the debrief — and deliberately do **not** repeat the key. Read the key first; teach from these.*

The session is built as a **funnel**, mirroring the interview it teaches: start wide and relational, narrow to doctrine and drafting, then widen again to reflection. The whole matter is engineered so that a student who skips the relationship cannot do the law well — the two rapport-gated facts (the February wall/doorway incident and the two hours the children were alone) and the concealed relationship are *upstream* of the custody analysis, the confidentiality reasoning, and the settlement plan. Let students feel that dependency rather than announcing it.

1. **Frame the no-villain reality first.** Before anyone touches the spreadsheet, establish that this is two decent, exhausted parents (the packet's own framing) — not a fault contest. Students primed to find a villain interview badly and counsel worse.
2. **Run the client interview live** against the distressed persona. Resist coaching mid-interview; let a student who races to the property questions *fail to earn* the gated facts, then use that in debrief. The persona will not reward pressure or flattery — that is the point.
3. **Surface the Rule 4.2 fork early.** Some student will want to "just ask Harlan." Let the in-app teaching moment fire and treat it as a feature, not a gotcha.
4. **Move to counseling**, where the double-edged income disparity is the crux: the same steady salary that wins stability creates maintenance exposure. Watch for students who tell the client only the comforting half.
5. **Close on strategy and drafting**, then reopen to reflection with the discussion questions below.

## Timing (a 110-minute block; scale to your period)

- **0:00–0:10 — Orientation & no-villain framing.** Set the relational stakes; remind students the packet is facts-only and the Meridian family-law research is theirs.
- **0:10–0:35 — Client interview (primary activity).** ~20–25 min of live interviewing against the distressed persona; the rapport-gated facts unlock only at min_turns 5 and 6 under their emotional preconditions, so a rushed interview simply will not reach them.
- **0:35–0:45 — Rule 4.2 checkpoint.** Debrief any attempted contact with Harlan; route through Priscilla Hovland.
- **0:45–1:05 — Counseling role-play.** Options-framing on custody, home, business, and — non-negotiably — maintenance exposure.
- **1:05–1:25 — Settlement strategy huddle.** Prioritize the durable schedule; treat property as tradeable; plan the business-valuation and forensic-cash discovery.
- **1:25–1:35 — Drafting orientation.** How the interview and case theory drive the petition, the rotation-aware parenting plan, and the property proposal.
- **1:35–1:50 — Debrief & self-reflection.** Work 3–4 of the questions below; end on the self-reflection prompt.

## Debrief discussion questions

1. **Emotional-client counseling.** Renata warms up "when she feels heard rather than managed." Which specific moves earned (or would have earned) the February incident and the latchkey disclosure — and what did an alarmed or interrupting reaction cost? Tie answers to the two-axis debrief (fact coverage *and* the client's felt experience), not to a technique checklist.
2. **Custody vs. property strategy.** Both parents have signaled they will settle the property if the schedule is fixed. Defend a plan that *trades* equity flexibility for a durable, rotation-aware calendar — and stress-test it: when would fighting the "sole vs. joint" label actually be worth it?
3. **The double-edged fact.** How do you counsel a client whose greatest strength (steady income, full benefits) is simultaneously her greatest exposure (maintenance)? Practice saying the hard half out loud without either false comfort or fatalism.
4. **Valuing "just me and my hands."** Is Harlan's business an enterprise or a toolbox? Walk the tangible-assets / enterprise-goodwill / personal-goodwill split, and design the discovery and expert you need — including how to handle an accountant who can neither confirm nor rule out unreported cash.
5. **Confidentiality under pressure.** Does Renata's undisclosed relationship belong anywhere in a served document? Reason from the duty of confidentiality and from what a no-fault court actually weighs — and separate "what we know" from "what we plead."
6. **The single incident.** How should the February wall/doorway episode appear in the parenting analysis — neither weaponized into abuse-per-se nor waved away? Use the GAL's calibrated "relevant, single, continued-attention" framing as the model.
7. **Self-reflection prompt (competition criterion 11).** *One free-text question to each student:* "What did your client most need from you in this interview that you almost missed — and what will you do differently next time?"
