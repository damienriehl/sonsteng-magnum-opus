# Instructor Notes — m01 Arbitration (Osgard v. Halden Regional Transit District) {#b:6405e2dd}

*Teaching stub. NOT for student distribution. Citations below are for instructor orientation only and are marked instructor-verified-at-publish. No answer key or intentional-error set is provided here; those are deferred.* {#b:d70bd46c}

## What this matter teaches {#b:6a334959}

This is the Practicum's arbitration shape: a discharge grievance under a collective-bargaining agreement, taken to final and binding labor arbitration. It exercises the front half of the litigation arc (case analysis, fact development, interviewing, written statement of the case) and the hearing skills of direct and cross-examination, without the full apparatus of court civil procedure. It pairs naturally with the interview engine because the grievant carries rapport-gated disclosures and there is a live Rule 4.2 teaching moment. {#b:6fa7084f}

## The legal spine (instructor orientation) {#b:529cbab7}

- **Just cause and the employer's burden.** The core question is whether the District had just cause to discharge. Under the parties' agreement (Exhibit 001, Art. 8.1 and 22.8), the District bears the burden. Direct students to research the common arbitral "seven tests" of just cause (notice, reasonable rule, investigation, fair investigation, proof, even-handedness, and proportional penalty) rather than supplying them. {#b:96c53e4a}
- **Protected safety activity.** Article 12 of the agreement contains an anti-retaliation clause for good-faith safety reporting. For the real-tier analog, instructors may reference Meridian's employment/wage-and-hour limitations backdrop (*Mer. Stat. § 181.20*, 3-year period, per *meridian.json*) and note that whistleblower/protected-activity doctrines are the student's research target; this exercise keeps the analysis inside the agreement. {#b:384657ca}
- **Remedy.** Article 22.7 authorizes reinstatement and back pay but bars the arbitrator from modifying the agreement — relevant to counseling the client on settlement versus a full award. {#b:1603b677}

## Pedagogical seams (surface in debrief) {#b:d90ed62b}

1. **Mixed inspection result** (Exhibit 007): leaks confirmed on 411 and 426; 418 held pressure. Tests whether students can hold a partly-adverse fact and still argue the reasonableness of the tag. {#b:34ed910c}
2. **Shifting rationale** (Exhibit 006 + Bexley statement): the District's stated reason narrows from the safety dispute toward insubordination across the grievance steps — a classic just-cause credibility lever. {#b:78165e41}
3. **Timing of the 2024 note** (Exhibit 005): appears only after the complaints begin. Good cross material. {#b:67c9d713}
4. **Rule 4.2.** Rennick is a manager of the represented adverse party. Employee-side students must not interview him directly; the app fires the teaching moment. Meridian numbering tracks the Model Rules: *Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2* (communication with represented persons). {#b:83b87211}
5. **The phone recording** (fact 041, concealed): a judgment-and-ethics discussion point about client-created evidence and counsel's obligations; handle in counseling, not as a gotcha. {#b:dafb0608}

## Interview design notes {#b:35421077}

- Osgard is **guarded**. Her two rapport-gated facts (the two-year-old near-miss; the unpaid-bill embarrassment) unlock only on the specified triggers — social pressure never works. The near-miss is the emotional engine of the whole case; students who earn it will counsel her far better on settlement. {#b:6dea19fb}
- Dellwood is **cooperative but self-protective**; his candor about the pull-out culture is rapport-gated on a confidentiality reassurance. {#b:172e4939}

## How to teach it — session arc and timing {#b:c858117a}

A companion **answer key** lives at `exercise/answer-key.md` (instructor-only) with the model analysis, the full disclosure map, and the graded-A deliverable outline. These notes stay on *how to teach*; the key holds *the answers*. Do not hand the key to students. {#b:3054dfab}

Suggested arc over roughly two weeks / four contact blocks; adjust to your clinic's cadence. {#b:bdd18f2d}

- **Block 1 — Intake and file mastery (~90 min).** Students read the entire case file cold and build a one-page chronology before any interview. Do a rapid cold-call round on "what is missing and what does not line up" to seed the seams. Confirm the conflicts screen and the aligned-interest posture with the Union (business layer) as a quick professional-responsibility warm-up. {#b:4a359ef6}
- **Block 2 — Interview lab (~2 hrs).** Students plan their Osgard funnel *before* opening the chat, then run one sustained interview each, followed by the Dellwood interview. Debrief on the two-axis model (fact coverage + the client's felt experience). This is where the rapport-gated near-miss either lands or does not; use the contrast between students who earned it and those who did not. {#b:ec6e3c05}
- **Block 3 — Theory and written work (~2 hrs + take-home).** Map facts to the just-cause elements, commit to a theory, and draft the statement of the case and the client letter. Workshop two or three drafts live, focusing on how each handles unit 418. {#b:4c0e505a}
- **Block 4 — Hearing prep and debrief (~2 hrs).** Direct of Osgard/Dellwood and cross of the (represented, un-interviewed) management witness. Close with the two-axis debrief and a professional-responsibility round on Rule 4.2 and the phone recording. {#b:0eb471fb}

**Timing watch-points.** Reserve real time for the interview debrief — it is where the pedagogy compounds. Do not let the just-cause doctrine hunt eat the whole matter; the research target is the seven tests and the safe-harbor interaction, not a treatise. Flag the Rule 4.2 moment early so students meet it as counseling, not as a trap sprung at the hearing. {#b:a86b19a4}

## Discussion questions (seminar / debrief) {#b:90925df7}

1. Osgard volunteers her refusal and her safety email but holds the two-year-old near-miss close. What in an interviewer's behavior earns that disclosure — and what predictably shuts it down? Compare transcripts where it surfaced against where it did not. {#b:b6484419}
2. Unit 418 held pressure on inspection. How should a first-rate advocate sequence and frame a partly-adverse fact like this — raise it first, bury it, or wait for the other side? What does each choice signal to a neutral? {#b:cbedf25f}
3. The District's stated reason narrows from "safety / chain of command" at Step 1 to "insubordination" by Step 3. Why is a shifting rationale such a powerful just-cause lever, and how do Bexley's contemporaneous notes change its evidentiary weight? {#b:eb167af9}
4. A student wants to interview Rennick to "hear his side." Walk through the Rule 4.2 analysis: why is a supervisory manager inside the represented-organization shield when coworker Dellwood is not, and what is the proper route to his account? {#b:a37bf950}
5. The client recorded a hallway conversation and never told anyone. How should counsel counsel her — on legality, on evidentiary use, and on disclosure — and why is "counseling first, tactics second" the right order here? {#b:a33b6712}
6. Osgard says she cares more about vindication than money and would reject a money-only settlement. How does that non-legal goal reshape settlement strategy, and how should a lawyer test whether a stated goal is the real one? {#b:61101b94}
