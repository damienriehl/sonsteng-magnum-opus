# Instructor Notes — m01 Arbitration (Osgard v. Halden Regional Transit District)

*Teaching stub. NOT for student distribution. Citations below are for instructor orientation only and are marked instructor-verified-at-publish. No answer key or intentional-error set is provided here; those are deferred.*

## What this matter teaches

This is the Practicum's arbitration shape: a discharge grievance under a collective-bargaining agreement, taken to final and binding labor arbitration. It exercises the front half of the litigation arc (case analysis, fact development, interviewing, written statement of the case) and the hearing skills of direct and cross-examination, without the full apparatus of court civil procedure. It pairs naturally with the interview engine because the grievant carries rapport-gated disclosures and there is a live Rule 4.2 teaching moment.

## The legal spine (instructor orientation)

- **Just cause and the employer's burden.** The core question is whether the District had just cause to discharge. Under the parties' agreement (Exhibit 001, Art. 8.1 and 22.8), the District bears the burden. Direct students to research the common arbitral "seven tests" of just cause (notice, reasonable rule, investigation, fair investigation, proof, even-handedness, and proportional penalty) rather than supplying them.
- **Protected safety activity.** Article 12 of the agreement contains an anti-retaliation clause for good-faith safety reporting. For the real-tier analog, instructors may reference Meridian's employment/wage-and-hour limitations backdrop (*Mer. Stat. § 181.20*, 3-year period, per *meridian.json*) and note that whistleblower/protected-activity doctrines are the student's research target; this exercise keeps the analysis inside the agreement.
- **Remedy.** Article 22.7 authorizes reinstatement and back pay but bars the arbitrator from modifying the agreement — relevant to counseling the client on settlement versus a full award.

## Pedagogical seams (surface in debrief)

1. **Mixed inspection result** (Exhibit 007): leaks confirmed on 411 and 426; 418 held pressure. Tests whether students can hold a partly-adverse fact and still argue the reasonableness of the tag.
2. **Shifting rationale** (Exhibit 006 + Bexley statement): the District's stated reason narrows from the safety dispute toward insubordination across the grievance steps — a classic just-cause credibility lever.
3. **Timing of the 2024 note** (Exhibit 005): appears only after the complaints begin. Good cross material.
4. **Rule 4.2.** Rennick is a manager of the represented adverse party. Employee-side students must not interview him directly; the app fires the teaching moment. Meridian numbering tracks the Model Rules: *Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2* (communication with represented persons).
5. **The phone recording** (fact 041, concealed): a judgment-and-ethics discussion point about client-created evidence and counsel's obligations; handle in counseling, not as a gotcha.

## Interview design notes

- Osgard is **guarded**. Her two rapport-gated facts (the two-year-old near-miss; the unpaid-bill embarrassment) unlock only on the specified triggers — social pressure never works. The near-miss is the emotional engine of the whole case; students who earn it will counsel her far better on settlement.
- Dellwood is **cooperative but self-protective**; his candor about the pull-out culture is rapport-gated on a confidentiality reassurance.
