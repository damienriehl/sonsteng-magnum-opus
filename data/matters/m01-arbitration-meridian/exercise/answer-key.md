## 1. Model Case Analysis {#b:b25f3a7b}

### 1.1 The governing frame — just cause under the agreement {#b:fc7f4454}

This is a discharge grievance under a collective-bargaining agreement, resolved by final and binding arbitration [m01.fact.006–.007]. The single dispositive question is whether the District had **just cause** to discharge Osgard (Exhibit 001, Art. 8.1), and by the parties' own agreement **the District carries the burden** (Exhibit 001, Art. 22.8) [m01.fact.050]. Two structural features of the CBA drive the whole analysis: {#b:3735eae4}

- **Art. 8.2 — progressive discipline.** Discipline "shall ordinarily be progressive," with severity proportional to the offense, "except that serious misconduct may warrant more severe discipline without prior warnings." {#b:728f09f5}
- **Art. 12 — the safety safe harbor.** "No employee shall be required to operate or certify as roadworthy any vehicle the employee reasonably believes to be unsafe" (Art. 12.1), and "[a]n employee shall not be disciplined or retaliated against for raising a good-faith safety concern through the District's safety-reporting procedures" (Art. 12.4). {#b:7ea9b783}

The remedy comes from the agreement itself: the arbitrator may order reinstatement and back pay but may not modify the agreement (Art. 22.7) [m01.fact.050]. {#b:9206cb3d}

Instructors should push students to research and deploy the arbitral **seven tests of just cause** (the Daugherty framework): (1) forewarning/notice of the rule and consequences; (2) reasonableness of the rule or order; (3) investigation before discipline; (4) fair and objective investigation; (5) substantial proof of guilt; (6) even-handed, nondiscriminatory application; (7) penalty proportional to the offense and to the employee's record. Meridian's professional-conduct numbering tracks the Model Rules (*Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2*); the just-cause tests are arbitral common law, not statute, so no Meridian statute supplies them — the student's job is to find and apply them. {#b:d39a0264}

### 1.2 Burden and quantum of proof {#b:17d5cf12}

The **burden** is not genuinely contested: Art. 22.8 puts it on the District. The **quantum** is where reasonable minds differ. Arbitrators split between a preponderance standard for ordinary misconduct and a "clear and convincing" (occasionally "beyond a reasonable doubt") standard where the charged conduct carries moral stigma or quasi-criminal overtones. Insubordination here is a workplace-conduct charge, not a stigmatizing crime, so most arbitrators would apply **preponderance** — but a first-rate student should flag the split, argue for the higher standard on the theory that a "pattern of insubordination" finding brands a tradesperson in a small labor market [m01.fact.052, persona fear "branded a troublemaker in a small trade"], and note the answer is arguable. {#b:f376e7f7}

### 1.3 The issues the facts actually raise {#b:8ad3519f}

1. **Was the September 22 refusal even "insubordination"?** This is the case's fulcrum. Osgard red-tagged units 411, 418, and 426 for weeping air lines and rear-service-brake pressure loss and entered them in the repair system [m01.fact.012–.013; Exhibit 004, RO-25-3341/-3342/-3343]. Rennick directed her to clear at least two for the morning pull-out [m01.fact.014; Exhibit 004 Supervisor Override Log]; she refused to certify them roadworthy [m01.fact.015]. **Art. 12.1 is a direct safe harbor**: an employee cannot be *required* to certify a vehicle she reasonably believes unsafe. The ordinary "obey now, grieve later" rule that defeats most insubordination defenses has a recognized safety exception, and here the CBA codifies it. If the refusal is protected, the Sept 22 "direct order" was not a lawful order she was obligated to obey, and test (2) (reasonableness of the order) and test (5) (proof of a punishable offense) both fail for the District. {#b:7b026e16}

2. **Was the refusal a "good-faith" and "reasonable" belief?** The contractor confirmed leaks outside tolerance on 411 and 426; 418 held pressure on the October 24 inspection [m01.fact.017; Exhibit 007]. The report expressly declines to opine on 418's condition on September 22 [Exhibit 007 Summary]. Two-of-three confirmed is strong evidence the belief was reasonable and in good faith; 418 does not defeat "reasonable belief," and the "tag first, verify second" norm on brakes is exactly what a lead brake mechanic is supposed to do [m01.fact.044; statement-dellwood]. {#b:4531d985}

3. **The October 9 reassignment — abandonment or housekeeping?** Rennick told Osgard to leave the brake bay; she first walked to the parts room to secure her tools and log out of the repair terminal (~15 minutes) before complying [m01.fact.024]. The District calls this "leaving an assigned post" [m01.fact.025; Exhibit 002 ¶2]. A 15-minute delay to close out an open repair on a safety-sensitive system is defensible as ordinary practice, not defiance — but this is the District's *best* independent conduct fact, and **reasonable minds differ** on whether a neutral would see securing tools as reasonable or as a pretextual sliver dressed up as post-abandonment. {#b:a3dc2f21}

4. **Progressive discipline / even-handedness (test 6 and 7).** Osgard had eleven years, "meets or exceeds" reviews through 2023, and two shop safety-record recognitions [m01.fact.004; Exhibit 005]. Her only prior entries are a minor 2023 timekeeping reminder and a single 2024 supervisor note — no formal discipline in nine years [m01.fact.028; Exhibit 005]. Discharge on this record skips every progressive step (Art. 8.2). The District must therefore prove **serious misconduct** to justify the jump; if the Sept 22 refusal is protected and the Oct 9 delay is thin, there is no serious misconduct and the penalty is disproportionate. {#b:08dff12d}

5. **Pretext / shifting rationale.** The District's stated reason narrows across the grievance steps: Step 1 emphasizes the safety email and "chain of command"; by Step 3 the written response never uses the word "safety" and rests entirely on insubordination and the reassignment [m01.fact.033; Exhibit 006; statement-bexley]. Bexley's contemporaneous notes capture the shift [m01.fact.034, .049]. A moving rationale is a classic just-cause credibility lever and strong circumstantial proof of retaliatory motive. {#b:b8c36ffd}

6. **Timing of the 2024 note.** The November 2024 note calling Osgard "argumentative and resistant to direction" is the only entry with teeth, and it surfaces right after she began pushing back on rushed brake work [m01.fact.029–.030; Exhibit 005]. The District says it reflects an independent performance concern [m01.fact.031; statement-rennick]. The file itself notes the placement is "contested" and offered "without characterization" [Exhibit 005 Notes for the File]. Good cross material; not conclusive. {#b:ebb88c60}

7. **Protected activity.** The October 1 email reported the brake condition and the return-to-service pressure and copied the external Regional Transit Safety Oversight Board [m01.fact.019–.020; Exhibit 003]. Art. 12.4 protects good-faith safety reporting "through the District's safety-reporting procedures." The District's theme that copying an outside board was "going around the chain of command" [m01.fact.022; statement-rennick] is exactly the retaliatory animus Art. 12.4 forbids — but note a genuine open question: Art. 12.4 protects reporting *through the District's procedures*, and the District may argue the external copy fell outside those procedures. The student should anticipate and neutralize this (the email went up the internal chain *and* copied the board; the internal report is squarely protected) [Exhibit 003 To/Cc lines]. {#b:7d63d2e7}

### 1.4 Strongest theory — each side {#b:7c1bd62d}

**Grievant/Union (the firm's client).** *Retaliation dressed up as insubordination.* The refusal to certify was not insubordination at all but conduct the CBA affirmatively protects (Art. 12.1); the safety email was protected reporting (Art. 12.4); the contractor's two confirmed leaks vindicate the core judgment [Exhibit 007]; the "insubordination" record materialized only after the complaints (the 2024 note, [m01.fact.030]); the District's rationale shifted step to step, betraying pretext [m01.fact.033–.034]; and an eleven-year clean record makes summary discharge a gross violation of progressive discipline (Art. 8.2). Remedy: reinstatement, full back pay and benefits, expungement [m01.fact.051; Exhibit 006 remedy request]. {#b:aaf745b2}

**Employer/District.** *A straightforward conduct case.* Osgard admits she refused a direct instruction from her superintendent [m01.fact.015], then failed to comply promptly with a lawful reassignment and left her post on October 9 [m01.fact.024–.025; Exhibit 002]. Unit 418 held pressure, proving she over-tagged and manufactured a service crisis [m01.fact.017, .048; Exhibit 007; statement-rennick]. The 2024 note documents a pre-existing pattern of resistance to direction [Exhibit 005], and Art. 8.2 allows severe discipline for serious misconduct without prior warnings. Safety was handled through proper channels; the discharge rests on conduct, not on any safety report [Exhibit 006 Step 2/3]. {#b:2c3bfd54}

### 1.5 What a first-rate student would argue, and the honest balance {#b:308a42db}

An A brief leads with the **Art. 12.1 safe harbor** to convert the marquee "insubordination" charge into protected activity, then runs the **seven tests** to show the District fails on order-reasonableness, proof, even-handedness (progressive discipline skipped), and proportional penalty; uses the **shifting rationale** and the **timing of the note** as circumstantial proof of pretext; **concedes unit 418** candidly and reframes it as the reasonable-precaution norm, quoting Exhibit 007's own caveat that it does not opine on the September 22 condition; and ties the remedy request to the client's stated interest in vindication, not just money [m01.fact.051–.052]. {#b:514f73fe}

Be honest about the seams: the **October 9 episode** is the District's one genuinely independent conduct fact and a neutral could credit it; the **quantum of proof** is arguable; **Art. 12.4's "through the District's procedures"** language gives the District a real (if weak) toehold on the external-copy point; and if the arbitrator finds *some* insubordination on October 9 but no just cause for *discharge*, the likely outcome is **reinstatement with a reduced/last-chance remedy rather than a clean win** — a realistic middle result the student should counsel the client about. Reasonable minds differ on whether this record supports full make-whole relief or a mitigated remedy. {#b:30cf16db}

## 3. Deliverable Model-Answer Outline {#b:7079b4eb}

### 3.1 Graded-A outline {#b:857d8274}

1. **Caption and submission.** Halden Regional Transit District v. Marceline Osgard and MAPW Local 7; final and binding arbitration before neutral arbitrator Frances Eklund under Art. 22.5 [m01.fact.035; matter.json caption]. {#b:a6516a2d}

2. **Issue statement.** "Whether the District had just cause under Art. 8.1 to discharge Marceline Osgard and, if not, what remedy is appropriate under Art. 22.7." Note the District's burden (Art. 22.8) [m01.fact.050]. {#b:5bb7bdf6}

3. **Statement of facts (chronological, exhibit-anchored).** Eleven-year tenure, "meets/exceeds" reviews, two safety recognitions [m01.fact.001, .003–.004; Exhibit 005] → Rennick's 2024 arrival and pull-out mandate [m01.fact.008–.011] → September 22 red-tags of 411/418/426 and the return-to-service order [m01.fact.012–.016; Exhibits 004, 003] → October 1 safety email copying the Oversight Board [m01.fact.019–.020; Exhibit 003] → October 9 reassignment and the 15-minute tool-securing [m01.fact.024] → October 15 discharge [m01.fact.026–.027; Exhibit 002] → grievance, step denials, shifting rationale, referral to arbitration [m01.fact.032–.035; Exhibit 006] → October 24 contractor inspection confirming leaks on 411/426, 418 within tolerance [m01.fact.017; Exhibit 007]. **A work marshals the seams into the narrative; it does not hide 418.** {#b:6bae3144}

4. **Argument.** {#b:88037700}
   - **A. The September 22 refusal was protected, not insubordinate.** Art. 12.1 safe harbor; the "obey now, grieve later" safety exception; therefore no lawful order was disobeyed [m01.fact.015; Exhibit 001 Art. 12.1]. {#b:de527fad}
   - **B. The District cannot carry its just-cause burden under the seven tests.** Order unreasonable (test 2); no serious misconduct proven (test 5); progressive discipline skipped on an 11-year clean record (tests 6–7; Art. 8.2) [Exhibit 005]. {#b:f206f0b2}
   - **C. The stated reason is pretext.** Shifting rationale across steps [m01.fact.033–.034; Exhibit 006; Bexley notes], timing of the 2024 note [m01.fact.029–.030; Exhibit 005], and animus toward the external safety report [m01.fact.022; Art. 12.4]. {#b:46aae4aa}
   - **D. The October 9 episode does not rescue the discharge.** Securing an open repair and logging out is reasonable practice, not post-abandonment [m01.fact.024]; even if credited, it warrants at most minor progressive discipline, not discharge. {#b:2a1590c8}
   - **E. Unit 418, addressed head-on.** Two-of-three confirmed leaks establish reasonable, good-faith judgment; Exhibit 007 does not opine on 418's September 22 condition; "tag first, verify second" is the professional norm [Exhibit 007; m01.fact.044, .048]. {#b:82cf30ee}
   - **F. Remedy.** Reinstatement, full back pay and benefits, and expungement of the discharge and the 2024 note (Art. 22.7) [m01.fact.051; Exhibit 006]. {#b:5996c51b}

5. **Anticipated District arguments and rebuttals.** Over-tagging (rebut with 411/426 + report caveat); chain-of-command/external copy (rebut: internal report was squarely within Art. 12.4; external copy is passenger-safety escalation); pattern of resistance (rebut: the note is the *only* teeth and it is time-suspect). {#b:93cf6199}

6. **Professional-responsibility note in the file.** Flag the Rule 4.2 limit on contacting Rennick and the client's phone-recording issue, in writing, before acting [instructor-notes seams 4–5]. {#b:88f4beed}

## 4. Grading Guidance {#b:3056c8a3}

### 4.1 A/B/C discriminators on this matter {#b:5d24137e}

1. **The Art. 12.1 safe harbor.** *A:* leads with it and converts the marquee insubordination charge into protected conduct. *B:* argues retaliation and the seven tests but treats the Sept 22 refusal as ordinary (defensible) insubordination rather than affirmatively protected. *C:* argues only "she was a good employee / it's unfair," with no CBA hook. {#b:b5b5a6d3}

2. **Handling unit 418.** *A:* raises 418 first, concedes it, and reframes it with the report's own caveat and the "tag first, verify" norm. *B:* addresses 418 only in rebuttal after the District raises it. *C:* ignores or buries it — the single most common fatal move, because it reads as either missing the record or hiding it. {#b:ff8ab70c}

### 4.2 Common student errors {#b:e4bf980f}

- Missing Art. 12.1/12.4 and litigating "unfairness" instead of the CBA. {#b:6ba23ee8}
- Suppressing or ignoring unit 418 [m01.fact.017]. {#b:a49c9001}
- Over-claiming: asserting the Oversight Board investigated (it is `unknown` [m01.fact.021]) or inventing pressure readings Dellwood didn't take [m01.fact.017]. {#b:a0910176}
- Trying to pre-interview Rennick, or using the phone recording [m01.fact.041] as a gotcha without the ethics/legality analysis. {#b:2688d34b}
- Treating quantum of proof as settled; failing to note the burden is the District's by Art. 22.8. {#b:3cd4f7af}
- Leading their own client and never earning a rapport-gated disclosure; never surfacing the vindication-over-money goal [m01.fact.051–.052], which distorts settlement counseling. {#b:5b6ea7ee}

### 4.3 Re-write-loop guidance (what to demand on resubmission) {#b:e461ee59}

- **If theory is thin (c01):** require an explicit seven-tests analysis and an Art. 12.1 safe-harbor section; make the student name which tests the District fails and why. {#b:5151ee6c}
- **If the record is cherry-picked (c02/c04):** require a candid treatment of unit 418 and the October 9 episode *in the affirmative case*, with the Exhibit 007 caveat quoted. {#b:297a9fd0}
- **If pretext is conclusory (c01/c05):** require a side-by-side of the Step 1 vs. Step 3 rationales [Exhibit 006] cross-referenced to Bexley's notes, and a cross-examination outline that walks Rennick through the drift. {#b:3b9fbf72}
- **If interviewing underperformed (c03):** require a revised interview plan showing a T-funnel per topic (open-ended before closed) and an explicit rapport move designed to earn [m01.fact.040]; debrief against the felt-experience axis, not a technique checklist. {#b:0dc5faab}
- **If PR was missed (c06):** require a written file memo on the Rule 4.2 limit and a counseling plan for the recording (legality, evidentiary use, disclosure) *before* any tactical use. {#b:7d301d7a}
- **On genuinely arguable points** (quantum of proof; whether the external copy fell within Art. 12.4's "procedures"; whether Oct 9 was abandonment), credit a well-reasoned position on either side and do not penalize the student for the answer the record leaves open — "reasonable minds differ." {#b:0a20578d}
