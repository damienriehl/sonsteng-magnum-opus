# Ground-Truth Facts — In re J.C., a Minor (m18)

## The client and her family

Jasmine Cordero, who is sixteen and goes by "Jaz," is a tenth-grade student at Encinal Ridge High School in Encinal Springs, in the County of Alta Mesa, California. [m18.fact.001] She lives with her mother, Ana Cordero, who is the sole parent in the household. [m18.fact.002] Ana works two jobs — a home-health aide on weekdays and weekend shifts at a garden-supply store — and was at work on the evening the incident occurred. [m18.fact.003] Jasmine is an honor-roll student and has never had any prior contact with police. [m18.fact.004] Ana knew that Jasmine's older brother had picked up a juvenile record years earlier, an experience the family remembers as damaging and expensive, and she is frightened this will repeat with her daughter. [m18.fact.005]

## The parking-lot incident

On the evening of Friday, April 10, 2026, at roughly 8:40 p.m., an incident took place in the shared student-and-staff parking lot at Encinal Ridge High School, after a home basketball game had let out. [m18.fact.006] Two of Jasmine's friends — Kayla Trejo, sixteen, and Andre Sotelo, sixteen — spray-painted graffiti on the exterior wall of the gymnasium and on parked cars. [m18.fact.007] The graffiti consisted of block letters, a set of initials, and a crude figure, in black and red paint. [m18.fact.008] Three vehicles were damaged: two were tagged with spray paint, and a third — a teacher's sedan — was also keyed in a long scratch down the driver's side. [m18.fact.009]

Jasmine was in the lot with Kayla and Andre when this happened. [m18.fact.010] Jasmine says she never sprayed anything and that she recorded part of the episode on her phone. [m18.fact.011] She filmed roughly forty seconds of video during the tagging; what that video shows is both important and sensitive. [m18.fact.012]

The damage was discovered the next morning, Saturday, April 11, 2026, by a custodian, who notified the school. [m18.fact.013] The following Monday, April 13, the school administration reviewed the parking-lot security-camera footage. [m18.fact.014] The footage is partial and poor: it shows several figures moving near the cars in dim light but does not clearly show who sprayed which vehicle. [m18.fact.015]

## The school's response and the prior dispute

Assistant Principal Gerald Marchetti said he recognized Jasmine on the footage and pulled her out of class first — before the other two students — on Monday, April 13. [m18.fact.016] Jasmine and Marchetti had a history: in February 2026, Jasmine had filed a written complaint with the district alleging that the dress code was being enforced unfairly against her, arising out of an incident over a headscarf and a graphic tee shirt. [m18.fact.017] Jasmine believes Marchetti singled her out first because of that earlier complaint. [m18.fact.018]

Marchetti questioned Jasmine in his office on April 13 before her mother arrived and before she had any lawyer; the School Resource Officer, Deputy Omar Haddad of the Alta Mesa County Sheriff's Department, was present for part of that conversation. [m18.fact.019] Jasmine answered the questions put to her at school that day without her mother or counsel present. [m18.fact.020] Deputy Haddad authored an incident report dated April 14, 2026. [m18.fact.021] The report names Jasmine, Kayla, and Andre as present and states that Jasmine "appeared to be recording" during the incident. [m18.fact.022]

## What the witnesses saw

A student, Tomás Villareal, was in the lot that night and saw the three of them; he says Jasmine held her phone up the whole time and did not spray anything. [m18.fact.023] Villareal also says it was Andre who keyed the teacher's car. [m18.fact.024] The keyed sedan belongs to a teacher, Priyanka Sundaram, who has provided a repair estimate for the repainting and the key-scratch work. [m18.fact.025] The total property-damage estimate — across the two spray-tagged cars, the keyed sedan, and cleanup of the gym wall — is approximately $2,850. [m18.fact.026] Because the damage is real and was not "recovered" the way a returned item would be, restitution in roughly that amount is a live term in any resolution. [m18.fact.027]

## The juvenile proceeding

The matter was referred to Alta Mesa County Probation and the District Attorney's juvenile unit. [m18.fact.028] A wardship petition under Welfare & Institutions Code § 602 was filed on May 4, 2026, captioned *In re J.C., a Minor*, alleging conduct that would be vandalism if committed by an adult. [m18.fact.029] The petition was assigned to the Juvenile Division of the Superior Court of California, County of Alta Mesa. [m18.fact.030] Jasmine appeared at her first hearing on May 12, 2026, and the allegations were denied on her behalf. [m18.fact.031]

Kayla Trejo has been charged separately and is represented by the Alta Mesa County Public Defender. [m18.fact.032] Andre Sotelo has also been referred and charged separately. [m18.fact.033]

## The diversion offer

On May 26, 2026, the District Attorney's juvenile unit, through Probation, offered an informal diversion resolution — an accountability program with community service, restitution, and a class — that would avoid any adjudication or wardship if Jasmine completes it successfully. [m18.fact.034] The deadline to accept or decline the diversion offer is June 19, 2026. [m18.fact.035] Diversion of this kind typically expects the minor to acknowledge responsibility for the conduct, which is in tension with Jasmine's position that she did not vandalize anything. [m18.fact.036]

## The engagement

Ana Cordero contacted Ellingboe & Ravndal LLP on April 17, 2026, and retained the firm to represent Jasmine. [m18.fact.037] Ana was at work during the incident itself and has no first-hand knowledge of what happened in the lot. [m18.fact.038] Ana felt strongly that Jasmine had been questioned at school before she could get there and without a lawyer, and that this was unfair. [m18.fact.039] Ana quietly blames herself for working so much and not being home the night the incident happened. [m18.fact.051]

## The sensitive facts

Several facts sit below the surface and shape the representation.

The forty-second phone video still exists on Jasmine's device; she has not shown it to anyone. It tends to show that she did not spray anything, but it also shows her laughing and, at moments, egging the others on. [m18.fact.040] Separately, the night before the incident, Jasmine had sent a message in the friends' group chat suggesting the school parking lot as "the spot," and she deleted that message afterward; she is terrified it will surface and make her look like a planner. [m18.fact.041]

Jasmine has been having panic attacks since the incident and has not told her mother how frightened she really is. [m18.fact.042] She also knows that Andre keyed Ms. Sundaram's car specifically out of spite because the teacher had failed him, and she has not wanted to be the one to name him. [m18.fact.043]

## What the co-respondent knows

Kayla Trejo, through her own counsel, has said she has a lawyer and was told not to talk about the case. [m18.fact.044] If pressed, Kayla will admit only that she left before police arrived and will not discuss the merits. [m18.fact.045] Privately, Kayla knows that Jasmine never sprayed anything, but she has not told investigators so — she is focused on her own case. [m18.fact.046] Kayla does not actually know what Jasmine's phone video shows and is uneasy about it. [m18.fact.047]

## The prosecution's posture

The District Attorney's office views Jasmine's phone video as potentially key evidence and, if diversion is refused and the matter is contested, may seek to subpoena the phone; at least one victim, Ms. Sundaram, is pressing for full restitution. [m18.fact.048]

## Open questions in the file

Jasmine does not know whether Kayla or Andre have been formally charged or what either of them told the school or police. [m18.fact.049] The security footage does not resolve who sprayed which car, and no single witness saw the entire sequence; the case turns on how a factfinder reads Jasmine's presence, her filming, and her stated intent.
