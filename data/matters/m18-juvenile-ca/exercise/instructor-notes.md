# Instructor Notes — In re J.C., a Minor (m18)

*Teaching notes only. Not for student distribution. No answer key is provided. Statutory pointers below are for the instructor and were verified against official California sources at publish; confirm currency before class.*

## What this matter teaches

A California juvenile-delinquency intake and disposition workup in which the central drama is **participation and intent**: the client was present and filming but says she never applied paint or keyed a car. The pedagogy braids fact investigation from a frightened minor, counseling a distressed client and parent, a real diversion-versus-hearing decision, and a live Rule 4.2 professional-responsibility trap.

## Research pointers (instructor-verified; California)

- **Juvenile court law generally:** Welfare & Institutions Code, Part 1, Chapter 2 (Juvenile Court Law); wardship petitions under **Welf. & Inst. Code § 602**. Records confidential; minor identified by initials.
- **Informal diversion / supervision:** the informal-supervision and diversion framework (see Welf. & Inst. Code §§ 654, 654.2, and related sections) — eligibility, terms, effect of completion, and non-completion consequences. Have students locate the current provisions themselves.
- **Underlying offense:** vandalism (Cal. Penal Code § 594) and the damage-amount thresholds; aiding-and-abetting / mere-presence principles for the "she only filmed" theory.
- **Statements and questioning of a minor at school:** have students research the rules on custodial interrogation and questioning of minors, and the effect of questioning by an administrator and an SRO without a parent or counsel present.
- **Juvenile restitution:** Welf. & Inst. Code § 730.6 framework; note the apportionment problem where the largest damage (the keyed car) is attributed by the eyewitness to a co-participant.
- **Professional responsibility:** California's no-contact rule — **Cal. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2** — as applied to the represented co-participant, Kayla Trejo; plus confidentiality (Rule 1.6) and the duties owed to a minor client with a parent participating.

## Teaching pivots

- **The rapport-gated facts.** Jasmine only reveals the surviving phone video, her panic attacks, and Andre's spiteful keying after the proper triggers (confidentiality reassurance, non-judgment, acknowledged emotion, following a hint, explaining the process). Students who pressure or flatter should not unlock them — flag this in debrief.
- **The double-edged video.** The video helps (she didn't spray) and hurts (she's laughing and encouraging). Use it to teach counseling on honesty and the strategic downside of putting the client's own recording in play.
- **Rule 4.2.** Kayla holds the exculpatory fact that Jasmine never sprayed, which makes the temptation to contact her acute. The correct move is to route through the public defender. Surface this as the professional-responsibility centerpiece.
- **Selective identification.** The prior dress-code complaint and the single identifying administrator invite an argument about suggestive/biased identification and selective enforcement — good material for the contested-hearing branch.
- **Restitution as leverage.** Because Jasmine's role is disputed and the keying is attributed to Andre, apportioned restitution is negotiable; connect this to the diversion terms.
