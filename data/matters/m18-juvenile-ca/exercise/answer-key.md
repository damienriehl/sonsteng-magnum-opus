## 1. Model Case Analysis (keyed to fact anchors)

### 1.1 Posture
The firm represents **the minor, Jasmine ("Jaz") Cordero (J.C.)** [role `m18.role.juvenile-defense-counsel`] on a Welfare & Institutions Code § 602 wardship petition filed May 4, 2026, alleging conduct that would be vandalism if committed by an adult [m18.fact.029], assigned to the Juvenile Division, Superior Court, County of Alta Mesa [m18.fact.030]. Allegations were denied May 12 [m18.fact.031]. A diversion offer is on the table with a **June 19 accept/decline deadline** [m18.fact.034]–[m18.fact.035]. Delinquency runs under the WIC, not the adult Penal Code process; the minor is captioned by initials and records are confidential (Welf. & Inst. Code § 602; Juvenile Court Law, Part 1, Ch. 2) `[instructor: verify before teaching]`.

### 1.2 Central issue — participation vs. mere presence (rubric c01/c03)
Jasmine was present and **filming** but says she never sprayed or keyed anything [m18.fact.011]. The two friends did the painting [m18.fact.007]; Andre keyed the teacher's sedan [m18.fact.024], [m18.fact.043]. Under California aiding-and-abetting law (People v. Beeman, 35 Cal.3d 547 (1984)), **mere presence, knowledge, or failure to prevent a crime is not enough** — the People must prove she acted with knowledge of the perpetrator's unlawful purpose and the intent to encourage or facilitate it `[instructor: verify before teaching]`. A model answer stresses the proof gaps: the surveillance is dim and does not show who did what [m18.fact.015], [m18.fact.050]; the one neutral eyewitness (Villareal) says she only held up her phone and never sprayed, and attributes the keying to Andre [m18.fact.023]–[m18.fact.024]. **But** the concealed and rapport-gated facts complicate innocence: the phone video shows her laughing and at moments egging the others on [m18.fact.040], and the deleted group-chat message the night before suggested the parking lot as "the spot" [m18.fact.041] — either could be spun as encouragement/planning. The vandalism elements and damage-amount thresholds (Cal. Penal Code § 594) are the substantive backbone `[instructor: verify before teaching]`.

### 1.3 Diversion vs. contested jurisdictional hearing (rubric c03, 55 pts)
- **Diversion (informal supervision/diversion; Welf. & Inst. Code §§ 654/654.2 framework)** `[instructor: verify before teaching]`: on successful completion, **no adjudication, no wardship, dismissal** — exactly Jasmine's stated priority of avoiding a record [m18.fact.034], [m18.fact.036]. The tension: diversion typically expects an **acknowledgment of responsibility**, which collides with her innocence claim [m18.fact.036].
- **Contested hearing** preserves the innocence claim and exploits the proof gaps, but risks a true finding and the very record she fears. A model answer states best-case (dismissal/reduced terms via negotiation given weak proof) and worst-case (adjudication + wardship + full restitution) and centers the child's priorities: no record, not naming her friends, being believed [m18.fact.049], persona objectives.
- **Restitution as leverage (Welf. & Inst. Code § 730.6):** total damage ~$2,850 [m18.fact.026], the largest slice being the keyed sedan attributed to Andre [m18.fact.024]. Because Jasmine's role is disputed and the biggest harm is someone else's doing, **apportioned** restitution is negotiable [m18.fact.027] `[instructor: verify before teaching]`. Strong answers tie the restitution position to the participation dispute.

### 1.4 Statements & identification (contested-hearing branch)
Jasmine was questioned at school April 13 by Assistant Principal Corvino and SRO Deputy Haddad **before her mother arrived and without counsel** [m18.fact.019]–[m18.fact.020]. Students should research custodial-interrogation and school-questioning rules for minors, including the age-as-a-factor analysis of J.D.B. v. North Carolina, 564 U.S. 261 (2011), and In re Gault, 387 U.S. 1 (1967) juvenile due-process guarantees `[instructor: verify before teaching]`. The **suggestive/biased-identification and selective-enforcement** angle: Corvino pulled Jasmine first [m18.fact.016], and she had filed a February dress-code complaint against him [m18.fact.017]–[m18.fact.018] — a motive to single her out that a model answer preserves for the hearing branch.

### 1.5 The hard facts to confront in counseling
The surviving phone video is **double-edged** [m18.fact.040]; the deleted "the spot" text [m18.fact.041] could surface (the DA may subpoena the phone [m18.fact.048]); her panic attacks [m18.fact.042] bear on her wellbeing and decision capacity; and she knows Andre keyed the car out of spite [m18.fact.043] but does not want to name a friend. A model answer counsels honestly on the strategic downside of putting her own recording in play and on candor in any accountability process, without pressuring a frightened child.

**RULE 4.2 — the centerpiece trap.** Kayla is a represented co-participant (Alta Mesa County Public Defender). Under Cal. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2 `[instructor: verify before teaching]`, defense counsel **may not communicate with her** about the subject of the representation without her PD's consent — route any inquiry through the public defender. The temptation is acute precisely because Kayla holds the **exculpatory fact** that Jasmine never sprayed [m18.fact.046]. A student who contacts Kayla directly trips the teaching moment and loses the c04.s01 points. The correct move: seek the fact through her counsel, through Villareal's testimony, or through the phone video.

## 4. Grading Guidance

- **Grade the interview, not the extraction.** With a frightened sixteen-year-old, whether the rapport-gated facts were *earned* through confidentiality reassurance, non-judgment, acknowledged emotion, following hints, and explaining the process is the whole assessment. Pressure or flattery that "works" is a debrief red flag.
- **Rule 4.2 is the centerpiece and a bright line.** Any direct contact with Kayla is a hard miss on c04.s01, however tempting her exculpatory knowledge. Credit students who route through the PD or reach the fact via Villareal/the video.
- **Hold both sides of the video.** Top students see the recording helps (she didn't spray) *and* hurts (laughing/hyping) [m18.fact.040] and counsel on the strategic downside of putting it in play — rather than treating it as a silver bullet.
- **Mere presence ≠ guilt.** Reward the Beeman aiding-abetting framing and the argument that presence + filming is not participation, tied to the proof gaps.
- **Diversion is a genuine dilemma.** Do not reward a reflexive "take the deal" or "always fight." Credit answers that weigh the responsibility-acknowledgment collision with the innocence claim against the no-record benefit, on the child's stated priorities.
- **Selective enforcement / suggestive ID** and the **un-counseled school questioning** are live for the hearing branch — credit students who preserve them.
- **Two-axis debrief** per `docs/research/interview-pedagogy.md`: Axis A fact coverage (+ Rule 4.2 flag) and Axis B relational experience rated in character; a Rule 4.2 violation drives the signed ethics item negative.
