# Instructor Notes — m11 Employment Arbitration (INSTRUCTOR ONLY) {#b:61e1f4da}

*Not for distribution to students. Teaching notes only — no answer key. Citations below are navigational starting points, instructor-verified at publish against the official Illinois sources in `data/jurisdictions/real/il.json`; confirm currency before teaching.* {#b:09952d2c}

## What this exercise is drilling {#b:4b5586a4}

- The pivot from court to **private arbitration**: the parties' agreement supplies procedure; the arbitrator decides fact and law; a court's role is limited to compelling arbitration and confirming/vacating an award. Students should feel how the forum changes strategy (streamlined record, employer pays the neutral, class waiver). {#b:f37da1f5}
- A **retaliation / wrongful-discharge** proof problem built on timing and pretext, with a documentary chronology that lets the causal story be assembled from exhibits. {#b:cd7e3087}
- A live **Rule 4.2 no-contact** teaching moment: the director of operations (Pfeiffer) and HR partner (Croll) are the represented employer's people; students must route contact through opposing counsel or discovery, not interview Pfeiffer directly. {#b:8918c032}

## Navigational research pointers (instructor-verified) {#b:53eeea48}

- **Arbitration enforceability / confirming or vacating an award:** Illinois Uniform Arbitration Act, 710 ILCS 5; the Federal Arbitration Act, 9 U.S.C. §§ 1–16, frames enforceability and the interstate-commerce question for a logistics employer. Note the FAA § 1 transportation-worker exemption is worth having students test against these facts. {#b:0e2577c5}
- **Retaliatory discharge (Illinois common law)** and statutory retaliation protections; wage-and-hour retaliation and off-the-clock pay under the Illinois Wage Payment and Collection Act and the Illinois Minimum Wage Law; safety-complaint protection. Let students find and frame the elements themselves. {#b:7fa2d8f1}
- **Professional conduct:** Ill. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2 (communication with a represented person). Discuss the managerial-employee scope for an organizational party. {#b:39996547}

## Chronology backbone (for grading the students' chronology) {#b:857dc505}

- 2025-09 verbal complaint to Pfeiffer → 2025-11-05 fire-exit photo → 2025-11-10 written HR complaint → 2025-11-12 Croll forwards to Pfeiffer → 2025-11-18 state hotline → 2025-12-05 PIP → 2026-01-08 RIF memo → 2026-01-09 termination → within 6 weeks, reposting. {#b:5a130af9}
- The November 12 internal forward (Exhibit m11.exh.004) is the knowledge linchpin against the employer's ignorance defense. {#b:dacac76b}

## Facilitation cautions {#b:34f1c1cd}

- The client persona holds three rapport-gated facts (blacklisting fear, private notebook, the August timecard edit) and one concealed fact (the October job application). Coach students that pressure never unlocks these — only the emotional preconditions do. {#b:6aa47144}
- Keep student drafts facts-only; the legal research is the point. {#b:0caafbcb}

## Teaching arc (suggested sequence) {#b:db2f3cf9}

- **Session 1 — Intake & rapport (client interview).** Students run the Adeyemi persona. The teaching objective is the T-funnel: broad open questions before closed gap-fillers, a genuine wellbeing inquiry, and *following hints* rather than steamrolling. Debrief on which rapport-gated facts they earned and why. {#b:694cd288}
- **Session 2 — Fact development & the 4.2 trap.** Witness interviews (Ruvalcaba, Nyarko) plus the deliberate Rule 4.2 moment: students who try to interview Pfeiffer or Croll get the coaching flag. Build the exhibit-anchored chronology. {#b:45a151c8}
- **Session 3 — Theory & forum.** Retaliation elements + the arbitration-enforceability layer (class waiver, and the FAA § 1 transportation-worker sleeper). This is the intellectual peak of the matter. {#b:be6497e9}
- **Session 4 — Written advocacy.** Draft the statement of the case (facts-only, exhibit cites) and the preliminary memo (where the law lives). {#b:e0140ffb}
- **Session 5 — Hearing & counseling.** Opening/cross/closing built on documents, then settlement counseling toward the client's real (non-monetary) objectives. {#b:16f2be12}

## Timing (rough clock) {#b:b3545e05}

- Client interview + debrief: ~90 min. {#b:02b15b83}
- Witness interviews + chronology: ~90 min. {#b:6b9da266}
- Theory/forum workshop: ~60–75 min. {#b:0316805c}
- Statement-of-the-case drafting: out-of-class, ~4–6 hrs; in-class critique ~45 min. {#b:13785d6d}
- Hearing simulation + settlement counseling: ~120 min. {#b:a958b4b6}

## Discussion questions {#b:577f4f32}

1. What single document defeats the employer's ignorance defense, and why can't the case rest on Pfeiffer admitting knowledge? {#b:572cb060}
2. Do the employer's two rationales (failed PIP + RIF) reinforce or undercut each other? What evidence turns the RIF into pretext? {#b:a03ecb1d}
3. If FAA § 1 exempts this warehouse supervisor, what happens to the forum — and does that help or hurt the client's real objectives? {#b:f4541736}
4. Is the class-action waiver enforceable, and what does that do to the crew-wide unpaid-wage recovery Adeyemi wants? {#b:4b3315ab}
5. The client's top priority is a neutral reference, not maximum damages. How should that reshape the settlement posture versus pressing to a hearing? {#b:55530146}
6. Why is pressuring the client for the timecard-edit fact both an ethics/relational failure and a strategic one? {#b:62a59885}
