# Instructor Notes — m15 (Wenzloff DWI Defense) {#b:0a7db4f9}

*Teaching notes only. Not for student distribution. Citations below are instructor-verified at publish against the official Minnesota sources; verify currency before each term.* {#b:aaedf4fd}

## What this exercise is really testing {#b:8b2f5d5a}

The pedagogical core is whether the student keeps the **commercial-license consequence** at the center of the whole representation. A student who treats this like an ordinary first-offense DWI misses the point: for a CDL holder the disqualification — one year for a first qualifying offense, lifetime for a second — is the client's real jeopardy, and the prior offense makes a second disqualification live. The best work drives every decision (suppression posture, plea target, timing) toward protecting the CDL, and treats jail exposure as secondary because that is what the client actually needs. {#b:d32687c0}

The second core is **rapport-earned fact development**. Four sensitive facts are gated: the fatigue-and-phone distraction behind the weaving, the mouthwash used to mask the beer smell, the uncounted third drink, and the concealed prior offense. Strong students earn these through open-ended invitation, wellbeing questions, and nonjudgmental responses — not pressure. Note the pedagogical tension: the mouthwash and the third drink are bad facts for the impairment picture but good facts for the mouth-alcohol theory, and the distraction admission cuts against the "the stop had no basis" argument while supporting an innocent explanation for the driving. Students should counsel around this, not bury it. {#b:e64a0b49}

## Key authorities for instructor reference (verify before citing) {#b:4f251cc2}

- **Impaired-driving offense and degrees:** Minn. Stat. ch. 169A — 169A.20 (driving while impaired); per-se concentration 0.08 (169A.20, subd. 1(5)); degrees at 169A.24–.27 (third degree = one aggravating factor, e.g., a qualifying prior impaired-driving incident within ten years, 169A.03, subd. 3 and 22). {#b:6e46f4e0}
- **Implied consent and license revocation:** Minn. Stat. § 169A.51 (chemical testing); § 169A.52–.53 (revocation and judicial review; note the short petition window); observation-period foundation for breath testing is developed in Minnesota case law. {#b:3032c295}
- **Commercial driver consequences:** Minn. Stat. ch. 171 (disqualification of commercial driving privileges) — one-year and lifetime disqualification provisions; confirm current text. {#b:05ec915a}
- **Stop / reasonable suspicion:** Fourth Amendment and Minn. Const. art. I, § 10; the tip-plus-corroboration analysis (reliability of an identified citizen informant vs. an anonymous tip; officer's own corroboration). Point students to current Minnesota Supreme Court and Court of Appeals decisions on informant tips and traffic stops. {#b:e29f61a2}
- **Field sobriety and breath-test reliability:** Minn. R. Evid. 702 foundation; mouth-alcohol and measurement-uncertainty challenges to the DataMaster; the pre-test observation period. {#b:3a81a53a}
- **No-contact rule (the Kallestad teaching moment):** Minn. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2. {#b:035279f9}
- Research pointers and official sources are in `data/jurisdictions/real/mn.json`; DWI offenses are collected in Minn. Stat. ch. 169A. {#b:10a57dd4}

## Discussion prompts {#b:f2060319}

- Which proceeding do you fight first — the criminal charge or the implied-consent revocation — and why do the clocks matter? {#b:8eae56c5}
- Is a single fog-line contact over a mile and a half, on top of an identified citizen's weaving report, enough for reasonable suspicion? What does the dashcam add or subtract? {#b:93c23302}
- How do the mouthwash, the alcohol-based cough syrup, reflux, and a possibly interrupted observation period combine into a mouth-alcohol theory at 0.09? {#b:f440a7ee}
- How should counsel counsel a client who omitted a prior DWI, when that prior is the whole reason his CDL is in lifetime jeopardy? {#b:fa621e35}

## Teaching arc (suggested sequence) {#b:dc78c569}

- **Session 1 — Client interview (the ashamed provider).** Students interview Wenzloff. The objective is to earn all four sensitive facts — the distraction, the mouthwash, the third drink, and (hardest) the prior DWI — through open invitation and non-judgment. Debrief on how their reaction to the shameful facts unlocked or shut down the truth, and on the double-edged nature of each fact. {#b:eda4a107}
- **Session 2 — Issue-spotting workshop.** Separate the criminal charge from the implied-consent revocation; build the four defense threads. Anchor the field-test and breath-test attacks to the BWC/dashcam and the observation-period gap. {#b:ff10e23a}
- **Session 3 — Suppression theory.** Draft the omnibus/suppression posture, leading with the mouth-alcohol/observation-period theory paired with the client's own facts. {#b:9c415d3a}
- **Session 4 — Counseling & the CDL.** Fill the client's license-timeline knowledge gap; explain the two clocks; set expectations honestly given the prior. {#b:140e8c1d}
- **Session 5 — Plea negotiation.** Build a negotiation plan targeting the CDL disqualification trigger, not jail/fine. {#b:70b562c6}
