# Ground-Truth Facts — State of Minnesota v. Travis O. Wenzloff (m15) {#b:3339d55e}

*Master narrative for the DWI defense exercise. This file is the single source of truth; every persona disclosure and rubric evidence item anchors back to a fact number here. Student-facing packets stay facts-only — no legal citations.* {#b:be378043}

## The client and what is at stake {#b:ad38e02a}

Travis O. Wenzloff is forty-three years old and works as a commercial HVAC technician for Cedarloop Mechanical Services, Inc., a heating-and-cooling contractor based in Big Lake, Sherburne County [m15.fact.001]. He holds a Class A commercial driver's license, and he drives a company service truck to job sites every working day; the CDL is the credential that makes him employable [m15.fact.002]. He is married with two school-age children and is the household's primary earner [m15.fact.003]. {#b:e06bde50}

## The night of the double shift {#b:29b6134f}

On Friday, January 17, 2025, the region was in a hard cold snap, and Travis worked a double [m15.fact.004]. He covered his scheduled service route and then took two emergency furnace calls after hours as failing furnaces stacked up in the subzero weather; by the time he finished the last call his day ran close to fourteen hours and he was exhausted [m15.fact.005]. On his way home he stopped at the Longspur Tap House and, by his own first account, had two beers over about two hours while he ate a burger and watched the end of a game [m15.fact.006]. {#b:15c5a82a}

There is a detail Travis does not volunteer and leaves out of his own count: near the end of the night he had a third beer, so "two beers" understates what he actually drank [m15.fact.007]. There is a second detail he is embarrassed to raise: before he pulled out of the parking lot he swished an alcohol-based mouthwash in the truck to cover the smell of beer, which means he knew he had been drinking and was thinking about how he would come across [m15.fact.008]. He had also been fighting a chest cold that week and, earlier that evening, had taken a dose of a prescription-strength cough syrup that carries an alcohol base [m15.fact.009]. Separately, Travis has diagnosed acid reflux — gastroesophageal reflux disease — that brings stomach contents and gas up into his mouth, especially when he is tired or has eaten late [m15.fact.010]. He left the tap house to drive home at about 10:45 p.m. [m15.fact.011]. {#b:17ab3164}

## The stop {#b:e8754b72}

Travis was tired and, as he drove the highway stretch toward home, he glanced down more than once at his phone to check the address for his first job the next morning; that distraction — not alcohol, in his telling — is why his truck drifted [m15.fact.012]. At about 10:50 p.m., another motorist, Corinne Vandersteen, called 911 to report a dark pickup weaving on Highway 10 [m15.fact.013]. Deputy Reid Kallestad of the Sherburne County Sheriff's Office located a pickup matching the description, fell in behind it, and followed for roughly a mile and a half [m15.fact.014]. Kallestad reported that he watched the truck touch the fog line once, and he initiated a traffic stop at 10:58 p.m. [m15.fact.015]. On contact, the deputy wrote that he detected an odor of alcohol, that Travis's eyes were glassy and watery, and that Travis fumbled while retrieving his license [m15.fact.016]. {#b:461a818b}

Kallestad had Travis step out to perform standardized field sobriety tests on the shoulder, where the temperature was around four degrees below zero, the wind was up, and the ground was packed with snow and ice [m15.fact.017]. Travis said he was freezing and asked whether the tests could be done somewhere else; the request was not accommodated and the tests went ahead where they stood [m15.fact.018]. The deputy administered the horizontal-gaze-nystagmus check, the walk-and-turn, and the one-leg-stand, recorded what he scored as multiple clues of impairment, and formed the opinion that Travis was impaired [m15.fact.019]. Fatigue from the fourteen-hour day, the extreme cold, and Travis's reflux each bear on how much those balance tests can carry [m15.fact.020]. {#b:0948aed9}

## Arrest and the breath test {#b:1d911f04}

After a preliminary roadside breath test, Kallestad placed Travis under arrest and transported him to the Sherburne County Jail [m15.fact.021]. At the jail the evidentiary breath test was run by Sergeant Alina Petrossian, a certified DataMaster operator [m15.fact.022]. A continuous fifteen-minute observation period is supposed to precede the test so that nothing from the mouth contaminates the sample, and it is an open question on this record whether the operator actually watched Travis without interruption for the full period [m15.fact.023]. During that window Travis burped and felt his reflux come up, and both the mouthwash and the alcohol-based cough syrup were still recent — each of which raises a mouth-alcohol concern about the reading [m15.fact.024]. The DataMaster reported an alcohol concentration of 0.09 [m15.fact.025]. {#b:940a3ea1}

## The charges, the license, and the CDL {#b:6fc435f7}

Travis was read the implied-consent advisory and served with a notice that his driver's license would be revoked, and his commercial driving privileges are exposed as well [m15.fact.026]. There is a fact Travis does not offer up: about seven years earlier he had a prior impaired-driving offense, which sits before the events in this file but is close enough in time to raise the stakes and to threaten a lifetime disqualification of his CDL [m15.fact.027]. Because of that prior, the State charged the current offense as Third-Degree Driving While Impaired rather than at the lowest level [m15.fact.028]. Alongside the criminal charge, a separate civil proceeding to revoke his license runs on its own clock through the implied-consent process [m15.fact.029]. For a commercial driver, a first impaired-driving disqualification takes the CDL for a year and a second takes it for life, so the license consequence — not jail — is the catastrophe Travis fears most [m15.fact.030]. His truck was towed and he was released the next morning [m15.fact.031]. {#b:3fdcdaba}

## Engagement and posture {#b:f2fe65ad}

Travis retained Ellingboe & Ravndal LLP on January 22, 2025, meeting first with Amara Ellingboe [m15.fact.032]. The firm agreed to a flat fee of $3,000 to carry the representation through the pretrial and contested-omnibus stage, the implied-consent license challenge, and any plea negotiation, and Travis deposited a $3,000 advance into the firm's trust account against that fee [m15.fact.033]. A not-guilty plea was entered, the defense served a demand for discovery, filed a timely petition for judicial review of the implied-consent revocation, and requested a contested omnibus hearing on probable cause and suppression in the Minnesota District Court for Sherburne County [m15.fact.034]. {#b:20ebd520}

The defense is developing four threads: that the stop lacked reasonable suspicion, turning on how reliable the citizen tip was and how much the deputy corroborated on his own; that the field sobriety tests are unreliable when performed in extreme cold, on ice, by an exhausted man with a reflux condition; that the 0.09 reading is vulnerable on mouth-alcohol and margin-of-error grounds and could reflect a true value below the per-se line; and, failing suppression, a negotiated resolution that protects the commercial license [m15.fact.035]. {#b:dfc97e4b}

## What the other witnesses say {#b:65a05cf8}

Corinne Vandersteen, the motorist who called 911, describes a dark pickup drifting within its lane and crossing the fog line, but she was two or three cars back and is not certain the truck that was ultimately stopped was the same one she watched [m15.fact.036]. Gwen Skovgaard, the service manager at Cedarloop Mechanical, confirms that Travis worked the double that day, that the emergency furnace calls were real subzero jobs, and that a technician who loses his CDL cannot keep his position [m15.fact.037]. Marisol Zavaleta, the bartender at the Longspur Tap House, recalls serving Travis a burger and, as she remembers it, two beers, and says he seemed worn out but not drunk when he left [m15.fact.038]. {#b:0787bb4e}

Deputy Kallestad's squad dashcam captured the stretch he followed and the stop, and his body-worn camera captured the field sobriety tests on the icy shoulder [m15.fact.039]. The DataMaster's stated measurement uncertainty could place the true value below 0.08, and a mouth-alcohol event during the observation period could have inflated the reading [m15.fact.040]. Travis's driving record shows the earlier impaired-driving offense and an otherwise clean recent history, with the Class A commercial endorsement noted [m15.fact.041]. The 911 dispatch log timestamps the citizen call at 10:50 p.m. and the stop at 10:58 p.m. [m15.fact.042]. {#b:7e6cb049}

The prosecution is handled by the Sherburne County Attorney's Office, which represents the State [m15.fact.043]. Travis's overriding, non-legal goal is simple: keep the CDL and the job, protect his family's income, and stay out of jail [m15.fact.044]. As of the simulation date, the omnibus hearing is pending, the implied-consent review is pending, no plea has been reached, and no fee has been drawn from the trust advance [m15.fact.045]. {#b:d9502ab3}
