# Ground-Truth Facts — Northlake Surgical Instruments, Inc. v. Adaeze Okwuosa {#b:9e40cab6}

*Internal master narrative for matter m06. Not student-facing. Every material fact carries a sequential anchor `[m06.fact.NNN]`. Personas, exhibits, and the rubric reference these anchors. Jurisdiction: State of Meridian, District Court, Halden County.* {#b:9f37224b}

## The parties and the covenant {#b:43c7705e}

Adaeze Okwuosa is a forty-one-year-old medical-device sales engineer who worked for Northlake Surgical Instruments, Inc. for twelve years before resigning in early 2026 [m06.fact.001]. Northlake is a privately held company headquartered in Norsholm, Halden County, that designs and sells orthopedic surgical implants and the specialized instrument trays hospitals use to install them [m06.fact.002]. Okwuosa started as a junior territory representative in 2014 and was promoted twice, finishing as Northlake's senior sales engineer for the Lake Verdant metro accounts — the company's largest and most profitable territory [m06.fact.003]. {#b:24eb8d2c}

On her first day in 2014, Okwuosa signed an employment agreement that included a non-competition and non-solicitation covenant; no one walked her through it, and she says she was told it was "standard paperwork everyone signs" [m06.fact.004]. The non-compete clause bars her, for one year after leaving, from working for any competing medical-device company anywhere in Northlake's four-state sales region [m06.fact.005]. A separate non-solicitation clause bars her for eighteen months from soliciting any Northlake customer she serviced or learned about while employed [m06.fact.006]. The agreement recites that it is supported by her at-will employment and that she would receive access to "confidential customer and pricing information" [m06.fact.007]. {#b:79395e15}

## Why she left {#b:881740de}

In the spring of 2025, Okwuosa was passed over for the open regional sales-manager position, which went to a colleague with less tenure and a weaker sales record [m06.fact.008]. She took the decision hard and began quietly interviewing in the fall of 2025 [m06.fact.009]. In December 2025 she accepted an offer from Verdant Medical Systems, LLC, a competitor headquartered in Elleston, Marrick County [m06.fact.010]. Her new role at Verdant is in a different product line — flexible endoscopy and gastrointestinal-suite equipment — and covers a different geographic territory than she worked at Northlake [m06.fact.011]. Okwuosa gave notice and resigned effective January 5, 2026 [m06.fact.012]. {#b:62a652d4}

## The pricing spreadsheet {#b:b2dfe25d}

Throughout her time at Northlake, Okwuosa relied on a master customer-pricing spreadsheet that she used almost daily to prepare quotes [m06.fact.013]. The spreadsheet lists, hospital by hospital, the negotiated per-unit pricing, volume-discount tiers, contract-renewal dates, and the name of each facility's purchasing contact [m06.fact.014]. Northlake maintained the spreadsheet on a shared network drive; in practice, most of the sales team could open it, and reps routinely emailed slices of it to one another and to inside sales [m06.fact.015]. Northlake never labeled the file "confidential," required no separate password to open it, and had reps sign only the general employment agreement, not any document-specific acknowledgment [m06.fact.016]. {#b:002ae6fd}

In the last week of December 2025, before her resignation took effect, Okwuosa copied the entire master pricing spreadsheet onto a personal USB drive [m06.fact.017]. She also forwarded to her personal email one internal message that happened to contain a hospital purchasing contact's direct phone number [m06.fact.018]. Over the years she had also kept a personal notebook of handwritten account notes, commission figures, and reminders about her customers' preferences [m06.fact.019]. Okwuosa's explanation is that she wanted her own commission and expense records and did not think of the pricing file as a company secret because "everyone had it" [m06.fact.020]. {#b:77b3078f}

## Northlake's discovery and response {#b:52046241}

After Okwuosa left, a Northlake IT security analyst ran a routine off-boarding review of her workstation and network activity [m06.fact.021]. The export log showed that a file matching the size and name of the master pricing spreadsheet had been copied to a removable USB device on December 29, 2025 [m06.fact.022]. It also flagged the single forwarded email to her personal account [m06.fact.023]. Northlake's sales leadership concluded she had taken the "customer list" to help her new employer [m06.fact.024]. {#b:d4f97ace}

On January 20, 2026, Northlake's counsel sent Okwuosa a cease-and-desist letter demanding that she return all company data, resign from Verdant, and honor the one-year non-compete [m06.fact.025]. When she did not resign from Verdant, Northlake filed a motion for a temporary injunction in Meridian District Court, Halden County, on February 10, 2026, seeking to enforce the non-compete and non-solicitation covenants and to bar her from any competitive work for a year [m06.fact.026]. The matter is assigned to Hon. Miriam T. Aldous [m06.fact.027]. Okwuosa retained Ellingboe & Ravndal LLP to oppose the injunction on February 16, 2026 [m06.fact.028]. {#b:b374e51c}

## The real business context {#b:03b349b2}

Northlake's stated concern is misappropriation of trade secrets and unfair competition [m06.fact.029]. In fact, Northlake had recently learned it was losing a large multi-year supply bid at a regional hospital system to Verdant, a loss its sales leadership took personally [m06.fact.030]. The injunction would sideline one of Verdant's newest hires during the very quarter that bid is decided [m06.fact.031]. Northlake's director of sales believes the pricing data is the company's crown jewel, even though it is loosely guarded in practice [m06.fact.032]. {#b:10df1863}

Okwuosa maintains that she has not solicited a single Northlake customer since leaving, and that her Verdant role does not call for her to sell to the orthopedic accounts she once served [m06.fact.033]. She is anxious and embarrassed: she cannot afford to lose the new job or be publicly branded a thief, and legal fees frighten her [m06.fact.034]. She does not actually know whether the prices in the spreadsheet are still current or whether a court would treat them as a protectable trade secret [m06.fact.035]. {#b:5f8f3e5e}

## Witnesses and their accounts {#b:0bd2a99d}

Mareike Hollis was Northlake's sales coordinator and worked alongside Okwuosa for six years [m06.fact.036]. Hollis will say that the pricing spreadsheet was passed around freely, lived on a drive that "half the building" could reach, and was emailed to reps and inside-sales staff without a second thought [m06.fact.037]. André Dupont, Northlake's IT security analyst, ran the off-boarding review and prepared the export-log report; he can testify only to what the logs show, not to what Okwuosa did with the file [m06.fact.038]. Curtis Bellweather, the Verdant hiring manager, will say Okwuosa was hired for the endoscopy line and a different territory, was never asked to bring customer data, and was instructed in writing not to use any former employer's confidential information [m06.fact.039]. {#b:3ea8465a}

Dr. Elena Calderaro is a hospital purchasing director who was one of Okwuosa's Northlake customers for years [m06.fact.040]. Calderaro will say Okwuosa was an unusually straight-dealing rep [m06.fact.041]. After Okwuosa moved to Verdant, the two had lunch, but they spoke about family and never about Verdant's products [m06.fact.042]. Calderaro volunteers that it was she who first asked Okwuosa about Verdant — Okwuosa never pitched her [m06.fact.043]. Calderaro has no idea whether the numbers in Northlake's spreadsheet are still accurate, because hospital contracts are renegotiated constantly [m06.fact.044]. {#b:9070a816}

## The legal fight in a nutshell {#b:dab801b0}

The dispute turns on two questions the students must research and argue. First, whether Northlake can meet the standard for a temporary injunction — likelihood of success on the merits, irreparable harm, the balance of equities, and the public interest [m06.fact.045]. Second, whether the loosely guarded pricing spreadsheet is a protectable trade secret at all, and whether a one-year, four-state non-compete is reasonable in scope, geography, and duration or is overbroad and unenforceable [m06.fact.046]. There is also a live professional-responsibility thread: Northlake and its personnel are represented, so defense counsel may not contact Northlake's director of sales directly without permission from Northlake's counsel [m06.fact.047]. Okwuosa's own conduct — copying the file and forwarding the email — is a serious fact defense counsel must confront candidly in advising her about risk and about what an injunction hearing will surface [m06.fact.048]. {#b:a7b353c0}
