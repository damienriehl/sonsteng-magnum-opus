## What the exercise is testing {#b:b5972efe}

This is a restrictive-covenant defense arising on an emergency motion. It exercises legal analysis and reasoning (SK-LP-02), fact gathering (SK-LP-07), counseling (SK-LP-10), litigation (SK-LP-14), professional-responsibility sensitivity (SK-LP-16), and legal drafting (SK-LP-17). The rubric total is 189, matching the master-outline pin for the non-compete shape. {#b:3824aa3d}

## Governing Meridian authorities (instructor-verified) {#b:9afb8098}

- **Temporary injunctions.** Analyze under the Meridian civil-procedure framework for temporary injunctive relief, Mer. R. Civ. P. 65. The familiar four factors — likelihood of success on the merits, irreparable harm, balance of equities, and public interest — should structure the student's memorandum. {#b:41b381e0}
- **Trade secrets.** Meridian Uniform Trade Secrets Act, Mer. Stat. § 512.09 (three-year limitations; discovery rule). The protectability question turns on whether the pricing workbook (a) derives independent economic value from not being generally known and (b) was the subject of reasonable measures to maintain secrecy. The facts (shared drive, no password, no confidential marking, routine emailing — see facts 015–016, 037) are deliberately weak on the reasonable-measures prong. {#b:d1a8da00}
- **Restrictive covenants.** No Meridian statute is provided; students research Meridian common-law reasonableness (scope, geography, duration, legitimate business interest) and whether Meridian courts blue-pencil overbroad covenants. The one-year, four-state non-compete (fact 005) is a strong overbreadth target; the non-solicitation clause (fact 006) is more defensible for the employer but is undercut by the absence of any actual solicitation (facts 033, 042–043). {#b:061e3e36}
- **Professional responsibility.** Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2 (communication with represented persons). Priscilla Danneker is a management-level representative of represented adverse party Northlake; direct contact by defense counsel is barred without opposing counsel's consent. The chat persona fires an in-app 4.2 teaching moment, not a silent block — debrief on whether the student recognized the limit. {#b:2036b63c}

## Teaching arc and the hard fact {#b:34485d35}

The pedagogical spine is the tension between a **sympathetic, overbroad covenant** and a **client who did something bad** (copied the whole file, forwarded an email — facts 017–018). Strong students will (1) attack protectability and covenant overbreadth, (2) separate the weak non-compete from the stronger-for-the-employer solicitation clause and note there is no evidence of solicitation, and (3) counsel candidly about the copying rather than hide it. The rapport-gated client facts (017–019) only surface with confidentiality reassurance and a non-judgmental posture — a counseling lesson in itself. {#b:32b61b9c}

## Common student errors to probe in debrief {#b:5c8e27b5}

- Conflating the trade-secret question with the covenant-enforceability question. {#b:864e0f81}
- Overlooking the Rule 4.2 problem and "interviewing" Danneker directly. {#b:587ada2f}
- Failing to confront the USB copy and email forward in the fact statement (candor to the tribunal and credibility). {#b:4d422195}
- Treating the pricing numbers as obviously secret without engaging the loose-handling and staleness evidence. {#b:d77514cf}

## Teaching arc (session-by-session) {#b:1d8ab71c}

This matter is built to run across roughly three teaching touchpoints. Adapt to your calendar; the sequence matters more than the count. {#b:1f70addd}

- **Session 1 — Intake, interview, and the trust problem.** Students run the client interview against Adaeze Okwuosa (guarded disposition) and review the case file to build a chronology. The pedagogical spine is the tension between a *sympathetic, overbroad covenant* and a *client who did something bad* (the USB copy and the forwarded email). The most damaging facts are rapport-gated and surface only with confidentiality reassurance and a non-judgmental posture — the interview is itself a counseling lesson. End the session by having students self-report which facts they earned and which they never reached. {#b:22e4ec8a}
- **Session 2 — Investigation, ethics gate, and theory of the case.** Students interview the available witnesses (Hollis, Dupont, Bellweather, Calderaro) and hit the Rule 4.2 wall at Priscilla Danneker, the represented director of sales — the app fires a teaching moment, not a silent block. Students separate the trade-secret question (Mer. Stat. § 512.09) from covenant enforceability (Meridian common law — not codified in the canon), and draft the theory of the case. This is where the enforceability/protectability distinction is won or lost. {#b:a33a1906}
- **Session 3 — Drafting and candid counseling debrief.** Students finalize the memorandum opposing the temporary injunction (Mer. R. Civ. P. 65), the witness-contact explanation, and the client-counseling memo. The debrief closes the loop: did the student confront the hard facts, respect the no-contact line, and counsel the frightened client honestly about risk and a possible narrow resolution? {#b:ed6130e1}

## Timing (rough minutes per activity) {#b:f16999c5}

Budget flexes with class size and whether interviews run live in class or as homework. A workable single-block allocation (~150 min) and the take-home split: {#b:f273417d}

- **Client interview (chat engine):** 30–40 min. Guarded client; students must earn the rapport-gated facts. Do not let students rush — the engine will not yield the worst facts to pressure. {#b:ed1a518a}
- **Case-file review and chronology:** 25–30 min. Seven exhibits plus four witness statements; the chronology is a graded deliverable. {#b:93573237}
- **Witness interviews (incl. the Rule 4.2 gate):** 25–30 min. Includes the deliberate stumble at Danneker; leave time to debrief the ethics moment while it is fresh. {#b:49dec7c6}
- **Analysis and drafting (memo + counseling memo):** take-home, 3–5 hrs. Not a class-time activity; the in-class minutes above are the setup. {#b:2ff206f2}
- **Counseling-and-ethics debrief:** 30–40 min. The payoff discussion; use the questions below. {#b:55665c8f}

## Discussion questions (debrief) {#b:0ddd75e5}

1. Okwuosa copied the entire pricing file and forwarded an internal email. How should those facts appear in the statement of facts opposing the injunction — and what is the cost of trying to bury them versus confronting them? {#b:3bcf80b2}
2. Northlake calls the spreadsheet its "crown jewel," yet it lived on a shared drive, carried no password or confidential label, and was emailed around routinely. What turns loosely handled information into — or out of — a protectable trade secret, and who are your best witnesses on that question? {#b:38a053d1}
3. Why is the non-solicitation clause a *stronger* clause for the employer than the non-compete, and why does it still likely fail on this record? What is the danger of arguing "she competes, therefore she solicited"? {#b:42294cb5}
4. You want to know why Northlake really sued now. The person who knows is Priscilla Danneker, the represented director of sales. What may you do, what may you not do, and how do you build a pretext argument without her? {#b:2e293a6a}
5. Your client's overriding goal is to keep her new job, not to win a principle. When does an aggressive fight serve her, and when does an early, narrow resolution serve her better — and how do you counsel a frightened client toward that choice? {#b:16b901a3}
6. Meridian's canon supplies a trade-secret statute but no non-compete statute. How should a lawyer reason — and write — when the governing rule is common law they must research rather than a statute they can cite? {#b:221c3f83}
