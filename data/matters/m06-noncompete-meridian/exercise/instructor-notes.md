# Instructor Notes — m06 Northlake v. Okwuosa (Non-Compete / Trade Secret)

*Instructor-facing only. Never distribute with the student packet. Citations below are to the Meridian canon and are marked instructor-verified-at-publish. No answer key or intentional-error set is included in this build.*

## What the exercise is testing

This is a restrictive-covenant defense arising on an emergency motion. It exercises legal analysis and reasoning (SK-LP-02), fact gathering (SK-LP-07), counseling (SK-LP-10), litigation (SK-LP-14), professional-responsibility sensitivity (SK-LP-16), and legal drafting (SK-LP-17). The rubric total is 189, matching the master-outline pin for the non-compete shape.

## Governing Meridian authorities (instructor-verified)

- **Temporary injunctions.** Analyze under the Meridian civil-procedure framework for temporary injunctive relief, Mer. R. Civ. P. 65. The familiar four factors — likelihood of success on the merits, irreparable harm, balance of equities, and public interest — should structure the student's memorandum.
- **Trade secrets.** Meridian Uniform Trade Secrets Act, Mer. Stat. § 512.09 (three-year limitations; discovery rule). The protectability question turns on whether the pricing workbook (a) derives independent economic value from not being generally known and (b) was the subject of reasonable measures to maintain secrecy. The facts (shared drive, no password, no confidential marking, routine emailing — see facts 015–016, 037) are deliberately weak on the reasonable-measures prong.
- **Restrictive covenants.** No Meridian statute is provided; students research Meridian common-law reasonableness (scope, geography, duration, legitimate business interest) and whether Meridian courts blue-pencil overbroad covenants. The one-year, four-state non-compete (fact 005) is a strong overbreadth target; the non-solicitation clause (fact 006) is more defensible for the employer but is undercut by the absence of any actual solicitation (facts 033, 042–043).
- **Professional responsibility.** Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2 (communication with represented persons). Priscilla Danneker is a management-level representative of represented adverse party Northlake; direct contact by defense counsel is barred without opposing counsel's consent. The chat persona fires an in-app 4.2 teaching moment, not a silent block — debrief on whether the student recognized the limit.

## Teaching arc and the hard fact

The pedagogical spine is the tension between a **sympathetic, overbroad covenant** and a **client who did something bad** (copied the whole file, forwarded an email — facts 017–018). Strong students will (1) attack protectability and covenant overbreadth, (2) separate the weak non-compete from the stronger-for-the-employer solicitation clause and note there is no evidence of solicitation, and (3) counsel candidly about the copying rather than hide it. The rapport-gated client facts (017–019) only surface with confidentiality reassurance and a non-judgmental posture — a counseling lesson in itself.

## Common student errors to probe in debrief

- Conflating the trade-secret question with the covenant-enforceability question.
- Overlooking the Rule 4.2 problem and "interviewing" Danneker directly.
- Failing to confront the USB copy and email forward in the fact statement (candor to the tribunal and credibility).
- Treating the pricing numbers as obviously secret without engaging the loose-handling and staleness evidence.
