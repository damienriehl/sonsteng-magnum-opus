# Answer Key — m06 · Northlake Surgical Instruments, Inc. v. Adaeze Okwuosa

*Instructor-facing model analysis, interview disclosure map, and rubric-mapped grading key. Never distribute with the student packet and never render into the student site. Legal authorities are drawn only from the Meridian canon (`data/jurisdictions/meridian.json`) and marked instructor-verified-at-publish. Where the canon supplies no rule — non-compete common-law reasonableness — this key says so and directs students to research Meridian common law; it does not fabricate a statute. Every factual proposition traces to a `[m06.fact.NNN]` anchor in `facts.md`. Teaching-session logistics (arc, timing, discussion questions) live in the companion `instructor-notes.md`; this file is the substantive key.*

**Posture:** Defense-side exercise. The student is an associate at Ellingboe & Ravndal LLP opposing Northlake's motion for a temporary injunction in Meridian District Court, Halden County [m06.fact.026], before Hon. Miriam T. Aldous [m06.fact.027]. No answer or counterclaim is on file; the injunction motion is the whole immediate battlefield.

**Governing authorities (canon-verified):**
- **Temporary injunctions** — Mer. R. Civ. P. 65. Four factors structure the memo: (1) likelihood of success on the merits, (2) irreparable harm not curable by money, (3) balance of equities/hardships, (4) public interest [m06.fact.045].
- **Trade secrets** — Meridian Uniform Trade Secrets Act, Mer. Stat. § 512.09 (three-year limitations; discovery rule). Protectability requires (a) information deriving independent economic value from not being generally known and (b) reasonable measures to maintain secrecy.
- **Restrictive covenants** — **No Meridian statute is supplied by the canon.** Students must research and argue **Meridian common-law reasonableness** (scope, geography, duration, legitimate business interest) and whether Meridian courts blue-pencil overbroad covenants. Do **not** credit — and do not yourself write — an invented "Mer. Stat." non-compete provision. Honest treatment of this gap is itself a graded competency.
- **Professional responsibility** — Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2 (communication with represented persons).

## 1. Model Case Analysis

### 1.1 The temporary-injunction standard, applied (Mer. R. Civ. P. 65)

Northlake is the movant and bears the burden on all four Rule 65 factors [m06.fact.045]. The defense theory is that Northlake fails on at least the first two and that the last two cut decisively for Okwuosa. An A-grade analysis treats the four factors as *interdependent* — a weak merits showing raises the bar on the equities and vice versa — rather than as a mechanical checklist, and it keeps the covenant-enforceability question analytically separate from the trade-secret question (they travel together in Northlake's rhetoric but are distinct doctrines with distinct elements).

**(a) Likelihood of success — the covenants.** Northlake seeks to enforce two distinct covenants that must be argued separately:
- The **non-compete** bars competitive employment for one year across a four-state region (Meridian plus the three adjacent states in which Northlake sells) [m06.fact.005]; the agreement itself ties the "region" to wherever the company sells [see employment-agreement §7]. This is the defense's strongest target for overbreadth (§1.3).
- The **non-solicitation** covenant bars soliciting, for eighteen months, any customer Okwuosa serviced or learned confidential information about [m06.fact.006]. This clause protects a more legitimate interest (existing customer relationships) and is the harder clause for the defense — but it is undercut by the complete absence of any actual solicitation [m06.fact.033, m06.fact.042, m06.fact.043].

**(b) Likelihood of success — the trade secret.** Northlake styles its motion as trade-secret misappropriation and unfair competition [m06.fact.029] and treats the pricing workbook as its "crown jewel" [m06.fact.032]. Under Mer. Stat. § 512.09 the protectability question turns on economic-value-from-secrecy and **reasonable measures** — and the reasonable-measures prong is where Northlake is weakest (§1.2).

**(c) Irreparable harm.** Northlake's asserted harm is loss of customer relationships and misuse of pricing data. The defense answer: (i) Okwuosa works a **different product line and territory** [m06.fact.011, m06.fact.039], so the feared competitive harm to the orthopedic accounts is speculative; (ii) she has solicited **no one** [m06.fact.033]; (iii) any pricing advantage is illusory because the numbers go stale fast as hospital contracts are constantly renegotiated [m06.fact.035, m06.fact.037, m06.fact.044]; and (iv) to the extent harm exists, lost sales are quantifiable money damages, which by definition are not "irreparable." A sophisticated memo notes that the log shows only a copy event and one forward — no use, no disclosure [m06.fact.038, it-export-log analyst note] — so harm is unproven, not merely reparable.

**(d) Balance of equities.** An injunction sidelines Okwuosa from her new job during the very quarter a large bid is decided [m06.fact.031], destroying her livelihood and branding her a thief [m06.fact.034], to protect a company whose own conduct (loose handling — §1.2) undercuts the interest it claims. The hardship is grossly asymmetric and favors the defense. The record permits the argument that Northlake's true motive is competitive retaliation over a lost supply bid, not protection of a secret [m06.fact.030] — but see the sourcing caution in §1.6.

**(e) Public interest.** Meridian public policy (which students must locate in common law) favors labor mobility and a worker's right to earn a living; over-enforcement of an unread, boilerplate covenant [m06.fact.004] against a rank-and-file salesperson disserves that interest. The public also has no interest in enforcing an overbroad covenant that merely suppresses competition rather than protecting a legitimate interest.

### 1.2 Trade-secret protectability — the deliberately weak reasonable-measures prong (Mer. Stat. § 512.09)

This is the analytical heart of the exercise, and the facts are engineered so the reasonable-measures element fails or nearly fails:
- The workbook lived on a **shared network drive** the whole sales department could open [m06.fact.015]; "half the building" could reach it [m06.fact.037].
- It carried **no "confidential" label** and **no separate password** beyond the general network login [m06.fact.016; pricing-spreadsheet-description; witness-dupont ("on the file itself, no")].
- Reps **routinely emailed slices** of it to one another and to inside sales [m06.fact.015, m06.fact.037].
- Only the general employment agreement — no document-specific acknowledgment — governed it [m06.fact.016].

Corroboration is strong and comes from **Northlake's own people**: Hollis (former sales coordinator, six years, no stake) says she emailed pieces "a hundred times" [m06.fact.036, m06.fact.037], and Dupont, Northlake's own IT analyst, confirms there was no file-level password or encryption [m06.fact.038; witness-dupont]. Even the represented director of sales concedes, when pressed, that it "wasn't locked down the way maybe it should have been" [m06.fact.015 as voiced in danneker persona].

The **economic-value** half is genuinely contestable — negotiated per-facility pricing, discount tiers, renewal dates, and purchasing contacts [m06.fact.014] can have competitive value. The defense's move is (i) concede nothing on value that it need not, and (ii) win on reasonable measures and on staleness: pricing that is renegotiated constantly and out of date within a season [m06.fact.035, m06.fact.037, m06.fact.044] derives little value "from not being generally known." An A-grade paper does **not** overclaim that the data is obviously non-secret; it argues the owner failed the measures the statute requires and that value is doubtful — reasonable minds could find some tabs (contacts, renewal dates) closer to protectable than others.

### 1.3 Non-compete overbreadth vs. the more-defensible non-solicitation clause

**Non-compete (fact 005).** One year is a duration many jurisdictions tolerate; the vulnerability is **geographic and functional breadth**. A four-state ban on working for *any* medical-device, surgical-instrument, or surgical-implant business [employment-agreement §7] sweeps far beyond Northlake's legitimate interest, because Okwuosa's new role is a **different product category** (flexible endoscopy/GI, not orthopedic implants) in a **different territory** [m06.fact.011, m06.fact.039]. The clause therefore restrains competition Okwuosa is not positioned to wage. Students must research **Meridian common law** on (i) reasonableness in scope/geography/duration, (ii) whether a covenant must protect a legitimate business interest rather than merely suppress competition, and (iii) whether Meridian courts **blue-pencil / reform** an overbroad covenant or **refuse it wholesale**. The canon supplies no statute here; a student who invents one, or who assumes the federal or another state's rule, should lose credit and be corrected. The blue-pencil question is genuinely open on this record — flag it as a place reasonable minds and unknown Meridian precedent diverge.

**Non-solicitation (fact 006).** This is the employer's *better* clause: eighteen months, limited to customers Okwuosa serviced or learned about — a recognized legitimate interest in protecting customer relationships. **But** the defense wins it on the facts, not the law: there is **zero evidence of solicitation**. Okwuosa has contacted no Northlake customer [m06.fact.033]; the one arguable contact — lunch with Dr. Calderaro — was social, initiated by Calderaro, and involved no pitch, price, or product [m06.fact.042, m06.fact.043]. Strong students separate these clauses and resist the temptation to treat "she competes" as "she solicited."

### 1.4 The hard fact — the client's own conduct

The defense cannot wish away that, in the last week before her resignation took effect, Okwuosa **copied the entire master pricing spreadsheet to a personal USB drive** [m06.fact.017] and **forwarded one internal email** containing a hospital contact's direct phone number to her personal account [m06.fact.018]; the IT export log documents both, dated December 29 and December 30, 2025 [m06.fact.022, m06.fact.023; it-export-log]. She also kept a personal notebook of account notes and commission math [m06.fact.019]. Her explanation is that she wanted her own commission and expense records and did not regard the file as a secret because "everyone had it" [m06.fact.020].

An A-grade deliverable **confronts these facts candidly** — states them in the fact section, does not bury them — and reframes them:
- The log establishes a copy and a forward and **nothing about use or disclosure**; Dupont expressly disclaims any basis to testify to intent or use [m06.fact.038; it-export-log analyst note].
- No bulk exfiltration, no mass download, no access outside her normal scope [it-export-log finding 3].
- Verdant never asked for the data, put her on a different line and territory, and made her certify in writing she would not use former-employer confidential information — a certification she signed *before* the copy events [m06.fact.039; verdant-offer-letter §5]. This blunts the misappropriation narrative and supports "no use, no disclosure."
- The copy nonetheless creates **real exposure** (breach of the return-of-property covenant [employment-agreement §9]; optics before the court; a return-and-delete remedy Northlake will demand) and is the fulcrum of the counseling deliverable (§1.5, criterion c04).

The pedagogical point: candor is both an ethical duty and the stronger advocacy posture — the copy looks far worse if opposing counsel surfaces it first than if the defense frames it honestly.

### 1.5 Counseling posture and resolution

The client's overriding goal is to **keep the Verdant job** [m06.fact.034]; she is frightened of fees and of being branded a thief. The model counseling analysis (feeding criterion c04) covers: (i) the realistic risk that some injunctive relief could issue even if the broad covenant fails (e.g., a narrow return-and-delete order on the copied file); (ii) the strong likelihood the sweeping non-compete is unenforceable as written; (iii) the near-certainty the solicitation claim fails absent any solicitation; (iv) the value of **immediate voluntary return and deletion** of the file and forwarded email to neutralize the worst fact and demonstrate good faith; and (v) a candid cost-benefit of an aggressive fight vs. an early, narrow negotiated resolution for a client who mainly wants job security. The client-counseling session is billed and dated May 22, 2026 [business time entry m06.te.0011].

### 1.6 What an A-grade deliverable concludes

- Northlake likely **fails Rule 65** on likelihood of success (weak trade-secret measures + overbroad non-compete + no solicitation) and on irreparable harm (speculative, quantifiable, no proven use), with the equities and public interest reinforcing denial.
- The **non-compete** is the defense's strongest ground (overbreadth in geography/function); the **non-solicitation** clause is legally stronger for Northlake but factually empty.
- The **trade-secret** claim founders on **reasonable measures** under § 512.09, corroborated by Northlake's own witnesses.
- The client's **copy and forward** are handled candidly and defused with the no-use/no-disclosure record and voluntary return.
- The student **openly flags** that Meridian common-law reasonableness and the blue-pencil rule are matters of common law the canon does not codify, and reasons from principle rather than inventing authority.
- **Sourcing discipline:** the "real motive" fact [m06.fact.030] is a *concealed* item held by the represented director of sales (Danneker) — a student generally cannot obtain it through a permissible interview (§2, Rule 4.2). An A-grade paper may argue pretext from admissible circumstantial facts (timing of the bid loss, the sidelining effect [m06.fact.031]) without asserting the concealed admission as though it were in hand. Grade down a paper that "quotes" Danneker's motive as if lawfully obtained.

### 2.3 Rule 4.2 — Priscilla Danneker, the represented director of sales (`m06.per.danneker`)

**The rule (Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2).** Danneker is a **management-level representative of the represented adverse party**, Northlake, whose counsel is Rennick & Vao LLP [m06.fact.047; danneker persona `rule_4_2`]. Under the no-contact rule, defense counsel **may not communicate with her about the subject of the representation without the consent of Northlake's counsel** [m06.fact.047]. The student must instead seek this information through Northlake's counsel or through **formal discovery** (once the case is in a posture that permits it). This is a hard professional-responsibility line the exercise deliberately places in the student's path (criterion c04).

**How the app behaves.** Danneker's persona is `interviewable_by` both sides but `represented_by_counsel: true`. When a defense student attempts to interview her, the app **fires an in-app 4.2 teaching moment — not a silent block.** It surfaces the rule and the counsel's identity so the student learns *why* the contact is barred, rather than simply finding the persona unavailable. The teaching-moment text is pinned in the persona: contact is barred without opposing counsel's consent; seek the information through counsel or formal discovery.

**What she would say (for the instructor's situational awareness only — the student should not obtain this by direct contact):** volunteered crown-jewel framing [m06.fact.032]; if asked, the one-year region-wide covenant is standard for all engineers [m06.fact.005] and Okwuosa had access to all confidential pricing [m06.fact.007]; rapport-gated concessions that the file lived on a shared drive and wasn't locked down [m06.fact.015] and was never stamped confidential or separately passworded [m06.fact.016]; **concealed** the real motive — the impending loss of a large hospital-system bid to Verdant [m06.fact.030]; and she genuinely doesn't personally know whether Okwuosa solicited anyone [m06.fact.033].

**How to grade a student who contacts her anyway.**
- **Recognizes and declines** the contact (routes through Rennick & Vao or notes discovery instead) → full c04 professional-responsibility credit; the strongest students *explain in the required witness-contact memo* why they did **not** contact Danneker [see exercise instructions (3)].
- **Attempts contact, heeds the in-app 4.2 prompt, and corrects course** → substantial credit; treat the teachable moment as learning, note it in the debrief.
- **Attempts contact and proceeds as though the teaching moment were not there / relies on facts "learned" from her** (e.g., asserts the concealed motive [m06.fact.030] as obtained fact) → a **violation**: dock c04 heavily and, per the interview-pedagogy signed ethics scale (−2 to +2), push the interview's ethics score negative. Any deliverable that launders Danneker's concealed or rapport-gated admissions into the memo as if lawfully obtained is both an ethics failure and an evidentiary error — grade accordingly.

## 4. Grading Guidance

### 4.1 A / B / C discriminators (against the point bands)

- **A (189) / A− (178).** Separates trade-secret from covenant-enforceability cleanly; wins § 512.09 on reasonable measures with the client's *own* corroborating witnesses; distinguishes the overbroad non-compete from the factually-empty non-solicitation clause; **states honestly** that Meridian non-compete reasonableness is common law the canon does not codify and reasons from principle (no invented statute); confronts the USB copy and email forward head-on and defuses them (no use/no disclosure, voluntary return); flawless Rule 4.2 handling with a written justification for not contacting Danneker; harvests all rapport-gated and the Calderaro knockout fact [m06.fact.043]; proposes a proportionate outcome.
- **B+ (167) / B (155).** Solid Rule 65 structure and a correct bottom line, but with one or two real gaps: e.g., merges the two doctrines somewhat, misses one rapport-gated fact, under-develops staleness, or treats the non-solicitation clause too casually. Recognizes Rule 4.2 but explains it thinly. Confronts the hard facts but frames them defensively rather than strategically.
- **C (132).** Reaches a defensible conclusion but mechanically: recites the four factors without interdependence; treats the pricing data as obviously secret without engaging handling/staleness; blurs enforceability and protectability; misses most rapport-gated facts; and either overlooks Rule 4.2 or handles it only after the in-app prompt. Below 132: rewrite (see §4.3).

### 4.2 Common errors to probe in the debrief (builds on `instructor-notes.md`)

- **Conflating** the trade-secret question with covenant enforceability — the most frequent structural error; they are distinct doctrines (§1.1–1.3).
- **Rule 4.2 miss** — "interviewing" Danneker directly and, worse, importing her concealed motive [m06.fact.030] or rapport-gated concessions into the memo as fact (§2.3, §1.6).
- **Candor failure** — omitting or burying the USB copy and forwarded email in the fact statement [m06.fact.017, m06.fact.018]; a credibility and ethics problem before it is ever an advocacy problem.
- **Treating the pricing numbers as obviously secret** without engaging the loose-handling [m06.fact.015, m06.fact.016, m06.fact.037] and staleness [m06.fact.035, m06.fact.044] evidence.
- **Inventing authority** — asserting a Meridian non-compete statute or importing another jurisdiction's rule instead of flagging the common-law gap.
- **Over-earning vs. under-earning trust** — never reaching the rapport-gated client facts (blind to the worst fact), or conversely claiming to have "unlocked" them through pressure/flattery, which the engine does not permit.
