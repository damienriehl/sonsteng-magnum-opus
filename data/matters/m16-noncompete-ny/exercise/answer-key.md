## 1. Model Case Analysis (keyed to fact anchors) {#b:c6e98fc0}

### 1.1 Posture and the ask {#b:615a5b58}
HVB moved by Order to Show Cause for a **preliminary injunction** in the Commercial Division, Supreme Court, Dutchess County [m16.fact.026], to (a) enforce a two-year, geographically unlimited non-compete [m16.fact.007], (b) bar Iyer from Sightline, and (c) compel return of confidential material. The firm represents **Iyer (defense)** [exercise role `m16.role.defense`] and opposes the injunction. The governing frame is CPLR Article 63 (§§ 6301, 6312) — likelihood of success, irreparable harm, and balance of equities `[instructor: verify before teaching]`. {#b:f2ce2508}

### 1.2 Issue A — Likelihood of success: covenant reasonableness (rubric c01.s01, 40 pts) {#b:716b1532}
The controlling standard is the New York common-law reasonableness test for employee restrictive covenants — the *BDO Seidman v. Hirshberg*, 93 N.Y.2d 382 (1999) three-prong test, resting on *Reed, Roberts Assocs. v. Strauman*, 40 N.Y.2d 303 (1976): a covenant is enforceable only to the extent it (1) is no greater than necessary to protect a legitimate interest (trade secrets, confidential customer information, or unique/extraordinary services), (2) does not impose undue hardship on the employee, and (3) is not injurious to the public `[instructor: verify before teaching]`. {#b:5bb64d84}

Applied to the facts, the covenant is a strong overbreadth target: {#b:70ac378d}
- **Duration/geography/scope.** Two years, *no geographic limit*, sweeping across "biometric identification, computer-vision matching, or related fields" [m16.fact.007] — it bars the only work Iyer is trained for. A model answer argues this exceeds any legitimate protectable interest and invites either refusal or judicial narrowing. {#b:9e30b328}
- **Legitimate interest is weak.** The "matching engine" rests substantially on published research and open-source libraries [m16.fact.009], lived in shared repositories readable by the whole engineering team [m16.fact.010], was discussed openly in all-hands [m16.fact.010], and was never marked "trade secret" as a whole [m16.fact.011]. What is arguably HVB's own — specific hyperparameters, curated training data, accumulated judgment [m16.fact.012] — Iyer took in no tangible form [m16.fact.019]. {#b:f6e8ff7e}
- **Consideration / adhesion.** Signed on day one with a dozen new hires, never negotiated, presented as non-negotiable [m16.fact.008] — a model answer raises the adhesive character and the question of independent consideration for an at-will covenant. {#b:652b64fa}
- **Blue-pencil / partial enforcement.** *BDO Seidman* permits partial enforcement of an overbroad covenant absent overreaching/bad faith; a strong student both invokes the § 12 blue-pencil clause and argues that HVB's coercive, anti-competitive purpose (below) counsels against saving the covenant `[instructor: verify before teaching]`. {#b:8f43a356}

### 1.3 Issue B — Trade-secret protectability and inevitable disclosure (rubric c01.s01) {#b:922c95c6}
Two sub-questions: {#b:e2509d62}
- **Is the engine a protectable trade secret?** Under the *Ashland Mgmt. v. Janien*, 82 N.Y.2d 395 (1993) / Restatement factors, protection requires that the information derive value from not being generally known and that the owner took reasonable measures to keep it secret `[instructor: verify before teaching]`. The record cuts hard against both prongs: public foundations [m16.fact.009], open internal handling, shared repos, no marking or need-to-know [m16.fact.010]–[m16.fact.011]. Frawley corroborates [m16.fact.032]. {#b:ab7776e6}
- **Inevitable disclosure.** New York courts are cautious/skeptical of the doctrine (the *EarthWeb, Inc. v. Schlack*, 71 F. Supp. 2d 299 (S.D.N.Y. 1999) line), generally requiring more than an employee's general knowledge and declining to enjoin absent a showing of actual or threatened misappropriation `[instructor: verify before teaching]`. Here the forensic record shows **no exfiltration** [m16.fact.018]–[m16.fact.019]; the new role is a clean-room gait project in a different product/market [m16.fact.015]–[m16.fact.016]; and Sightline built an ethical wall and instructed her to bring nothing [m16.fact.033]. General skill and knowledge in the employee's head are hers to use [m16.fact.022], [m16.fact.037]. A model answer argues inevitable disclosure has no footing on these facts and that HVB proceeds on "assumption and inference" — as Steinhauer privately concedes [m16.fact.030]. {#b:c60884e7}

### 1.4 Issue C — Irreparable harm, equities, public interest (rubric c01.s02, 30 pts) {#b:731d76c0}
- **Irreparable harm** is doubtful where there is no proof anything was taken [m16.fact.019], [m16.fact.030] and money/ordinary competition is the real complaint. {#b:632e6f5f}
- **Balance of equities** tips to Iyer: a two-year bar destroys her livelihood in a narrow specialty [m16.fact.035], while HVB's showing is speculative. {#b:e18f0431}
- **Public interest** favors labor mobility and disfavors enforcing overbroad covenants — and the record reveals an anticompetitive motive: HVB lost a county-wide bid to Sightline two months before Iyer resigned and wants to slow a rival and deter departures [m16.fact.031]. This is the deliberate NY-vs.-California contrast: California voids employee non-competes by statute (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 16600; *Edwards v. Arthur Andersen LLP*, 44 Cal.4th 937 (2008)) `[instructor: verify before teaching]` — a strong student notes that Iyer would face no covenant at all in California and uses the public-policy pull. {#b:78601b61}

### 1.5 The client's soft facts (candor / credibility) {#b:733fc19c}
A model answer surfaces and neutralizes, rather than buries: the emailed personal folder containing one hand-drawn architecture diagram [m16.fact.021]; the personal engineering notebook she took, believing it her own skill [m16.fact.022]; the recruiting-flavored talk with Vandermeer [m16.fact.023]; and — the worst — the year-old boast that she could "rebuild the engine anywhere" [m16.fact.024]. None is fatal, but candor in the opposition papers protects credibility and blunts opposing counsel. The diagram's provenance is arguable and should be addressed head-on; the notebook is defensible as general skill; the Vandermeer talk is not solicitation of a *current* employee (she left in 2024, [m16.fact.034]); the boast is bravado, not a plan, but must be prepared for. {#b:6220c021}

**RULE 4.2 — the graded professional-responsibility trap.** Steinhauer is a co-founder and management-level representative of the represented adverse party HVB (counsel: Halbrook Renner & Iselin LLP). Under N.Y. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2 (22 NYCRR Part 1200) `[instructor: verify before teaching]`, **defense counsel may not contact him directly** about the subject of the representation without HVB counsel's consent. The correct move is to seek his information through opposing counsel or formal discovery (deposition, interrogatories, document demands). A student who "interviews" Steinhauer directly triggers the in-app teaching moment and loses the c04 Rule-4.2 points. His rapport-gated concessions ([m16.fact.010]–[m16.fact.011]) and concealed motive ([m16.fact.031]) are what a *properly noticed deposition* would aim at — good material for a discovery-plan discussion, not a live interview. {#b:72c23df9}

## 4. Grading Guidance {#b:e0471963}

- **Reward the separation of questions.** The single most common error is conflating covenant enforceability with trade-secret misappropriation and treating inevitable disclosure as a settled, employer-friendly rule. Top papers keep the three analyses distinct and note New York's skepticism. {#b:e9d4f98c}
- **Reward candor.** A student who states the diagram, notebook, and boast plainly and neutralizes them should out-score one who hides them and "wins" cleaner-looking on paper — hiding is both an ethics and a credibility failure that opposing counsel will exploit. {#b:2e45ea2f}
- **The Rule 4.2 line is bright.** Any direct contact with Steinhauer is a hard miss on c04's PR component regardless of what "facts" it produced. Credit students who plan discovery/opposing-counsel routing instead. {#b:8808ab2c}
- **Interview technique over extraction.** Grade whether the rapport-gated/concealed facts were earned through the closed triggers (confidentiality reassurance, non-judgment, acknowledged emotion, following hints) — not whether the text merely appeared. Pressure or flattery that "works" is a red flag for the debrief. {#b:4ed1336b}
- **Reasonable-measures is the pressure point.** Strong answers hammer the shared repositories, open discussion, and absence of marking [m16.fact.010]–[m16.fact.011] on the reasonable-measures and "generally known" prongs, rather than assuming the engine is obviously secret. {#b:ce5a1e55}
- **Two-axis debrief (per `docs/research/interview-pedagogy.md`):** score fact coverage (elicited / revealed-if-asked-never-asked / rapport-gated-never-earned + the Rule 4.2 flag) on Axis A, and the client's felt experience (rapport, listening, understanding goals, explanation/next steps, confidence) on Axis B, rated in character. A Rule 4.2 violation drives the signed ethics item negative. {#b:d822045c}
