## 1. Model Case Analysis

The charge is **Fourth-degree Driving While Impaired**, a first offense at the per-se threshold of 0.08, under **Mer. Stat. § 787.11**, with a parallel administrative revocation under the implied-consent statute **§ 787.15** [m05.fact.028] [m05.fact.031]. To convict on the per-se theory the State must prove (a) Halvard drove or operated a motor vehicle, and (b) had an alcohol concentration of 0.08 or more within two hours of driving. The State may also, or alternatively, proceed on an **impairment/under-the-influence theory** resting on the officer's observations and the field tests, which does not depend on the numeric reading. A first-rate analysis separates these two theories, because the defense levers hit them differently: the margin-of-error attack wounds the **per-se** theory but leaves the **impairment** theory standing on the observations, and vice-versa.

### Issue 1 — Lawfulness of the stop (reasonable articulable suspicion)

Broshears stopped the sedan for an inoperative rear passenger-side taillight [m05.fact.013]. Under the traffic-stop standard, an actually-observed equipment violation supplies reasonable articulable suspicion, and an officer's subjective or pretextual motive does not by itself invalidate a stop supported by an objective violation. So the doctrinal question is **not** motive but **whether the record supports that the taillight was in fact out.**

- Devon believed the light worked and thought the car had passed a recent check [m05.fact.014]. That is a genuine dispute of fact, but it is weak standing alone (a lamp can fail between checks).
- The decisive evidence is the **dashcam**, which "captures the rear of the subject vehicle in the seconds before the stop," where "the illumination of the passenger-side rear lamp is a matter for review of the footage" [m05.fact.041]. The worksheet, the report, and the citation all rest on Broshears' say-so; the objective record that could confirm or refute it is the video.
- A first-rate student does **not** assert the stop was unlawful. The student says: the stop's validity turns on the footage; if the dashcam shows the lamp illuminated, the equipment basis collapses and the stop (and everything downstream) is suppressible as fruit; if it shows the lamp dark or is inconclusive, the stop stands. **Reasonable minds differ, and the tape decides.** Rahimi cannot resolve it — he "wasn't looking," it was "dark and snowing," and he cannot say whether the lamp worked [m05.fact.040]. Arguing the stop was invalid *without* first anchoring to the footage is an over-claim (see §4).

### Issue 2 — Expansion to a DWI investigation

Once lawfully stopped, Broshears reported an odor of alcohol from the vehicle, bloodshot/watery eyes, and slow speech [m05.fact.015], plus Devon's admission that he had "had a couple beers" [m05.fact.016]. On this record the expansion from an equipment stop to a DWI investigation is **well-supported** — odor + admission alone typically justify field testing. This is the defense's weakest pressure point; a student should concede the expansion and spend leverage elsewhere. (The odor/eyes/speech observations are attackable at *weight* — bloodshot/watery eyes and slow speech are equally consistent with a fourteen-hour double shift, see Issue 3 — but not as a basis to bar the investigation.)

### Issue 3 — SFST administration and validity

Broshears ran HGN, walk-and-turn, and one-leg-stand and scored "multiple clues," concluding impairment [m05.fact.020]. The reliability attack is strong on the two balance tests and must be argued **test-by-test**, because a student who lumps them together reveals they don't understand the tests:

- **Conditions.** The tests were done on the right shoulder, later measured at roughly a **seven-percent cross-slope** toward a storm drain [m05.fact.033] [m05.exh.009], **packed with ice and snow** [m05.fact.018], in **18°F** weather with the municipal log showing untreated shoulders [m05.fact.032]. Devon **asked to move to flatter ground and was refused** [m05.fact.019] — corroborated by the bodycam audio at 22:47:20 [m05.fact.041] and even by Broshears' own report ("would prefer to perform the tests somewhere flatter; the tests were completed at the location of the stop").
- **The worksheet understates the conditions.** Broshears recorded only "Paved shoulder, snow present" and did **not** measure the grade [m05.fact.033 — Broshears "couldn't give you a number on how sloped it was"]. The gap between the officer's bland entry and the defense's measured 7% slope + ice is itself impeachment.
- **The walk-and-turn and one-leg-stand are divided-attention balance tests** whose validity assumptions include reasonably level, dry footing. On a 7% icy grade the recorded clues (stepped off line, used arms for balance, swayed, put foot down) are exactly what the surface would produce in a sober person — the very ground Devon flagged as "slippery" on the worksheet.
- **HGN is different — do not overclaim.** Horizontal-gaze nystagmus is an eye test, **not** a balance test; slope and ice do **not** invalidate it. Its own soft spots are administration conditions the worksheet actually notes (headlights/flashlight, passing traffic) and foundation for how it was administered. A first-rate student keeps HGN separate and does not pretend the icy slope neutralizes it.
- **Two body facts degrade the balance tests further**, and both are rapport-gated (see §2): a diagnosed **inner-ear condition, benign positional vertigo**, that makes one-leg and heel-to-toe tasks hard even sober [m05.fact.021], and **fatigue from a fourteen-hour double shift** [m05.fact.022] [m05.fact.011]. These reduce the weight of the clues but do not, standing alone, suppress anything.

**Bottom line on the SFSTs: this is a weight-of-the-evidence argument, not an automatic suppression.** The tests were not *administered unlawfully* in a way that bars them; they are *unreliable* here and should carry little weight before a fact-finder and in the probable-cause calculus. **Reasonable minds differ** on how much the clues are worth once the conditions are known.

### Issue 4 — Probable cause to arrest

PC rests on the totality: odor, bloodshot/watery eyes, slow speech [m05.fact.015], the admission [m05.fact.016], the SFST clues [m05.fact.020], and the preliminary roadside breath test [m05.fact.023]. Even if a student succeeds in draining the balance-test clues of weight (Issue 3), **odor + admission + PBT + HGN + observation** may still clear the low probable-cause bar. So the honest defense position is that PC to arrest is **defensible for the State** and is *not* the best suppression target — the better targets are the stop (if the tape helps) and the breath sample's foundation. A student who bets the case on "no probable cause to arrest" is picking the wrong hill. **Reasonable minds differ**, but the weight of this record favors the State on PC.

### Issue 5 — Breath-test foundation and the two-hour window

- **The two-hour window is satisfied — do not argue otherwise.** Devon left to drive at ~10:35 p.m. [m05.fact.012]; the stop was 10:41 p.m. [m05.fact.013]; the first evidentiary sample was at **11:28 p.m.** [m05.exh.002], roughly **47–53 minutes after driving** — comfortably inside the two-hour limit of § 787.11. A student who argues the sample fell outside two hours has misread the record and loses credibility.
- **The real vulnerabilities are the reading itself and mouth alcohol.** The instrument reported **exactly 0.08** [m05.fact.027] — precisely on the per-se line — and the DMT-G's stated measurement uncertainty could place the true value **below 0.08** [m05.fact.042]. At 0.08 flat, the margin of error is not academic: if the true value is under 0.08, the **per-se theory fails entirely** (the impairment theory may survive on observations).
- **Mouth-alcohol / reflux.** Devon burped and felt acid reflux in the minutes right before he blew [m05.fact.026] — a classic mouth-alcohol concern that can inflate a reading. Foundation cuts both ways: the observation period ran **23:13–23:28 (15 minutes)** [m05.exh.002], which is consistent with a proper deprivation period, **but** the operator log "does not note any mouth-check for foreign substances, belching, or regurgitation during the observation period" [m05.exh.002]. The silence in the log is the lever — the State can't affirmatively show the officer watched for and ruled out a belch, and the client reports one occurred. This is the single strongest foundation attack, though it depends on a gated, embarrassing, uncorroborated client fact and the fact-finder's credibility call. **Reasonable minds differ.**

### Issue 6 — Implied-consent advisory adequacy

The advisory was read at 11:12 p.m. [m05.fact.024] and, in substance, told Devon that testing was required after arrest, that refusal is a separate offense, and that he could consult counsel before deciding if it did not unreasonably delay the test [m05.exh.004] — the elements § 787.15 requires. The log shows he was asked about contacting an attorney ("declined / proceeded") and consented [m05.fact.025]. On this record the advisory is **facially adequate**; there is no advisory-defect suppression theory the record compels. A student who manufactures an advisory defect here is over-reaching. The advisory's real significance is the **collateral consequence** it sets in motion — the administrative revocation with its own clock [m05.fact.031] — which is the client's true fear and belongs in counseling and the advice letter, not in a suppression motion.

### Issue 7 — Degree and aggravating factors

Fourth-degree is **correct** on this record, and confirming that is part of the analysis. Run the canon's aggravators and show each is absent:

- **BAC 0.16+?** No — 0.08 [m05.fact.027].
- **Prior within 10 years?** No — the DVS abstract shows only a 2023 speeding ticket and no alcohol-related offenses, suspensions, or crashes [m05.fact.043] [m05.exh.006].
- **Child under 16 in the vehicle?** No — Rahimi (adult coworker) had already been dropped off [m05.fact.040].
- **Test refusal?** No — Devon consented [m05.fact.025].

With **zero aggravators**, the charge cannot be enhanced above fourth degree, and the clean record [m05.fact.004] [m05.fact.043] is the backbone of both the first-offense posture and the reduction pitch (§ below). A student should affirmatively verify the absence of aggravators rather than assume it.

### Issue 8 — The drinking timeline (Copper Larder guest check vs. reported driving time)

The corroborated consumption is **two draft ales and one toast shot**: amber ale 21:20, toast shot 22:05, amber ale 22:35, water 22:50 [m05.exh.007], matching Vandehey's tab [m05.fact.039] and Selkirk's account [m05.fact.038], with a full meal throughout [m05.fact.010]. Devon left to drive ~10:35 p.m. [m05.fact.012]. Two independent observers (Selkirk, Vandehey) describe him as **tired, not drunk**, steady, not slurring [m05.fact.038] [m05.fact.039], and Rahimi says he drove normally and kept his lane [m05.fact.040].

- **The candor problem the student must catch:** Devon's own "two beers over three hours" [m05.fact.007] **omits the whiskey shot** [m05.fact.008] — a concealed fact. The student needs the true count to advise honestly and to avoid being surprised at the omnibus/negotiation. It cuts both ways: the shot makes consumption slightly worse, but a modest three-drink spread over ~3 hours **with a full meal** is consistent with a borderline reading that could genuinely sit at or below 0.08 — which *supports* the margin-of-error theory rather than undercutting it.
- **Minor timeline seam (realistic ambiguity, not a defense):** the tab rings an ale at 22:35 and water at 22:50, while Devon says he left at 22:35; the check flags times as "approximate as entered by the POS." A first-rate student notes the seam without over-reading it.

### Strongest theory — State vs. defense

- **State's best case:** valid equipment stop (if the tape holds), a well-supported expansion, multiple impairment indicia plus SFST clues plus PBT giving probable cause, a facially adequate advisory, and an in-window evidentiary reading at the per-se line. The State wins if the dashcam confirms the dark lamp and the fact-finder credits the 0.08.
- **Defense's best case (in order of strength):**
  1. **Breath-sample foundation / margin of error + mouth alcohol** [m05.fact.026] [m05.fact.042] [m05.exh.002] — the per-se theory lives or dies here at exactly 0.08.
  2. **Stop legality contingent on the dashcam** [m05.fact.014] [m05.fact.041] — potential case-ender if the footage shows an illuminated lamp; a jump ball otherwise.
  3. **SFST unreliability** [m05.fact.018] [m05.fact.019] [m05.fact.021] [m05.fact.022] [m05.fact.033] — weight, not suppression; drains the impairment theory.
  4. **Degree/mitigation and the negotiated exit** — clean record + first offense + genuinely borderline reading make a reduction to careless driving realistic.
- **Plea posture:** because the client's real stake is the **license and the job**, not jail [m05.fact.003] [m05.fact.031] [m05.fact.045], the honest conclusion is that a **negotiated reduction to careless driving** may serve him better than betting everything on a suppression motion that is a jump ball — while the suppression threat (especially the 0.08 foundation) is the leverage that makes the reduction attainable. The two strategies are complementary, not either/or: develop the omnibus motion to create negotiating leverage, then convert it.

**Conclusion for the primary deliverable (preliminary case-analysis memorandum):** a first-rate memo identifies the charge and both theories, spots all of the above, ranks the levers honestly (foundation > stop > SFSTs > mitigation), refuses to over-claim on the stop or the two-hour window, verifies the absence of aggravators, and frames suppression as leverage toward the reduction the client actually needs — all while flagging the Rule 4.2 constraint on reaching Broshears (§2).

## 4. Grading Guidance

### A / B / C discriminators for this matter

1. **Earning the gated facts vs. taking the flattering count.**
   - **A:** surfaces all four gated/concealed facts — vertigo [m05.fact.021], fatigue [m05.fact.022], reflux [m05.fact.026], toast shot [m05.fact.008] — through empathy and reconciliation against the tab, and uses each correctly (fatigue explains the observations; vertigo + slope drain the balance tests; reflux drives mouth-alcohol; the shot fixes candor).
   - **B:** gets one or two gated facts and the timeline but doesn't connect them to the legal levers.
   - **C:** accepts "two beers, felt fine" [m05.fact.007], never unlocks the gates, and analyzes a case that isn't the real one.
2. **Ranking the levers honestly vs. over-claiming.**
   - **A:** foundation > contingent stop > SFST weight > mitigation; concedes PC is defensible and the advisory is adequate; refuses to assert the stop is unlawful without the dashcam and never argues the two-hour window.
   - **B:** spots the issues but over-weights a weak one (e.g., leads with "no probable cause") or treats SFST suppression as automatic.
   - **C:** makes claims the record won't bear (stop unlawful on the client's say-so; sample outside two hours; advisory defective) and loses credibility.
3. **HGN vs. balance-test discipline.**
   - **A:** keeps HGN (an eye test) separate from walk-and-turn/one-leg-stand (balance tests) and argues the slope/ice against the balance tests only.
   - **C:** claims the icy slope invalidates *all three* tests — a tell that the student doesn't understand the SFST battery.
4. **Client-centered strategy vs. lawyer-centered heroics.**
   - **A:** keeps the **license and job** [m05.fact.003] [m05.fact.031] [m05.fact.045] at the center, treats suppression as **leverage** toward a careless-driving reduction, and runs the administrative clock as its own workstream.
   - **C:** chases a courtroom win on a jump-ball motion while ignoring the collateral consequence the client actually fears.

### Common errors to penalize

- Arguing the **stop was unlawful** without anchoring to the dashcam [m05.fact.041]; Rahimi cannot supply it [m05.fact.040].
- Arguing the sample fell **outside the two-hour window** (it didn't — ~47–53 minutes) [m05.fact.012] [m05.exh.002].
- **Manufacturing an advisory defect** the record doesn't support [m05.exh.004].
- Treating the **SFSTs as automatically suppressed** rather than as a weight argument; lumping HGN with the balance tests.
- **Missing or asserting aggravators** — confirm all four are absent (0.08 not 0.16; clean record; no child; consented) [m05.fact.043] [m05.fact.025].
- **Conceding facts the record doesn't compel** — e.g., accepting the "two beers" count [m05.fact.007] and never reconciling it against the guest check [m05.exh.007], or conceding impairment because the number is 0.08 when the margin of error may put it below [m05.fact.042].
- **Rule 4.2 breach** — contacting Broshears directly instead of routing through the prosecutor [m05.fact.044]; and **scope creep** beyond the flat-fee engagement.
- Over-promising the client an outcome, or leaving the administrative-license process unexplained.

### Re-write-loop guidance

- If the draft **misses the gated facts**, send the student back to the *interview*, not the memo — the analysis is only as good as the record elicited. Prompt: "What did Devon *not* volunteer, and what in your questioning would have earned it?"
- If the draft **over-claims on the stop**, require the student to condition every stop argument on what the dashcam shows and to state the both-ways outcome.
- If the draft is **lawyer-centered** (all suppression, no client goal), require a paragraph tying the strategy to the license/job and the administrative clock, and a fallback path to careless driving.
- If the draft **muddles the two breath theories**, require the student to state separately what happens to the *per-se* case and the *impairment* case if the reading is knocked below 0.08.
- If the draft **mishandles Rule 4.2**, debrief it as a teaching moment (route through the prosecutor) rather than a mere lost point, and have the student rewrite the PR section.
