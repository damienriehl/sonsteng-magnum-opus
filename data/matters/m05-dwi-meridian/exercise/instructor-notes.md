# Instructor Notes — m05 DWI (State of Meridian v. Devon R. Halvard) {#b:4d1cf881}

*Teaching notes only. Not part of the student packet. Contains a few statute/rule pointers for the instructor, marked instructor-verified-at-publish. No answer key and no intentional-error list — those are deferred.* {#b:e506163f}

## What this matter is teaching {#b:6a92e23f}

This is the first-offense DWI simulation. The pedagogical center is the **client interview** with a distressed first-time defendant, followed by **issue-spotting, counseling, and plea negotiation**. Students are defense counsel. The interview persona (Devon Halvard) is deliberately built so that the strongest defense facts — a diagnosed vestibular condition, a fourteen-hour double shift, an acid-reflux/burp immediately before the breath test, and an omitted birthday toast — do **not** surface unless the student earns them through rapport and non-judgmental follow-up. A student who bulldozes will get "two beers, felt fine" and miss the case. {#b:77da5baa}

## Meridian legal anchors (instructor-verified-at-publish; keep out of student materials) {#b:73349697}

- Impaired-driving offense and per-se limit: **Mer. Stat. § 787.11**; per se at an alcohol concentration of 0.08 within two hours of driving; first offense with no aggravating factors is Fourth-degree DWI (misdemeanor). {#b:f859911a}
- Implied consent / testing and revocation: **Mer. Stat. § 787.15**; refusal is a separate offense; administrative revocation runs parallel to the criminal case. {#b:801f0c71}
- Aggravating factors (none present here on the current record): 0.16-or-more reading, prior within ten years, child under 16 in the vehicle, or test refusal. {#b:8dc0d281}
- Communication with a represented person: **Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2** — the Rule 4.2 teaching moment with Officer Broshears. {#b:63a313ec}
- Presiding judge for Halden County DWI: **Hon. Darnell Whitcomb** (per the Meridian canon). {#b:61b68989}

## Discussion pressure points (no scoring guidance here) {#b:44192723}

- **The stop.** Single equipment violation; have students test it against the dashcam rather than the report alone. {#b:6e5bb031}
- **Field sobriety tests.** Roughly seven-percent sloped, icy shoulder; eighteen-degree night; fatigue and vestibular condition. Weight-of-evidence discussion, not a guaranteed suppression. {#b:bfd33d72}
- **The 0.08 sample.** Exactly at the per-se line; stated instrument uncertainty and the possible mouth-alcohol event are the levers. Note the operator log's silence on a mouth-check. {#b:dfeac1b8}
- **Suppression vs. negotiation.** The license — not jail — is the client's real stake. A reduction to careless driving may serve the client's goals better than a risky motion. Have students reason about the client's actual objectives. {#b:abfe79a7}

## Professional-responsibility watch items {#b:734847f1}

- Do not let students contact Officer Broshears directly; route through the prosecutor (Rule 4.2). The persona engine fires the teaching moment; debrief it rather than treating it as a mere block. {#b:d00bb6f5}
- Flat-fee scope: representation runs through the omnibus and plea stage; a full jury trial or contested implied-consent trial is a separate engagement. Watch for scope creep in student advice. {#b:00746a20}

## Business-of-law note {#b:c749dde3}

Flat fee $2,500, held as an advance in trust and drawn only as earned; as of the simulation date nothing has been drawn (matter pending). Good vehicle for discussing flat-fee ethics, trust-accounting of advances, and refund of unearned fees. {#b:030b0aa3}

## Suggested teaching arc and timing {#b:405688d7}

A two-session arc works best; the interview is the spine, and everything else hangs off what the student elicited. {#b:bd9dbf0e}

- **Pre-class (async).** Students read the full case file and run **at least one** ungraded interview through the simulated-client engine. Tell them up front that the persona will not hand over the strongest facts to a bulldozer — that framing is the lesson, not a spoiler. {#b:85ba8b0c}
- **Session 1 — the interview (≈90 min).** (a) 10 min: set the two-axis debrief (fact coverage + the client's felt experience) so students know they are scored on rapport, not just checklist facts. (b) 30 min: two or three volunteers run live interviews; the rest score on the same rubric. (c) 30 min: debrief which gated facts came out and *why* — trace each unlock to a rapport move, and each miss to a bulldozing or accusatorial move. (d) 20 min: the candor problem — how to reconcile "two beers" against the guest check without shaming the client into silence. {#b:cfe71607}
- **Between sessions.** Students draft the preliminary case-analysis memo and the one-page client-advice letter. {#b:d7343ef7}
- **Session 2 — analysis, counseling, negotiation (≈90 min).** (a) 25 min: issue-spotting workshop on the pressure points below, insisting on honest ranking rather than a laundry list. (b) 20 min: counseling role-play focused on the license fear and the parallel administrative clock. (c) 30 min: mock plea negotiation toward careless driving; half the room argues the State. (d) 15 min: the Rule 4.2 moment and flat-fee scope, debriefed as professional-judgment questions, not gotchas. {#b:ba00e528}

## Facilitation cautions {#b:0f63e6bc}

- **Let the misses happen.** The pedagogy depends on students feeling the cost of a rushed interview. Do not coach the gated facts out for them in the live run; surface the technique gap in the debrief instead. {#b:f9210df9}
- **Keep suppression honest.** Steer the room away from "we win the motion" certainty. The stop is a dashcam-dependent jump ball and the SFST attack is weight, not automatic suppression. Reward students who say "reasonable minds differ." {#b:b27e1eef}
- **Center the client, not the doctrine.** The strongest legal theory and the best outcome for Devon can diverge. Push students to choose strategy against his actual objective — the license and the job. {#b:58b216bf}
- **Debrief Rule 4.2, don't just score it.** When the engine fires the no-contact moment, treat it as a professional-judgment discussion about routing through the prosecutor, not a silent penalty. {#b:2ffecedf}

## Discussion questions {#b:cf47a280}

1. Which single fact in this file would you most want the dashcam to confirm, and how does the stop analysis change depending on what the footage shows? {#b:8446a4b1}
2. The evidentiary reading is exactly 0.08. Walk through what happens to the State's *per-se* case and its *impairment* case if the margin of error puts the true value below the line — do they rise and fall together? {#b:e1655350}
3. The client says "two beers, felt fine." What in your interview technique earns the facts he is embarrassed to volunteer, and what technique guarantees you never hear them? {#b:70a9e872}
4. HGN, walk-and-turn, one-leg-stand: which of these does the icy, seven-percent slope actually undermine, and which does it not — and why does the distinction matter to your credibility? {#b:a2719bb6}
5. Devon fears losing his license more than he fears jail. How should that reorder your priorities among a suppression motion, a plea to careless driving, and the parallel administrative proceeding? {#b:af721509}
6. You want Officer Broshears' account of the stop. Walk through the professionally correct way to get it — and what has gone wrong if you have contacted him directly. {#b:42a94fbb}
