# Instructor Notes — m05 DWI (State of Meridian v. Devon R. Halvard)

*Teaching notes only. Not part of the student packet. Contains a few statute/rule pointers for the instructor, marked instructor-verified-at-publish. No answer key and no intentional-error list — those are deferred.*

## What this matter is teaching

This is the first-offense DWI simulation. The pedagogical center is the **client interview** with a distressed first-time defendant, followed by **issue-spotting, counseling, and plea negotiation**. Students are defense counsel. The interview persona (Devon Halvard) is deliberately built so that the strongest defense facts — a diagnosed vestibular condition, a fourteen-hour double shift, an acid-reflux/burp immediately before the breath test, and an omitted birthday toast — do **not** surface unless the student earns them through rapport and non-judgmental follow-up. A student who bulldozes will get "two beers, felt fine" and miss the case.

## Meridian legal anchors (instructor-verified-at-publish; keep out of student materials)

- Impaired-driving offense and per-se limit: **Mer. Stat. § 787.11**; per se at an alcohol concentration of 0.08 within two hours of driving; first offense with no aggravating factors is Fourth-degree DWI (misdemeanor).
- Implied consent / testing and revocation: **Mer. Stat. § 787.15**; refusal is a separate offense; administrative revocation runs parallel to the criminal case.
- Aggravating factors (none present here on the current record): 0.16-or-more reading, prior within ten years, child under 16 in the vehicle, or test refusal.
- Communication with a represented person: **Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2** — the Rule 4.2 teaching moment with Officer Broshears.
- Presiding judge for Halden County DWI: **Hon. Darnell Whitcomb** (per the Meridian canon).

## Discussion pressure points (no scoring guidance here)

- **The stop.** Single equipment violation; have students test it against the dashcam rather than the report alone.
- **Field sobriety tests.** Roughly seven-percent sloped, icy shoulder; eighteen-degree night; fatigue and vestibular condition. Weight-of-evidence discussion, not a guaranteed suppression.
- **The 0.08 sample.** Exactly at the per-se line; stated instrument uncertainty and the possible mouth-alcohol event are the levers. Note the operator log's silence on a mouth-check.
- **Suppression vs. negotiation.** The license — not jail — is the client's real stake. A reduction to careless driving may serve the client's goals better than a risky motion. Have students reason about the client's actual objectives.

## Professional-responsibility watch items

- Do not let students contact Officer Broshears directly; route through the prosecutor (Rule 4.2). The persona engine fires the teaching moment; debrief it rather than treating it as a mere block.
- Flat-fee scope: representation runs through the omnibus and plea stage; a full jury trial or contested implied-consent trial is a separate engagement. Watch for scope creep in student advice.
