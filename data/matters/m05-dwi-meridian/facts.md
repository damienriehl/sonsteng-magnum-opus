# Ground-Truth Facts — State of Meridian v. Devon R. Halvard (m05) {#b:7ad1fb05}

*Master narrative for the DWI defense exercise. This file is the single source of truth; every persona disclosure and rubric evidence item anchors back to a fact number here. Student-facing packets stay facts-only.* {#b:f8bccd79}

## The client and his stake {#b:5bcdb208}

Devon R. Halvard is twenty-nine years old and works as a line cook at The Copper Larder, a small bistro on Wexford Avenue in Norsholm, Halden County [m05.fact.001]. He rents half of a duplex about six miles from the restaurant and drives an older sedan to and from every shift [m05.fact.002]. There is no bus service running when the kitchen closes near midnight, so his driver's license is, in his own words, the thing that lets him keep his job [m05.fact.003]. He has never been arrested before and has no prior criminal or impaired-driving record [m05.fact.004]. His whole reason for hiring counsel is simple and non-legal: keep the license, keep the job, and avoid jail or a permanent record [m05.fact.045]. {#b:d46ff64c}

## The night of the birthday dinner {#b:a78da0a4}

On the evening of Saturday, February 7, 2026, the crew held a small after-hours birthday dinner for the sous chef, Margo Selkirk, once the dining room had closed [m05.fact.005]. Devon had been on the line since early that afternoon; he finished plating around 9:15 p.m. and pulled up a chair at the staff table [m05.fact.006]. By his own account he drank two draft ales over roughly three hours while the party ate and talked [m05.fact.007]. {#b:6c8d6c89}

There is a fact Devon does not volunteer and initially leaves out of his own count: when the cake came out around 10:00 p.m., he also drank a whiskey shot as part of the birthday toast, so "two beers over three hours" understates what he actually consumed [m05.fact.008]. The bartender that night, Trina Vandehey, poured for the table and rang two draft ales and a round of toast shots to Devon's tab [m05.fact.009]. Devon ate a full meal — the family staff dinner plus shared plates from the party [m05.fact.010]. He had also worked a double shift that day, close to fourteen hours on his feet, and was worn out by the time the party wound down [m05.fact.011]. He left to drive home at about 10:35 p.m. [m05.fact.012]. {#b:b01dee81}

## The stop {#b:7b457c11}

At 10:41 p.m., Officer Curtis Broshears of the Norsholm Police Department stopped Devon on Wexford Avenue a few blocks from the restaurant, reporting that the sedan's rear passenger-side taillight was out [m05.fact.013]. Devon was surprised; he believed the light worked and thought the car had passed a recent check [m05.fact.014]. In his report, Broshears wrote that he smelled an odor of alcohol from the vehicle, that Devon's eyes were bloodshot and watery, and that his speech was slow [m05.fact.015]. Asked whether he had been drinking, Devon acknowledged he had "had a couple beers" at a coworker's birthday [m05.fact.016]. {#b:ec1f26c1}

Broshears asked Devon to step out for field sobriety testing [m05.fact.017]. The shoulder where the officer had him perform the tests was sloped and packed with ice and old snow [m05.fact.018]. Devon asked whether they could move to flatter ground; the request was not accommodated, and the tests went ahead where they stood [m05.fact.019]. Broshears administered the horizontal-gaze-nystagmus check, the walk-and-turn, and the one-leg-stand, scored what he recorded as multiple clues of impairment, and concluded Devon was impaired [m05.fact.020]. {#b:0aa5f406}

Two facts about Devon's body matter to how those balance tests should be read, and he is slow and embarrassed to raise either. He has a diagnosed inner-ear condition — benign positional vertigo — that affects his balance and makes one-leg and heel-to-toe tasks genuinely hard for him even sober [m05.fact.021]. And the double shift had left him exhausted, which further degraded his coordination that night [m05.fact.022]. {#b:7cee3e28}

## Arrest and the breath test {#b:6d122bd8}

After a preliminary roadside breath test, Broshears placed Devon under arrest and transported him to the Norsholm Police Department [m05.fact.023]. At 11:12 p.m. the officer read Devon the Implied Consent Advisory [m05.fact.024]. Devon consented to an evidentiary breath test on the department's DMT-G instrument [m05.fact.025]. There is another fact Devon keeps to himself out of embarrassment: he burped and felt a wave of acid reflux in the minutes right before he blew, which — if true — raises a mouth-alcohol concern about the sample [m05.fact.026]. The instrument recorded two samples and reported an alcohol concentration of 0.08 [m05.fact.027]. {#b:4725a2f7}

## The charges and the administrative hit {#b:4764e8fe}

Devon was charged with Fourth-degree Driving While Impaired — a first offense at the per-se threshold of 0.08 — under Meridian's impaired-driving law [m05.fact.028]. He was separately cited for the taillight equipment violation [m05.fact.029]. He was released the next morning; his car had been towed [m05.fact.030]. Beyond the criminal case, his license is exposed to administrative revocation through the implied-consent process, and that — not jail — is the loss that frightens him most, because it directly threatens his ability to get to work [m05.fact.031]. {#b:35fde283}

## The scene, measured later {#b:b1d246b4}

The weather that night was light snow with temperatures around eighteen degrees, and the municipal road report logged icy conditions across Norsholm [m05.fact.032]. When the defense later had the stop location looked at, the shoulder where the tests were performed was found to grade toward a storm drain at roughly a seven-percent slope — not level ground [m05.fact.033]. {#b:7079bfe8}

## Engagement and posture {#b:710138a9}

Devon retained the firm of Ellingboe & Ravndal LLP on February 16, 2026, meeting first with Marcus Ravndal [m05.fact.034]. The firm agreed to a flat fee of $2,500 to carry the representation through the pretrial and contested-omnibus stage and any plea negotiation, and Devon deposited a $2,500 advance into the firm's trust account against that fee [m05.fact.035]. A not-guilty plea was entered at arraignment, discovery was demanded, and the defense requested a contested omnibus hearing on probable cause and suppression, to be heard by the Hon. Darnell Whitcomb of the Meridian District Court, Halden County [m05.fact.036]. As of the simulation date the omnibus hearing is still pending and no fee has been drawn from the trust advance. {#b:05d824de}

The defense is developing four threads: that the taillight stop was pretextual or unsupported; that the field sobriety tests are unreliable when done on an icy, sloped shoulder; that the 0.08 reading — exactly at the per-se line — is vulnerable on mouth-alcohol and margin-of-error grounds; and, failing suppression, a negotiated resolution down to careless driving [m05.fact.037]. {#b:a9da2642}

## What the other witnesses say {#b:af0c7dd6}

Margo Selkirk, the birthday celebrant and sous chef, remembers Devon having two ales and one toast shot, and describes him as seeming "fine, just tired" when he left [m05.fact.038]. Trina Vandehey's bar tab for the table confirms two ales and one shot rung to Devon [m05.fact.039]. Aabid Rahimi, a fellow line cook who caught a ride partway home before being dropped off, says Devon drove normally, kept to his lane, and did not swerve [m05.fact.040]. {#b:c1ee85a2}

Officer Broshears' squad dashcam captured the taillight and the driving, and his bodycam captured the field sobriety tests on the sloped shoulder [m05.fact.041]. The evidentiary reading of 0.08 sits exactly at the per-se limit, and the DMT-G's stated measurement uncertainty could place the true value below 0.08 [m05.fact.042]. Devon's state driving record shows a single speeding ticket from 2023 and no alcohol-related offenses [m05.fact.043]. {#b:671f0425}

The prosecution is handled by an Assistant City Attorney for Norsholm, who represents the State [m05.fact.044]. {#b:b43828ca}
