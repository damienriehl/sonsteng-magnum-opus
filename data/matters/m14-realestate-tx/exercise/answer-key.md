## 1. Model Case Analysis (keyed to fact anchors) {#b:5b4a90ab}

This is a **three-variable transactional negotiation**, not a lawsuit — the A-grade student refuses to treat the three title problems as independent and trades across them toward a closeable deal [m14.fact.039]. Both sides want to close [m14.fact.038]; the exercise is about *interests behind positions* and cross-issue trades. {#b:4a5b4f84}

### The three title problems, correctly separated (who controls each cure) {#b:1ef8b748}

**Problem 1 — the access easement (buyer's dealbreaker).** The tract has no public-road frontage; its only all-weather access is Cimarron Road, which crosses the neighboring Sandoval parcel [m14.fact.015]. The recorded 1991 easement's metes-and-bounds description follows a fence line that does **not** match where the road runs today [m14.fact.016] — the survey confirms the physical road lies 60–110 feet north of the described route and, for part of its length, crosses Sandoval's land outside any recorded easement [m14.fact.024]. Result: the title company will **not insure access as written** [m14.fact.017], which is fatal because Nakamura's fully-approved loan is conditioned on the lender's title company insuring access without exception [m14.fact.033]. **This is the buyer's true dealbreaker.** Governing law the student must find: express-easement construction and recording (Tex. Prop. Code, Titles 2–3), plus the landlocked-tract doctrines — easement by necessity, easement by estoppel, and easement by implication `[instructor: verify before teaching]`. Cure menu: a **corrective/re-granted easement**, a **negotiated grant from Sandoval**, or a **suit to establish an easement**. The key actor is **Sandoval**, not Whitfield — Sandoval will grant a clean surveyed easement for cash plus a promise construction traffic won't tear up his cattle guards [m14.fact.029]. A top student sees that the cheapest, fastest cure (buying an easement from Sandoval) is entirely within the *buyer's* power and doesn't need Whitfield at all. {#b:309006be}

**Problem 2 — the severed mineral estate (a constraint, not a curable defect).** A 1978 deed reserved the minerals, now owned by third party Ocotillo Minerals, LP [m14.fact.018]. **Whitfield cannot convey what he does not own** — the deed will except the minerals regardless of warranty form. The real risk to Nakamura's *surface-intensive solar plan* is the **surface/mineral dominance doctrine**: Texas treats the mineral estate as dominant, so the mineral owner may use as much surface as reasonably necessary to develop the minerals (see the accommodation doctrine; Getty Oil Co. v. Jones, 470 S.W.2d 618 (Tex. 1971)) `[instructor: verify before teaching]`. There is no current drilling and no produced lease [m14.fact.020], which lowers near-term risk — but the A-answer researches the **accommodation doctrine** and how a solar array (a fixed, surface-intensive use) interacts with a dominant mineral estate, and considers a surface-use waiver/accommodation agreement from Ocotillo. This is a *diligence and risk-allocation* issue, not something to "fix" in the deed. {#b:c64337a2}

**Problem 3 — the seller's newly demanded well reservation (the trade good).** During title review Whitfield asked — for the first time, contrary to the original contract [m14.fact.022] — to reserve a surface easement around the Cimarron well [m14.fact.021]. The survey shows the well sits **inside the footprint the solar developer wants** [m14.fact.025], and an old pipeline runs from the well to Whitfield's homestead [m14.fact.026]. This is where interest-based negotiation earns its keep: Whitfield's *interest* is water for his homestead, not that specific patch of ground. A reserved surface easement must be papered into contract and deed under the statute of frauds (Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 26.01) `[instructor: verify before teaching]`; negotiate its **size, location, duration, and relocatability** so the well/pipeline is served **without blocking the solar footprint** — e.g., a bounded, relocatable easement, or Whitfield takes water via a metered tap/new line the buyer funds. The A-answer turns the seller's "line in the sand" into a solvable engineering-and-drafting problem. {#b:0c2bb57f}

### The confidential dynamics (the negotiation's real engine) {#b:c7d57376}

- **Nakamura's hidden leverage-loss.** His signed solar option/ground lease (~180 acres) is worth far more than the land, but the developer can **walk if he can't deliver clear access + unobstructed western pasture by March 31, 2026** [m14.fact.030] — *earlier* than the extended April 30 closing [m14.fact.011]. That March 31 deadline is his real pressure point, which he does **not** want Whitfield to know [m14.fact.031]. Concealed: he'd actually pay ~$40,000 more or buy the Sandoval easement himself rather than lose the tract, though his stated position is that title defects should *reduce* the price [m14.fact.032]. {#b:6e1b05a4}
- **Whitfield's hidden pressure.** The well is his homestead's **only reliable water** [m14.fact.035] (concealed), and he's under a **summer-2026 balloon payment** and needs the sale to close — can't afford to walk or litigate [m14.fact.036] (concealed). He privately knows the road always ran where it runs and the 1991 description was just sloppy [m14.fact.037]. {#b:bb31889a}

The elegant read: **both sides secretly need this deal and secretly have deadlines.** Neither can afford a fight. That mutual (hidden) urgency is the zone of agreement — the A-student who surfaces the client's own priorities and *infers* the seller's likely interests can structure a deal that gives Whitfield reliable water (bounded/relocated easement or a funded replacement line), gives Nakamura an unobstructed flat and insurable access (buyer-funded Sandoval grant + corrective easement), excepts the minerals with an accommodation strategy, and allocates cure costs against a modest price adjustment — closeable well before March 31. {#b:767c7333}

### The A-grade conclusion {#b:cacb2a0e}

Recommend: (1) cure access via a buyer-funded clean grant from Sandoval + corrective easement, so the lender's title exception clears [m14.fact.033]; (2) accept the mineral exception but pursue a surface-accommodation/waiver from Ocotillo to protect the array; (3) grant Whitfield a **bounded, relocatable** well easement (or fund a replacement water line) that keeps the solar footprint clear; (4) trade cost allocation/price against those cures; and (5) drive to close on the **March 31 timeline** the client actually faces — without ever revealing that deadline or his reservation price to the other side. Deed form: negotiate **special vs. general warranty** as risk allocation around the easement and mineral exception. {#b:c3f09c3f}

## 3. Deliverable Model-Answer Outline (mapped to rubric.json criteria + weights) {#b:14bd7ad6}

Total declared: **185.5 points.** {#b:094298d6}

### c01 — Fact development & title-issue diagnosis (40 pts) {#b:7bd36033}
- **c01.s01 Access-easement defect (20):** Recognizes the recorded 1991 easement doesn't match the surveyed road [m14.fact.016], [m14.fact.024] and that the title company won't insure access [m14.fact.017] — the loan-fatal defect [m14.fact.033]. {#b:b5083993}
- **c01.s02 Mineral & well reservations grasped (20):** Correctly characterizes the severed mineral estate (third-party owned, not conveyable) [m14.fact.018] and the seller's newly demanded well reservation [m14.fact.021] as **distinct** issues — one a constraint, one a negotiable trade. {#b:a1022cee}

### c02 — Negotiation strategy & execution (60 pts) — *the largest criterion* {#b:362b6a9f}
- **c02.s01 Interests vs. positions / SSNP quality (30):** Identifies the client's true interests (unobstructed flat, insurable access, the hidden March 31 deadline [m14.fact.031]) AND infers the seller's likely interests (homestead water [m14.fact.034], need to close [m14.fact.036]) — not just stated positions. Best answers name the mutual hidden urgency as the ZOPA. {#b:3629ce8b}
- **c02.s02 Cross-issue tradeoffs to a closeable deal (30):** Proposes realistic trades — buyer-funded Sandoval easement grant, corrective easement, bounded/relocatable well easement (or funded replacement water line), mineral accommodation strategy, deed-form and cost/price allocation — that **both sides could accept** and that clear before March 31. {#b:a2da1abb}

### c03 — Client counseling on options & risk (35 pts) {#b:5aa859d9}
- Plain-language advice on the realistic options, costs, risks, and timelines; elicits client-only decisions (walk vs. close, how much to spend on the easement, whether to concede the well). Honors the client's real deadline without revealing it to the other side. {#b:c05bed85}

### c04 — Drafting deed & access terms (30 pts) {#b:75d541bc}
- Clear proposed terms: **deed form and warranty** (special vs. general), **access-easement cure and cost allocation**, and **the well reservation** (bounded, located, duration, relocatable) papered to satisfy the statute of frauds `[instructor: verify before teaching]` and to except the minerals. {#b:10d79792}

### c05 — Professional responsibility / no-contact (20.5 pts) {#b:c4c81999}
- Recognizes Whitfield is represented, refrains from direct contact under Rule 4.02, AND **counsels the client to stop his direct texting** [m14.fact.041] and route through Faircloth. The client-texting wrinkle is what separates a full-credit answer from a rote one. {#b:c964d35d}

## 4. Grading Guidance {#b:f24e7cd9}

### A / B / C discriminators {#b:b4b9c632}
- **A (176–185.5):** Treats the three problems as an **interlocking trade**, not a checklist; surfaces the client's solar option and March 31 deadline through rapport; infers the seller's hidden water and financial pressure; structures a closeable deal (buyer-funded access cure + relocatable well easement + mineral accommodation + price/cost allocation) that clears before March 31; guards the client's reservation price absolutely; and handles Rule 4.02 **including** shutting down the client's direct texting. {#b:b3972671}
- **B (158–167):** Diagnoses all three title issues and proposes reasonable cures; spots Rule 4.02; but treats the issues as independent, under-earns the confidential facts, or mis-times the negotiation by anchoring to the April 30 closing rather than the real March 31 deadline. {#b:9cf6546e}
- **C (~139):** Misreads the mineral estate as curable or as Whitfield's to convey; treats the well reservation as a flat "no"; misses that access is the loan-fatal dealbreaker; contacts the seller directly or ignores the client's texting; or reveals the client's leverage. {#b:b0991292}

### Common errors (watch for these) {#b:c31c3ff1}
1. **Contacting Whitfield directly OR ignoring the client's direct texting** [m14.fact.041] — the signature ethics failure (c05). Both must be caught and cured. {#b:a9d77de8}
2. **Trying to "fix" the mineral estate** — students who think the deed can convey or clear the third-party minerals misunderstand severance; the deed excepts them, and the real work is the accommodation strategy for the solar use. {#b:3f8c1368}
3. **Treating the well as a binary** — a student who advises "just refuse the reservation" misses that Whitfield's *interest* (water), not that patch of ground, is what matters, and that a relocatable easement or funded replacement solves it. {#b:91c2b4b3}
4. **Anchoring to the wrong deadline** — planning to the April 30 closing and missing that the developer can walk March 31 [m14.fact.031]; the whole timeline is wrong. {#b:c373eaf4}
5. **Revealing the client's leverage** — disclosing the solar value or the reservation price [m14.fact.032] to the other side hands away the negotiation. {#b:b017ed5d}
6. **Missing that access is buyer-solvable** — not seeing that a buyer-funded Sandoval grant sidesteps Whitfield entirely and is the fastest cure. {#b:3146ad95}

### Re-write demands (trigger a mandatory revision if) {#b:9cbd5f3b}
- The student **contacted Whitfield directly** or **failed to counsel the client to stop texting him** — require a written Rule 4.02 analysis and a corrected communications plan. {#b:22a1784c}
- The analysis **treats the severed minerals as curable/conveyable by Whitfield** — require a corrected severance/accommodation analysis. {#b:b209e462}
- The negotiation plan **anchors to April 30** and misses the March 31 developer deadline — require a re-timed strategy. {#b:599d10d6}
- The draft deal terms **omit a statute-of-frauds-compliant, bounded well reservation** or leave access uninsurable — require corrected terms. {#b:54d21cf3}
