# Witness Statement — Dr. Elias Vandermolen {#b:de663708}

**Witness:** Elias Vandermolen, M.D.
**Role:** Harold Prewitt's treating physician (family medicine)
**Date of statement:** July 8, 2025
**Taken at:** Heron Point Family Medicine, Manatee County, Florida {#b:a6a44944}

---

I am Dr. Elias Vandermolen. I have practiced family medicine in Manatee County for twenty-two years. Harold Prewitt was my patient for more than a decade, until his death in February of this year. {#b:2172c416}

Beginning in 2023, I followed Harold for two things running together: a movement disorder and heart trouble. I diagnosed him with early-stage Parkinson's disease. What most people notice with that is the tremor — his right hand shook, and it got worse when he was tired or stressed. I want to be careful about what the diagnosis means. Parkinson's is a movement disorder first. It can come with some slowing of thinking over time, and Harold did have days where he was foggy and slow to find a word. But a tremor and a slow day are not the same thing as being unable to understand your own affairs. {#b:8b50cd43}

On February 28, 2024, Harold came in for an appointment. His wife had told me the family wanted him seen around that time because he was going to update some legal papers, and I made a point of assessing his orientation and understanding at that visit. My notes from that day, which I have reviewed, record the following. Harold knew who he was, where he was, and roughly the date. He could describe his property in general terms — the house on the water, his accounts, his belongings. He knew he had a wife, Vivian, and a son, Dale, and he named them both. He understood that a will is the document that says who gets your things after you die, and he told me he intended to sign a new one soon. {#b:60a9b812}

What Harold did not have was a reliable memory for recent, day-to-day details — what he had eaten, who had called that week, small things. That is consistent with his condition and his age. But his memory for long-standing matters — his family, his house, the arc of his life — was intact at that visit. {#b:d4b701b5}

I have been asked whether, in my medical opinion, Harold understood what he was doing when he made a will around that time. Based on my examination of him on February 28, he understood the general nature and extent of his property, he knew who his wife and son were, and he understood what a will does. His hand shook when he wrote, but his mind was his own that day. I did not observe him to be confused about those matters, and I saw no sign that anyone was pressuring him, though I would not necessarily have seen that in an office visit. {#b:f68c60c3}

I offer this as his physician, from my own records and recollection. I am not taking a side between Mr. Prewitt's wife and his son. {#b:64aeca50}

*/s/ Elias Vandermolen, M.D.* {#b:1f488010}
