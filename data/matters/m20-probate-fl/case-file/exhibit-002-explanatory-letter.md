# Exhibit 002 — Explanatory Letter of Harold W. Prewitt (March 14, 2024) {#b:abae25b9}

*Case-file exhibit m20.exh.002. A letter written in the testator's voice and signed the same day as the will. Facts only.* {#b:94fe7ec5}

March 14, 2024 {#b:9e38a6ba}

To my family, and to anyone who wonders why I set things up the way I did — {#b:177a0a92}

I am writing this in my own words, with my lawyer's help, on the same day I am signing my new will. I want the reasons in one place so nobody has to guess. {#b:7f063c4e}

I am leaving the house on Heron Point to my wife, Vivian. I know my old will only let her live there and then passed the house to my son. I have thought hard about it, and I have changed my mind on purpose. Vivian gave up her own place and her own work to take care of me. She has been at my side every single day through the tremor, the heart scares, all of it. The house is the thing I have to give her, and I want her to have it, free and clear, without having to worry that it gets taken out from under her someday. {#b:55282e52}

Dale, this is not me being angry at you. Years ago I paid off a great deal of your debt — around eighty thousand dollars — when you needed it, and I was glad to do it. That was real help at a hard time, and I count it as part of what I've given you. We have not been close these last years, and I wish that were different. I am leaving you fifty thousand dollars. It is less than I am leaving Vivian, and that is my decision, made with a clear head. {#b:f3f61c71}

Nobody talked me into this. Vivian was not in the room when I met with the lawyer, and she is not in the room as I sign. These are my wishes. {#b:ff52cc42}

_/s/ Harold W. Prewitt_
Harold W. Prewitt {#b:f83d8177}
