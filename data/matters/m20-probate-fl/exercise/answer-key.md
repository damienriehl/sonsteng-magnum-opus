## 1. Model Case Analysis (keyed to fact anchors)

### 1.1 Posture
The firm represents **the Estate of Harold W. Prewitt, through Vivian Prewitt-Cole as personal representative** [role `m20.role.proponent-counsel`], **defending** the March 14, 2024 will [m20.fact.025] against Dale's June 2025 petition to revoke probate [m20.fact.044]. The contest asserts three grounds the student must **separate**: (a) lack of testamentary capacity, (b) undue influence, and (c) an improper homestead devise [m20.fact.045]. Pending in the Probate Division, Circuit Court, Twelfth Judicial Circuit, Manatee County [m20.fact.042]–[m20.fact.043]. The will leaves the Heron Point home outright to Vivian, pours the residue into a revocable trust for her, gives Dale a $50,000 bequest, and names Vivian PR [m20.fact.027]–[m20.fact.028].

### 1.2 Issue A — Testamentary capacity (rubric c02.s01, 30 pts)
Capacity is measured **at the moment of execution**, and asks whether the testator understood (i) the nature/extent of his property, (ii) the natural objects of his bounty, and (iii) the practical effect of making a will (the Florida capacity formulation; see Fla. Stat. ch. 732) `[instructor: verify before teaching]`. **A diagnosis is not incapacity.** Harold's early-stage Parkinson's with a tremor and some slowing [m20.fact.013] does not establish incapacity. The estate's proof is strong and contemporaneous: Dr. Vandermolen's **Feb. 28, 2024** assessment — two weeks before signing — recording that Harold understood his property, knew his wife and son as the objects of his bounty, and knew he was making a will, with long-standing memory intact despite some recent-events lapses [m20.fact.031]–[m20.fact.033]; the disinterested attesting witness Tancredi (Harold "clearly stated he wanted Vivian to have the house," though tired) [m20.fact.034]; Vivian's account he was himself that morning [m20.fact.035]; and the **self-proving affidavit** [m20.fact.026]. The countervailing "bad days" evidence (Bastien) [m20.fact.052] goes to weight, not to capacity-at-execution. **Model conclusion: capacity is the estate's strongest ground.**

### 1.3 Issue B — Undue influence & the presumption (rubric c02.s02, 30 pts)
This is the real battleground and is **distinct from capacity** — it asks whether someone overbore Harold's free will. In Florida a **presumption of undue influence** arises where a substantial beneficiary (i) occupied a **confidential relationship** with the decedent and (ii) was **active in procuring** the will — the classic *In re Estate of Carpenter*, 253 So.2d 697 (Fla. 1971) factors; and by statute the presumption **shifts the burden of proof** to the beneficiary (Fla. Stat. § 733.107(2)) `[instructor: verify before teaching]`. The contestant stacks the presumption facts: Vivian lived with Harold and was his caregiver [m20.fact.014], held his **durable power of attorney** [m20.fact.015], was a joint account signer [m20.fact.016], **first raised updating the will and made the appointment** [m20.fact.047], and benefits from a departure from the 2019 life-estate-then-Dale plan [m20.fact.011]. The estate answers with **independence-of-execution** counter-facts: Harold **met counsel alone** while Vivian waited in reception [m20.fact.021]–[m20.fact.022]; he gave **independent, documented reasons** (Vivian's sacrifice; Dale's earlier ~$80k gift and estrangement) [m20.fact.023]–[m20.fact.024]; a **disinterested witness** [m20.fact.026], [m20.fact.034]; and his own **signed explanatory letter** in his voice [m20.fact.029]–[m20.fact.030]. A model answer concedes the presumption likely arises, then argues the estate rebuts it with the alone-meeting, the documented reasons, and the letter — while candidly handling the adverse facts (below).

### 1.4 Issue C — Homestead devise (rubric c03, 40 pts) — the Florida hook
A **pure question of law** layered on top. The Heron Point home was Harold's homestead [m20.fact.006]; at death he was survived by a **spouse (Vivian)** and **one adult son (Dale)** and had **no minor children** [m20.fact.036]. Under Art. X, § 4(c), Fla. Const., and Fla. Stat. § 732.401, homestead devise is restricted when the decedent is survived by a spouse **or a minor child** `[instructor: verify before teaching]`. **Key teaching payoff:** because Harold left **no minor child**, the homestead **may be devised to the surviving spouse in fee** — which is exactly what the 2024 will does [m20.fact.037], [m20.fact.040]. Dale's blanket theory that homestead "can't be devised away from the descendant" [m20.fact.038] therefore **likely fails on the merits** — but only research reveals the surviving-spouse exception. The **prenuptial waiver** adds nuance, not a defeat: Vivian waived her elective share and homestead *rights* [m20.fact.009], [m20.fact.039]; a valid spousal waiver (Fla. Stat. § 732.702) can *free* an owner to devise homestead more broadly, but here the owner voluntarily devised it **to** the waiving spouse, and § 6 of the prenup preserves voluntary gifts [instructor notes] `[instructor: verify before teaching]`. **Model conclusion: the waiver does not void an affirmative gift of the home to Vivian; Dale's homestead theory fails.**

### 1.5 The adverse facts to confront (candor)
The estate must be candid, not defensive, about: Vivian **first raised** the update and **made the appointment** [m20.fact.047]; the bitter phone fight with Dale two months before signing [m20.fact.048]; and ~**$12,000 in account withdrawals, some undocumented**, for care/reimbursement [m20.fact.049], which raise **fiduciary-accounting and self-dealing/candor** concerns [m20.fact.050]. Bastien's overheard "Dale only calls when he needs money" remark [m20.fact.053] is a modest bad fact. A model answer flags the withdrawals as a fiduciary-accounting issue to **cure and account for**, not bury, and prepares a proper PR accounting.

**RULE 4.2 — the graded trap.** Dale is represented by Renfield Ostrowski, Esq. Under R. Regulating Fla. Bar 4-4.2 `[instructor: verify before teaching]`, estate counsel **may not contact him directly** about the contest without his lawyer's consent — route through Ostrowski and formal discovery. Attempting to interview him fires the teaching moment and loses c04.s02. His motive/debt facts [m20.fact.055]–[m20.fact.056] are impeachment targets to reach through discovery, not a direct interview.

### 2.4 The advocate-witness problem (rubric c04.s01) — the distinctive PR beat
Because **Amara Ellingboe drafted the will and supervised its execution**, she is a **likely necessary witness** on capacity and the absence of undue influence [m20.fact.057] (the alone-meeting, Harold's stated reasons, the drafting memo exh.005, the letter). Under the advocate-witness rule, **R. Regulating Fla. Bar 4-3.7** `[instructor: verify before teaching]`, a lawyer generally may not act as advocate at a trial in which the lawyer is likely to be a necessary witness (subject to exceptions). The firm is a **two-lawyer** practice, so a model answer reasons about staffing: have the **other firm lawyer** (or outside/co-counsel) try any contested hearing while Ellingboe testifies, and note the rule's imputation nuances. Missing this is a common and heavily weighted error.

## 4. Grading Guidance

- **Separate the three challenges.** The core discipline is keeping capacity, undue influence, and homestead distinct. The signature error is treating the physician's assessment as proof of no undue influence — it speaks only to **capacity**. Penalize the merge; reward the separation.
- **The homestead payoff rewards research.** Do not credit a student who assumes Dale's homestead theory automatically wins **or** automatically loses. The A answer researches the surviving-spouse / no-minor-child exception and the prenup's preservation of voluntary gifts and concludes the devise to Vivian stands.
- **The advocate-witness catch is heavily weighted.** A student who misses that the drafting firm is now a witness — and does not reason about staffing the two-lawyer firm under Rule 4-3.7 — misses a distinctive, foreseeable point.
- **Candor on the withdrawals.** The client is sympathetic, which tempts students to skip the hard questions. Reward students who probe the ~$12k withdrawals [m20.fact.049] and counsel a proper accounting; penalize those who bury them because the client is likable.
- **Rule 4.2 bright line.** Any direct contact with Dale is a hard miss on c04.s02. Credit routing through Ostrowski / discovery.
- **Undue-influence rebuttal is fact-intensive.** Credit the alone-meeting, documented independent reasons, disinterested witness, and the signed explanatory letter as the estate's rebuttal of the presumption — and candid handling of the presumption facts (POA, caregiver, arranged appointment, plan departure) rather than pretending they don't arise.
- **Two-axis debrief** per `docs/research/interview-pedagogy.md`: Axis A fact coverage (+ Rule 4.2 flag) and Axis B relational experience rated in character (this grieving client rates being *believed* and heard highly).
