## What this exercise is teaching

This is a client-interview and issue-spotting exercise built on a Florida will contest. The pedagogical spine is the discipline of separating three challenges students reflexively merge: **testamentary capacity**, **undue influence**, and a **pure homestead-devise question of law**. The facts are deliberately two-sided: nearly every "good" fact for the estate has a shadow, and a strong student will hold both. The homestead layer is the distinctively Florida hook.

## The three issues (instructor-verified)

- **Testamentary capacity.** Measured at the moment of execution. Florida's formulation asks whether the testator understood the nature and extent of his property, the natural objects of his bounty, and the practical effect of the will. A diagnosis (here early-stage Parkinson's with some cognitive slowing) does not equal incapacity. The estate's best capacity evidence is the physician's Feb. 28, 2024 assessment (exh.006), the drafting attorney meeting the client alone (exh.005), the disinterested attesting witness (witness-tancredi), and the self-proving affidavit. See generally Fla. Stat. ch. 732.
- **Undue influence.** In Florida a **presumption of undue influence** arises where a substantial beneficiary occupied a confidential relationship with the decedent and was active in procuring the will (the classic *Carpenter* factors). The presumption shifts the burden of production; the contestant will stack caregiver-status, the power of attorney, control of access, arranging the appointment (fact.047), and the departure from the 2019 plan. The estate answers with independence-of-execution facts: the client met counsel alone, gave documented independent reasons, and signed his own explanatory letter (exh.002).
- **Homestead devise (the Florida hook).** Art. X, § 4(c), Fla. Const., and Fla. Stat. § 732.401 restrict devise of homestead when the decedent is survived by a spouse or a minor child. Key teaching point: Harold left **no minor child** and **was survived by a spouse**, so the homestead **may** be devised to the surviving spouse in fee — which is exactly what the 2024 will does. Dale's blanket "you can't devise homestead away from the descendant" theory therefore likely **fails on the merits**, but only research reveals this — a satisfying payoff. The prenuptial waiver (exh.004) adds nuance: a spouse's valid waiver of homestead rights can free an owner to devise homestead more broadly (see Fla. Stat. § 732.702), but here the owner devised it *to* the waiving spouse voluntarily; § 6 of the prenup expressly preserves voluntary gifts. Strong students will see the waiver does not defeat an affirmative devise to Vivian.

## Professional-responsibility layer (instructor-verified)

- **Lawyer as witness.** Because Amara Ellingboe drafted and supervised execution, she is a likely necessary witness on capacity and undue influence. Discuss the advocate-witness rule (R. Regulating Fla. Bar 4-3.7) and whether the firm can both testify and try the contest, or must bring in the other lawyer or outside counsel.
- **Rule 4.2.** Dale is represented by Renfield Ostrowski. A student for the estate may not contact him directly (R. Regulating Fla. Bar 4-4.2). The chat engine surfaces this as a teaching moment, not a silent block.
- **Fiduciary / candor.** Vivian's ~$12,000 in withdrawals, partly undocumented (fact.049), raise fiduciary-accounting and candor issues the student should flag, not bury.

## Interview coaching

The rapport-gated facts (fact.047, fact.048 for the client; fact.053, fact.054 for the aide) unlock only through genuine open-ended invitation, confidentiality reassurance, acknowledged emotion, and non-judgmental response — never through pressure. Students who lead with accusatory questions will not get the hard facts and should be coached on why.

## Common student misses

- Treating the physician's assessment as proof of no undue influence (it speaks only to capacity).
- Missing that the drafting firm is now a witness.
- Contacting Dale directly.
- Assuming Dale's homestead theory automatically wins — or automatically loses — without researching the surviving-spouse exception and the prenup's preservation of voluntary gifts.
- Failing to probe the withdrawals because the client is sympathetic.
