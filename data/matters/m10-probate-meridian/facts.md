# Ground-Truth Facts — In re the Estate of Mabel R. Thornquist {#b:ede207dc}

## The family and the property {#b:a7b12c41}

Mabel R. Thornquist was born in 1941 and was the widow of Ferdinand Thornquist, who died in 2017 [m10.fact.001]. For the last decades of her life she lived at the family lake home on Loon Rush Lake, a modest year-round house on the water in Sable County that she and Ferdinand had bought and improved over forty years [m10.fact.002]. Mabel had two adult children: her daughter, Eleanor Thornquist-Reddick, and her son, Ronald Thornquist [m10.fact.003]. Eleanor, who is fifty-eight, lives in Brightwater, roughly fifteen minutes from the lake home [m10.fact.004]. Ronald, who is fifty-five, lives in Elleston, about three hours away, and works as a building contractor [m10.fact.005]. {#b:6bccfc25}

Mabel had signed an earlier will in 2015. That prior will divided her estate in roughly equal shares between Eleanor and Ronald [m10.fact.006]. The lake home is Mabel's principal asset, valued at approximately $420,000; the total estate, including bank and investment accounts and personal property, is worth approximately $560,000 [m10.fact.045]. {#b:49a96e1f}

## Mabel's decline and Eleanor's caregiving {#b:502f31ef}

In early 2024 Mabel's health began to decline. She had a fall at the lake home in February 2024 and began experiencing intermittent memory lapses [m10.fact.007]. Her longtime physician, Dr. Theodore Brashear, diagnosed her with early-stage vascular-type cognitive impairment [m10.fact.008]. In March 2024, Eleanor moved into the lake home and became Mabel's primary caregiver, managing her medications, meals, appointments, and daily needs [m10.fact.009]. {#b:0b7f1bba}

That spring, Eleanor took on formal responsibility for Mabel's affairs. In April 2024 Mabel signed a financial power of attorney naming Eleanor as her attorney-in-fact [m10.fact.010]. Around the same time, Eleanor was added as a joint signer on Mabel's checking account so she could pay Mabel's bills [m10.fact.011]. {#b:677ae7c0}

Ronald was largely absent during this period. He had not visited his mother in about three years before her death [m10.fact.012]. The estrangement traced back to 2016, when Mabel gave Ronald $60,000 to start a contracting business that later failed; family relations soured over the money afterward [m10.fact.013]. Ronald resented that Eleanor lived nearby and, in his view, controlled who had access to their mother [m10.fact.014]. {#b:7a05ef6a}

## The new will {#b:4baea90a}

In September 2024, arrangements were made for Mabel to update her will, and she came to the offices of Ellingboe & Ravndal LLP [m10.fact.015]. Eleanor drove Mabel to the office and waited in the reception area during the meeting [m10.fact.016]. Amara Ellingboe, the senior attorney, met alone with Mabel; Eleanor was not in the room while the will was discussed [m10.fact.017]. Mabel told Ellingboe that she wanted Eleanor to have the lake home [m10.fact.018]. She gave two reasons: that Eleanor had given up a great deal to care for her, and that Ronald had already received a large gift during her lifetime [m10.fact.019]. {#b:9bda8092}

The new will was executed on October 18, 2024, at the firm's office [m10.fact.020]. It was witnessed by two attesting witnesses — Harriet Grenfell, a longtime friend of Mabel's, and Delia Fenwick, the firm's paralegal [m10.fact.021]. The will was notarized and included a self-proving affidavit [m10.fact.022]. Under the new will, the lake home and the residue of the estate pass to Eleanor [m10.fact.023]. Ronald receives a cash bequest of $25,000 [m10.fact.024]. Eleanor is named personal representative [m10.fact.025]. {#b:a40d4603}

At Mabel's request, the firm also drafted an explanatory letter, written in Mabel's voice, setting out in plain terms why she was leaving the lake home to Eleanor and a smaller sum to Ronald [m10.fact.026]. Mabel signed the explanatory letter the same day she signed the will, and it was kept with the will in the firm's file [m10.fact.027]. {#b:769da129}

## The capacity evidence {#b:c713fba0}

Dr. Brashear examined Mabel on October 4, 2024 — about two weeks before the will signing [m10.fact.028]. In his notes he recorded that Mabel understood the general nature and extent of her property, knew the natural objects of her bounty (her two children), and understood that she was making a will [m10.fact.029]. He also noted that while Mabel had lapses concerning recent day-to-day events, her memory of long-term matters and family relationships was intact [m10.fact.030]. {#b:92b0818d}

Lorna Sundberg, a home health aide, worked weekday afternoons at the lake home through 2024 and into 2025 [m10.fact.031]. She observed that Mabel had "good days and bad days," alert and clear at some times and confused at others [m10.fact.032]. By Eleanor's account, Mabel was alert and oriented on the morning of the signing [m10.fact.033]. Harriet Grenfell recalls that Mabel seemed tired that afternoon, but that she clearly stated she wanted Eleanor to have the lake place [m10.fact.034]. {#b:3da14b43}

## Death and the contest {#b:f9f21105}

Mabel died on June 22, 2025, at the lake home — about eight months after signing the new will [m10.fact.035]. Eleanor filed the will for probate in the Probate Division of the Meridian District Court, Sable County [m10.fact.036]. On August 11, 2025, the court entered an order admitting the will to probate and appointing Eleanor as personal representative [m10.fact.037]. On September 15, 2025, Ronald filed an objection and petition contesting the will [m10.fact.038]. Ronald alleges that Mabel lacked testamentary capacity and that Eleanor exercised undue influence over her [m10.fact.039]. He claims that Eleanor isolated their mother and controlled her finances [m10.fact.040]. The contest is assigned to the Hon. Malcolm Ferris [m10.fact.047]. {#b:41e7cba8}

## The complicating facts {#b:9f56e529}

Several facts cut against the estate's clean story. Eleanor was the one who first raised the idea that Mabel "should update her will," and Eleanor made the appointment with the firm [m10.fact.041]. About two months before the signing, Eleanor and Ronald had a bitter phone argument in which Ronald said the lake place should be sold and the proceeds split between them [m10.fact.042]. Between 2024 and 2025, Eleanor withdrew roughly $8,000 from Mabel's account for care expenses and reimbursements to herself, and some of those withdrawals are not well documented [m10.fact.043]. Eleanor is worried that the withdrawals could look like self-dealing [m10.fact.044]. {#b:51669c8f}

Ronald's own position is not free of complication. He privately concedes that his mother's decision to favor Eleanor hurt him and that money is part of why he is contesting [m10.fact.049]. Ronald is carrying significant debts and needs an inheritance [m10.fact.050]. Lorna Sundberg once overheard Eleanor tell Mabel, in a moment of frustration, that "Ronald doesn't care about you" [m10.fact.051]. Sundberg was paid through Eleanor and is uneasy about being pulled into the family dispute, fearing her account will be called biased [m10.fact.052]. {#b:ef598b59}

## The firm's position {#b:143bd788}

Ellingboe & Ravndal LLP drafted both the will and the explanatory letter, and the firm now defends the will against the contest; because Amara Ellingboe drafted and supervised the execution, she is a potential witness on capacity and the absence of undue influence [m10.fact.046]. The firm handled the original estate-planning work — the will and the explanatory letter — for a flat fee of $1,800, which was paid in 2024 [m10.fact.048]. {#b:f25414ba}

The defense therefore turns on two separate questions that the contest deliberately blurs together. The first is whether Mabel understood, at the moment she signed, what she owned, who her children were, and what a will does. The second is whether Eleanor overbore her mother's free will, given that Eleanor lived with her, held her power of attorney, arranged the appointment, and stood to gain the lake home. The record supplies evidence on both sides of each question, and the estate must be candid about the facts that cut against it — the undocumented withdrawals, Eleanor's role in setting up the meeting, and the overheard remark about Ronald — while marshaling the physician's assessment, the private attorney meeting, the disinterested witness, and Mabel's own signed letter of explanation. {#b:3df0bb24}
