# Instructor Notes — In re the Estate of Mabel R. Thornquist {#b:13f68862}

*Teaching notes for the instructor only. Do not distribute to students. No answer key is provided; intentional-error and model-answer layers are deferred. Citations below are to the Meridian canon and are marked instructor-verified-at-publish.* {#b:870f60ea}

## What this exercise is teaching {#b:6f8756e1}

This is a client-interview and issue-spotting exercise built on a will contest. The pedagogical spine is the discipline of separating **testamentary capacity** from **undue influence** — two challenges students reflexively merge. The matter is deliberately two-sided in its facts: nearly every "good" fact for the estate has a shadow, and a strong student will hold both. {#b:fdc6e555}

## The two doctrines (instructor-verified) {#b:d74e6f64}

- **Testamentary capacity.** Measured at the moment of execution. The classic formulation asks whether the testator understood the nature and extent of her property, the natural objects of her bounty, and the nature of the testamentary act. A diagnosis of cognitive impairment does not equal incapacity; lucid-interval and moment-of-execution evidence matter. The estate's best capacity evidence is the physician's October 4, 2024 assessment (exh.005), the drafting attorney meeting the client alone (exh.004), the disinterested attesting witness (witness-grenfell), and the self-proving affidavit. Meridian's will-contest limitations period is **1 year after the order admitting the will**, Mer. Stat. § 524.16 — confirm the contest here (filed Sept. 15, 2025 against an Aug. 11, 2025 order) is timely. {#b:e0c49634}
- **Undue influence.** In Meridian, as under general common law, a **presumption of undue influence** can arise from a confidential relationship plus suspicious circumstances plus the beneficiary's active participation in procuring the will; when it arises, the burden of going forward shifts to the proponent. The contestant will stack the classic factors: caregiver-beneficiary, financial power of attorney, control of access, participation in arranging the appointment (fact.041), and a departure from a long-standing equal split. The estate answers with independence-of-execution facts: the client met counsel alone, gave documented independent reasons, and signed her own explanatory letter (exh.002). {#b:1d5f9e8f}

## Professional-responsibility layer (instructor-verified) {#b:0afea694}

- **Lawyer as witness.** Because Amara Ellingboe drafted and supervised execution, she is a likely necessary witness on capacity and undue influence. Discuss the advocate-witness rule (Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 3.7) and whether the firm can both testify and try the contest, or whether it must arrange for the firm's other lawyer or outside counsel to try it. This is a real teaching moment about a small firm defending its own drafting work. {#b:cb590491}
- **Rule 4.2.** Ronald is represented by Placidia Ohnstad. A student representing the estate may not contact him directly about the contest (Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2). The chat engine surfaces this as a teaching moment, not a silent block. {#b:f51f382a}
- **Fiduciary / candor.** Eleanor's roughly $8,000 in withdrawals, partly undocumented (fact.043), raise fiduciary-accounting and candor issues the student should flag, not bury. {#b:d0ab97e0}

## Interview coaching {#b:fc381415}

The rapport-gated facts (fact.041, fact.042 for the client; fact.051, fact.052 for the aide) unlock only through genuine open-ended invitation, confidentiality reassurance, acknowledged emotion, and non-judgmental response — never through pressure. Students who lead with accusatory questions will not get the hard facts, and should be coached on why. {#b:23812a46}

## Common student misses {#b:5c5b2b9a}

- Treating the physician's letter as proof of no undue influence (it speaks only to capacity). {#b:430b0d07}
- Missing that the drafting firm is now a witness. {#b:e27a2722}
- Contacting Ronald directly. {#b:4ae25e91}
- Failing to probe the withdrawals because the client is sympathetic. {#b:2dcda64e}

## Teaching arc (how the session should move) {#b:042922af}

1. **Set the frame — two doctrines, not one.** Open by insisting on the capacity/undue-influence split before any facts are discussed. The whole matter is designed so that students who blur them fail predictably (see the answer key's Common Errors). Put the two questions on the board: *Did she understand what she was doing?* vs. *Did someone overbear her free will?* {#b:9cbcc8ab}
2. **Prep and chronology.** Students build a chronology and an interview plan from the case file. The trap to surface early: nearly every "good" estate fact has a shadow (the private meeting *and* Eleanor arranging the appointment; the explanatory letter *and* the possibility it reads as scripted; the disinterested witness *and* the tired afternoon). {#b:1cfa0711}
3. **The client interview (the graded core).** Eleanor is `distressed` and defensive; the two rapport-gated facts and the concealed withdrawals unlock only through confidentiality reassurance, open invitation, and a non-judgmental response — never pressure. This is where relational skill and fact-gathering are tested together. {#b:fb7aa03b}
4. **Analysis + counseling.** Students separate the grounds, map the evidence, and counsel a grieving fiduciary about the adverse facts (the withdrawals especially) with candor and care. {#b:a97483cf}
5. **The professional-responsibility reveal.** Save deliberate time for the advocate-witness dilemma and the Rule 4.2 no-contact issue — these are the moves that most distinguish strong students, and they are easy to skate past if the debrief runs short. {#b:0f82f650}
6. **Optional witness interview.** If assigned, Sundberg (neutral, available to both sides) tests whether students can handle a witness whose account both helps and hurts. {#b:77525d4b}

## Timing (roughly a 2-hour block; scale to your slot) {#b:1a704dff}

- **Framing + chronology review — 15 min.** Lock in the two-doctrine split; take chronologies. {#b:54f13f8e}
- **Interview-plan share-out — 15 min.** Which facts do students most need to confirm, and how will they earn the sensitive ones? {#b:b5efc1c2}
- **Client interview (Eleanor) — 30–40 min.** Live or asynchronous; ~20 exchanges. If live, rotate students so several attempt the rapport-gated turns. {#b:16c2c9a5}
- **Optional Sundberg interview — 15 min.** Only if the section has time. {#b:04a1711f}
- **Analysis / counseling work — 20 min.** Draft the capacity-vs-undue-influence split and the counseling approach to the withdrawals. {#b:7e3e3a00}
- **Debrief discussion — 25–30 min.** Run the questions below; the advocate-witness dilemma should get the largest single share. {#b:86b5820f}

## Debrief discussion questions {#b:dd068b90}

1. **The two doctrines.** Where does the *same* fact (the private meeting; the explanatory letter; Eleanor holding the POA) point one way on capacity and the opposite way on undue influence? What does that teach about why the doctrines must be argued separately? {#b:c647b448}
2. **Advocate-witness dilemma (spend real time here).** Amara Ellingboe drafted the will and is the only witness to the private meeting — should she testify, try the contest, both, or neither? Walk the Rule 3.7(a) exceptions, then the harder question: can her *partner* try the case while she testifies, or does the firm's stake in defending its own drafting work impute a conflict to the whole two-lawyer firm? What would you actually do on Monday morning? (See the answer key's Rule 3.7 subsection.) {#b:0bf8c8c4}
3. **Earning the hard facts.** Which interviewing moves unlocked Eleanor's role in arranging the will and the account withdrawals — and which moves shut her down? What does a `distressed` client teach about pressure vs. patience? {#b:b64dc30e}
4. **Counseling the sympathetic fiduciary.** How do you tell a grieving daughter that her undocumented withdrawals look like self-dealing *without* either alarming her into concealment or soft-pedaling a real fiduciary/candor problem? {#b:60dda915}
5. **Rule 4.2 in a family fight.** Ronald is family, not a stranger — does that make the no-contact rule feel different? Why does it still bind, and what is the correct route to his account? {#b:c710f6b9}
6. **If the will falls, then what?** If the 2024 will is set aside, does the 2015 will revive, or does the estate pass by intestacy — and why does Ronald end up in roughly the same place either way? What does the ~$25,000-vs-~$280,000 gap tell you about the real drivers of this contest? {#b:1ebf2e1d}

## Facilitation notes {#b:67333cf8}

- **Don't let the ethics issues get crowded out.** The advocate-witness and no-contact points are the highest-value discriminators; protect their time in the debrief. {#b:942e10c5}
- **Reward candor over cleverness.** The strongest students are the ones who *name* the estate's bad facts (the withdrawals, the arranged appointment, the overheard remark) and plan for them — not the ones who build a tidy one-sided story. {#b:0708673b}
- **Calibrate on the relational axis.** A student who "got" a gated fact by pressuring the client should score *lower*, not higher — the persona engine will not reward pressure, and neither should you. {#b:0ffcc041}
