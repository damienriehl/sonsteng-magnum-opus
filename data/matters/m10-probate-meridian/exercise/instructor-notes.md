# Instructor Notes — In re the Estate of Mabel R. Thornquist

*Teaching notes for the instructor only. Do not distribute to students. No answer key is provided; intentional-error and model-answer layers are deferred. Citations below are to the Meridian canon and are marked instructor-verified-at-publish.*

## What this exercise is teaching

This is a client-interview and issue-spotting exercise built on a will contest. The pedagogical spine is the discipline of separating **testamentary capacity** from **undue influence** — two challenges students reflexively merge. The matter is deliberately two-sided in its facts: nearly every "good" fact for the estate has a shadow, and a strong student will hold both.

## The two doctrines (instructor-verified)

- **Testamentary capacity.** Measured at the moment of execution. The classic formulation asks whether the testator understood the nature and extent of her property, the natural objects of her bounty, and the nature of the testamentary act. A diagnosis of cognitive impairment does not equal incapacity; lucid-interval and moment-of-execution evidence matter. The estate's best capacity evidence is the physician's October 4, 2024 assessment (exh.005), the drafting attorney meeting the client alone (exh.004), the disinterested attesting witness (witness-grenfell), and the self-proving affidavit. Meridian's will-contest limitations period is **1 year after the order admitting the will**, Mer. Stat. § 524.16 — confirm the contest here (filed Sept. 15, 2025 against an Aug. 11, 2025 order) is timely.
- **Undue influence.** In Meridian, as under general common law, a **presumption of undue influence** can arise from a confidential relationship plus suspicious circumstances plus the beneficiary's active participation in procuring the will; when it arises, the burden of going forward shifts to the proponent. The contestant will stack the classic factors: caregiver-beneficiary, financial power of attorney, control of access, participation in arranging the appointment (fact.041), and a departure from a long-standing equal split. The estate answers with independence-of-execution facts: the client met counsel alone, gave documented independent reasons, and signed her own explanatory letter (exh.002).

## Professional-responsibility layer (instructor-verified)

- **Lawyer as witness.** Because Amara Ellingboe drafted and supervised execution, she is a likely necessary witness on capacity and undue influence. Discuss the advocate-witness rule (Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 3.7) and whether the firm can both testify and try the contest, or whether it must arrange for the firm's other lawyer or outside counsel to try it. This is a real teaching moment about a small firm defending its own drafting work.
- **Rule 4.2.** Ronald is represented by Placidia Ohnstad. A student representing the estate may not contact him directly about the contest (Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2). The chat engine surfaces this as a teaching moment, not a silent block.
- **Fiduciary / candor.** Eleanor's roughly $8,000 in withdrawals, partly undocumented (fact.043), raise fiduciary-accounting and candor issues the student should flag, not bury.

## Interview coaching

The rapport-gated facts (fact.041, fact.042 for the client; fact.051, fact.052 for the aide) unlock only through genuine open-ended invitation, confidentiality reassurance, acknowledged emotion, and non-judgmental response — never through pressure. Students who lead with accusatory questions will not get the hard facts, and should be coached on why.

## Common student misses

- Treating the physician's letter as proof of no undue influence (it speaks only to capacity).
- Missing that the drafting firm is now a witness.
- Contacting Ronald directly.
- Failing to probe the withdrawals because the client is sympathetic.
