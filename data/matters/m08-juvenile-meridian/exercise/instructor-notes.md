# Instructor Notes — m08, In re the Welfare of T.M., a Minor

*Teaching notes only. Never distribute to students; keep out of the student packet. Citations below are to the Meridian canon and are marked instructor-verified-at-publish. No answer key or intentional-error layer here — those are deferred.*

## What this exercise is really testing

This is a **juvenile-defense intake and disposition** problem. The pedagogical center of gravity is not doctrine but **interviewing a frightened minor** and **counseling toward a hard disposition choice**, with a professional-responsibility trap sitting in plain sight. Strong students will (a) get Tobias's own account before the adults reshape it, (b) reach the two rapport-gated facts (naming Kellan; the "I kind of knew" admission) using the closed rapport triggers rather than pressure, (c) spot Rule 4.2 before contacting the co-respondent, and (d) tie the recovered-console fact to restitution leverage.

## Doctrine pointers (instructor-verified; Meridian canon)

- **Juvenile jurisdiction / procedure.** Juvenile Division of the Meridian District Court; delinquency petitions captioned *In re the Welfare of <initials>, a Minor*; records confidential (see meridian.json, court_structure.trial_courts.divisions, Juvenile Division). Assign Hon. Consuela Escamilla (juvenile) per the judge pool.
- **Theft value threshold.** Value governs the severity level; the analog research target is the general theft statute. Note the litigable point: the console's shelf value ($549.99) versus the store's near-zero *actual loss* after intact recovery — students should distinguish "value of property taken" from "restitution owed."
- **Fifth-degree assault.** Intent to inflict or attempt bodily harm; the doorway push's intent is the contested element. A self-treated scrape with no medical care is weak on "bodily harm."
- **Rule 4.2 (Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2).** No contact with a represented person about the subject of the representation absent counsel's consent. Kellan Dubourg is represented (public defender). This is the marquee ethics moment; the persona is configured to fire the in-app teaching moment for defense interviewers.
- **Confidentiality with a parent present (Rule 1.6 analog).** Flag the risk that a third party's presence (even a parent) can affect privilege; and that the client is the child, not the parent.
- **SOL / timing.** Not a limitations issue; the operative deadline is the **May 15, 2026** diversion-acceptance cutoff.

## Facts students should surface

- The **handoff is obscured** on CCTV and the **doorway contact is ambiguous** — reasonable-doubt fuel for a contested hearing (exh.007).
- **Recovered, undamaged** console → restitution leverage (exh.004).
- Client's **rapport-gated** disclosures: names Kellan (fact.030) only after confidentiality reassurance + a non-judgmental response; the "I kind of knew" admission (fact.031) only after acknowledged emotion + no interruption.
- **Concealed** prior uncharged shoplift (fact.032) — raises the honesty-in-diversion counseling problem; do not let students coach a false accountability statement, and discuss the ethics of accountability statements.
- Mother's **rapport-gated** items (unexplained game; overheard phone call) go to the retaliation concern and to disposition supports.

## Discussion prompts

1. Should counsel try to interview Kellan at all? Walk the Rule 4.2 analysis and the proper channel (his counsel).
2. Diversion vs. hearing: how do you counsel a client who wants "no record" but maintains he did nothing intentional, when diversion may require accepting responsibility?
3. How do you keep the child's voice primary when a distressed parent wants to decide?
4. Restitution: how does intact recovery reshape the number and the leverage?

## Common student errors

- Leading the minor client and adopting the adults' framing.
- Contacting the co-respondent directly (Rule 4.2 miss).
- Treating shelf value and restitution as the same number.
- Pushing diversion for speed without reconciling it with the innocence claim.
