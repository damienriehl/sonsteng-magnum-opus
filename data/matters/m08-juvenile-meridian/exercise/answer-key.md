## 1. Model Case Analysis (keyed to fact anchors)

### The alleged delinquent acts (elements the State must prove)

The petition alleges two counts of conduct that would be crimes if committed by an adult, adjudicated in the Juvenile Division of the Meridian District Court under the confidential, initials-only delinquency posture [m08.fact.022] `[instructor: verify before teaching — meridian.json court_structure.trial_courts.divisions, Juvenile Division]`. A delinquency adjudication requires proof **beyond a reasonable doubt** of every element (the standard the student must locate and state — the packet supplies none).

- **Count I — Theft.** Elements the State must prove: (1) the child took, or exercised control over, (2) movable property of another (the Nexus X console owned by VoltHaus), (3) without consent, (4) with intent to deprive the owner permanently, and (5) a value that governs the severity level [m08.fact.022], per the petition's own framing [delinquency-petition, exh.003]. The contested element is **mental state** — whether Tobias *knowingly* participated or was an unwitting recipient of a box pushed into his hands [m08.fact.008], [m08.fact.017].
- **Count II — Assault in the fifth degree.** Elements: the child (1) intentionally inflicted, or attempted to inflict, (2) bodily harm on Curtis Vanderhoek [m08.fact.022]. The contested element is again **intent** — a deliberate shove versus a panicked push to get past a person blocking the door [m08.fact.023]. Both counts turn on the same two hidden facts: whether Tobias was part of the plan, and whether the shove was deliberate [m08.fact.017], [m08.fact.018].

### The State's strongest theory (know it to beat it)

The A-grade student can articulate the prosecution's best case before dismantling it:

- **Theft via participation / accomplice framing.** The State leans on the loss-prevention officer's account that Tobias appeared to be "with" the older group, took the box knowingly, and left the store holding unpaid merchandise [m08.fact.018]. Tobias did not set the box down or call for help; he walked toward the exit with it [m08.fact.009], and he was caught in the parking lot after dropping it [m08.fact.013]. The console's shelf value is $549.99 [m08.fact.007], which the State will use as the "value of property taken" to push the severity level.
- **Assault as a deliberate shove.** Vanderhoek's written account is that the push was hard, two-handed, and on purpose — that Tobias "drove into" him to break free, knocking him into a display and onto one knee [m08.fact.012], [m08.fact.018]. The State says a purposeful shove to escape supplies the intent element.
- **What the State does not know.** Two facts would help or hurt the State but are outside its knowledge. The co-respondent's private truth — that he handed the box to Tobias *on purpose* to use the unknown younger boy as a distraction [m08.fact.037] — actually undercuts the accomplice theory, but Dubourg conceals it. Tobias's uncharged prior shoplift at the same store [m08.fact.032] would support a knowledge/pattern inference, but it is uncharged and concealed; a student must never surface or invite it into the record (see §4).

### The juvenile-respondent's strongest theory (our client)

- **Theft — unwitting receipt, not knowing participation.** Tobias's account is that the two older teens grabbed the console, one pushed the box into his hands near the entrance saying "hold this," and both bolted [m08.fact.008]; he panicked and walked toward the exit instead of setting it down [m08.fact.009]. He is not close with the older teens [m08.fact.005] and had no advance knowledge of the plan [m08.fact.017]. The one neutral witness corroborates the core of this: sales associate Naomi Quintero saw the older teens hand the box to the younger boy and run, and says he "looked confused, not like he was in charge of anything" [m08.fact.019]. This is the mere-presence / unwitting-recipient defense — receiving property from another is not the same as participating in its taking.
- **Assault — a panicked reflex, not intent to harm.** Tobias says he pushed past Vanderhoek trying to get away in a panic, not to hurt him [m08.fact.017], [m08.fact.023]. The injury is a scraped, reddened elbow that Vanderhoek self-treated with a first-aid kit; he sought no medical care and finished his shift [m08.fact.015] — weak support for the "bodily harm" element. A panicked push to get past a person blocking the only exit is a classic absence-of-intent argument.
- **Value / loss.** The console was recovered undamaged just outside the doors and returned to sellable stock; the store's actual out-of-pocket loss was effectively **zero** [m08.fact.016], [store-inventory, exh.004]. The A student distinguishes **value of the property taken** (which may still be measured at shelf price for charging) from **restitution owed** (near zero after intact recovery) — a distinction that matters for both the severity level argument and the diversion negotiation.

### Strength of the identification and the proof gaps

This is **not** a stranger-identification case — Tobias was detained at the scene with the box [m08.fact.013]. The real "identification-reliability" problem is what the evidence can and cannot resolve about the **two contested moments**:

- **The obscured handoff.** The ceiling CCTV captured the older teens at the display pallet and the group drifting to the front, but a seasonal merchandise endcap partly blocks the point of transfer, so the frames do not clearly show the box being handed over or taken [m08.fact.020], [cctv-stills, exh.007]. Vanderhoek concedes in both his statement and the LP report that the exact transfer was partially obscured from his sightline by the same endcap [m08.fact.010], [lp-incident-report, exh.002]. **No witness clearly saw the handoff as a knowing taking by Tobias.**
- **The ambiguous doorway contact.** The CCTV shows contact and the officer going down, but the precise force and hand orientation are "not clearly resolvable at this frame rate and angle" [m08.fact.020], [cctv-stills, exh.007]. Quintero looked down to help a customer and missed the contact entirely; she did not hear anything said [m08.fact.019].
- **Witness reliability ledger.** *Vanderhoek* is an interested witness (the alleged victim and the store's LP officer) whose most damaging conclusions — "with the group," "on purpose" — are inferences, and whose view of the handoff was blocked [m08.fact.010], [m08.fact.018]. *Quintero* is the neutral witness but was ~30 feet away, looked away at the key moment, and heard nothing — yet what she is sure of (handoff + boys running + confused younger kid) helps the defense [m08.fact.019]. *Nwosu* speaks only to character and disposition supports, not the incident [m08.fact.044], [m08.fact.045]. *Officer Bagley* took statements and referred the matter; he adds procedure, not eyewitness detail on the contested moments [m08.fact.021]. Together these are **genuine reasonable-doubt fuel** on both counts.

### The diversion offer as the counseling/strategy fulcrum

The County Attorney's April 20 letter offers diversion — a written accountability statement, a 6–8 session accountability program, 30 hours of community service, any restitution owed, and 90 days of clean conduct — after which the petition is dismissed with **no adjudication of delinquency** [m08.fact.025], [diversion-offer-letter, exh.005]. The decision deadline is **May 15, 2026** [m08.fact.028]. This is the fulcrum of the whole matter for three reasons:

1. **It matches the client's overriding priority.** Tobias's dominant fear is that any adjudication or record ruins his plans for college and possibly the military [m08.fact.029]. Diversion is the only path that guarantees no adjudication — exactly what he says he wants.
2. **It collides with his innocence claim.** Diversion requires *accepting responsibility* in a written statement [diversion-offer-letter, exh.005], yet Tobias maintains he did nothing knowing or intentional [m08.fact.017]. The A-grade answer confronts this head-on rather than papering over it (see the honesty problem, §4).
3. **Restitution is live leverage.** Because the console was recovered undamaged, the store has not submitted a fixed restitution figure and its actual loss is near zero [m08.fact.016], [m08.fact.026], [store-inventory, exh.004]. Counsel should negotiate restitution toward nominal/zero rather than shelf price.

### What an A-grade deliverable concludes

A top answer reaches a **candid, client-centered recommendation**: on this record, **accepting a properly negotiated diversion is the realistic best outcome**, because (a) it delivers the client's paramount goal — no adjudication and no record [m08.fact.029]; (b) the alternative is a contested adjudicatory hearing before Hon. Consuela Escamilla [m08.fact.027] that, even with strong reasonable-doubt material, risks the very adjudication he most fears; and (c) the restitution term can be negotiated to near zero on the intact-recovery leverage [m08.fact.016]. **But** the A answer does not sell diversion glibly. It:

- Preserves the innocence claim by negotiating **accountability-statement language that is truthful** — accepting responsibility for walking out holding the box and pushing past the officer [m08.fact.009], not confessing to a theft plan he denies [m08.fact.017];
- Presents the genuine trade-off so the *client* decides — diversion's certainty vs. a contested hearing that could exploit the two proof gaps [m08.fact.020];
- Keeps the child's voice primary against a guilt-ridden, overwhelmed parent who may push for the fastest resolution [m08.fact.039], [rubric c02.s02];
- Flags the collateral honesty risk of the concealed prior shoplift [m08.fact.032] and the snitch/retaliation fear around naming Kellan [m08.fact.030], neither of which the student should invite into the record.

The candid bottom line: this is a *strong reasonable-doubt case that the client should probably still resolve by diversion*, because the downside asymmetry (an adjudication on his record) outweighs the upside of a contested win for a fifteen-year-old whose stated priority is a clean future.

## 4. Grading Guidance

### A / B / C discriminators (keyed to the letter_grade_map bands)
- **A (209–220):** Secures Tobias's own account first; earns **both** rapport-gated facts via the correct triggers without pressure [m08.fact.030], [m08.fact.031]; builds reasonable doubt on **both** proof gaps [m08.fact.020]; distinguishes value from restitution and uses the intact-recovery leverage [m08.fact.016]; **flawless Rule 4.2 handling** (routes through Kellan's counsel) [m08.fact.035]; counsels toward a properly negotiated diversion that honors the client's no-record priority [m08.fact.029] **while** preserving truthful accountability language and refusing to coach a false statement about the concealed prior [m08.fact.032]; keeps the child's voice primary over the anxious parent [m08.fact.039].
- **B (187–198):** Solid chronology and both defenses; spots Rule 4.2; but reaches only one gated fact, treats restitution and shelf value as the same number, or recommends diversion for speed without reconciling it with the innocence claim.
- **C (165–186):** Leads the minor and adopts the adults' framing; misses a proof gap; conflates value and restitution; hand-waves the diversion/innocence tension; shaky but not fatal on ethics.
- **Below C (< 165):** Contacts Kellan directly (Rule 4.2 violation); pressures the client for gated facts; or counsels a false accountability statement.

### Common errors (build on instructor-notes; do not duplicate)
1. **Contacting the co-respondent directly** — the signature ethics failure; automatic hit on c04.s01 [m08.fact.035].
2. **Leading the minor / adopting the adults' framing** — costs c01.s01 and the relational axis.
3. **Conflating shelf value with restitution** — misses the intact-recovery leverage [m08.fact.016]; costs c03.s02.
4. **Selling diversion for speed** without reconciling the accept-responsibility term with the innocence claim [m08.fact.017] — costs c03.s01.
5. **Mishandling the concealed prior** — either coaching a false accountability statement or (worse) inviting the uncharged charger incident [m08.fact.032] into the record; both are integrity failures under c04.s02.
6. **Letting the parent decide** — losing the child's voice and priorities [m08.fact.039]; costs c02.s02.

### Re-write-loop demands (trigger a mandatory revision if)
- The student **interviewed Kellan directly** — require a written Rule 4.2 analysis and a corrected investigation plan routing through his counsel.
- The **chronology omits either proof gap** (obscured handoff or ambiguous doorway contact) or misorders the incident/petition/diversion sequence — require a corrected, exhibit-anchored chronology.
- The counseling plan **treats restitution as shelf price** — require a corrected restitution position grounded in the $0 net loss [m08.fact.016].
- The deliverable **coaches a false accountability statement** or surfaces the concealed prior into the record — require a corrected, honest counseling plan and an ethics note on candor in the accountability process.
- The recommendation **overrides the client's stated priorities** or lets the parent decide — require a revision that re-centers the fifteen-year-old client's voice [m08.fact.029].
