# Ground-Truth Facts — In re the Welfare of T.M., a Minor (m08) {#b:8d41cc90}

*Master narrative for the matter. This is the authoritative account against which every persona, witness statement, and exhibit is pinned. Student-facing packets stay facts-only; no law is cited here.* {#b:94fe5ac0}

## The family {#b:1211b524}

Tobias "Toby" Merrow is fifteen years old, a tenth-grade student at Larkmont High School in Norsholm, Halden County [m08.fact.001]. He lives in a rented duplex with his mother, Dana Merrow, who is raising him alone; Tobias's father has not been part of his life since Tobias was seven [m08.fact.002]. Dana works two jobs — days as a home-health aide and several evenings a week at a grocery store — so household money is always tight, and she is frequently away from home during Tobias's after-school hours [m08.fact.003]. Tobias has no prior contacts with the juvenile or criminal system; this is his first [m08.fact.004]. He vaguely knows the two older teenagers involved in the incident from around the neighborhood but is not close with either of them [m08.fact.005]. {#b:2dd7c819}

## The incident {#b:d7594176}

On Saturday, March 14, 2026, at roughly 4:40 p.m., Tobias walked over to VoltHaus Electronics, a big-box electronics store in the Riverside Commons shopping center in Norsholm, to look at a newly released gaming console called the Nexus X [m08.fact.006]. VoltHaus stocks the Nexus X at a shelf price of $549.99, and its inventory system later showed one Nexus X console unaccounted for after the incident [m08.fact.007]. {#b:1ae8ccad}

According to Tobias, while he was near the front of the store the two older teens pulled a boxed Nexus X off a display pallet, and one of them pushed the box into Tobias's hands near the entrance vestibule, said "hold this," and then both of them bolted out the doors [m08.fact.008]. Tobias, suddenly holding the box and seeing the older boys run, panicked and walked toward the exit himself instead of setting it down or calling for help [m08.fact.009]. {#b:dbf4bd33}

Curtis Vanderhoek, a loss-prevention officer employed by VoltHaus, had already been watching the older teens on the floor and moved toward the doors [m08.fact.010]. Vanderhoek stepped in front of Tobias in the vestibule between the two sets of automatic doors and told him to stop and hand over the box [m08.fact.011]. A brief physical struggle followed: Tobias pushed past him, and Vanderhoek was knocked backward off balance, hitting a freestanding display stand and going down onto one knee, scraping his elbow [m08.fact.012]. Tobias dropped the boxed console just outside the first set of doors and was caught in the parking lot a moment later by Vanderhoek and a second store employee [m08.fact.013]. The two older teens were not caught that afternoon [m08.fact.014]. {#b:e19988f9}

Vanderhoek's injury was minor: a scraped and reddened elbow that he cleaned with the store's first-aid kit. He did not seek medical treatment and returned to his shift [m08.fact.015]. The console box was scuffed but the unit inside was undamaged and returned to inventory, so the store's actual out-of-pocket loss was effectively nothing once it was recovered [m08.fact.016]. {#b:5f7fb93b}

## Competing accounts {#b:8049bba0}

The accounts of what happened diverge on the two facts that matter most — whether Tobias was part of the plan, and whether the shove was deliberate. {#b:94860330}

Tobias insists he had no idea the older teens were going to grab a console, that he was simply handed the box, and that when he pushed past Vanderhoek he was trying to get away in a panic, not trying to hurt him [m08.fact.017]. In Vanderhoek's written account, by contrast, Tobias appeared to be "with" the older group, took the box knowingly, and shoved him hard and on purpose to break free [m08.fact.018]. {#b:32cce75c}

A VoltHaus sales associate, Naomi Quintero, was working a register about thirty feet away. She saw the two older teens hand a box to a younger boy near the entrance and then run, which partially corroborates Tobias's version, but she was not close enough to hear anything or to see the contact in the doorway clearly [m08.fact.019]. The store's ceiling CCTV captured the older teens at the display pallet and the contact in the doorway, but the actual handoff of the box is partly blocked from the camera by a merchandise endcap, leaving that key moment ambiguous on the video [m08.fact.020]. {#b:77209936}

## Charges and posture {#b:bbfb6a1b}

Norsholm Police Department Officer Trent Bagley responded to the store, took statements, released Tobias to his mother that evening, and referred the matter to juvenile intake [m08.fact.021]. The Halden County Attorney's Office subsequently filed a delinquency petition captioned *In re the Welfare of T.M., a Minor*, in the Juvenile Division of the Meridian District Court, alleging conduct that would be theft and fifth-degree assault if committed by an adult [m08.fact.022]. The assault allegation turns entirely on intent — a deliberate shove versus a panicked push to get past a person blocking the door [m08.fact.023]. Tobias made his first appearance in the Juvenile Division on April 2, 2026, where a denial was entered on his behalf [m08.fact.024]. {#b:51a826c4}

## The diversion offer {#b:de444198}

On April 20, 2026, the County Attorney extended a written offer to place Tobias in the county's juvenile diversion program: if he completes community service, an accountability program, and pays any restitution the store claims, the petition would be dismissed with no adjudication of delinquency on his record [m08.fact.025]. Because the console was recovered undamaged, the amount of restitution the store can actually claim is a live negotiation point [m08.fact.026]. The alternative to diversion is a contested delinquency hearing before Hon. Consuela Escamilla, sitting in the Juvenile Division [m08.fact.027]. The County Attorney set a decision deadline of May 15, 2026 for accepting or rejecting the diversion offer [m08.fact.028]. {#b:f6fe63ac}

Tobias's overriding fear is that any adjudication or record will follow him and ruin his plans — he has talked about college and possibly the military, and believes a "record" closes those doors [m08.fact.029]. {#b:ad3c640f}

## What Tobias holds back {#b:a25f303f}

Two things Tobias does not volunteer. First, one of the older teens is a sixteen-year-old he knows by name — Kellan — whom he had talked with a few times before; Tobias is frightened of being seen as a snitch and of retaliation if he names him [m08.fact.030]. Second, Tobias privately concedes that when the box was pushed into his hands he "kind of knew something was off" but froze instead of dropping it [m08.fact.031]. {#b:70878481}

Deeper still, and concealed, is that several months earlier Tobias slipped a phone charger from the same VoltHaus store into his backpack and walked out without paying, and was never caught; he is terrified this uncharged incident will surface [m08.fact.032]. Tobias genuinely does not know whether the two older teens were ever identified or charged [m08.fact.033]. {#b:639324c2}

## The co-respondent {#b:6bdd09f3}

In fact, the older teen Tobias calls Kellan is Kellan Dubourg, age sixteen [m08.fact.034]. Investigators later identified Dubourg from the CCTV footage and a tip, and the County Attorney charged him separately; he is represented by the Halden County Public Defender's office [m08.fact.035]. Dubourg ran from the store and was not caught the day of the incident [m08.fact.036]. Privately, Dubourg handed the box to Tobias on purpose, meaning to use the younger, unknown-to-security boy as a distraction while he and the other teen slipped out [m08.fact.037]. Dubourg does not actually know what the store's video does and does not show [m08.fact.038]. {#b:c0aa93b3}

## The mother's perspective {#b:0444c7bc}

Dana Merrow was at her grocery-store shift when the store called; the call, and the drive to the police station, left her badly shaken and consumed with guilt that her work schedule leaves Tobias alone too much [m08.fact.039]. She had noticed weeks before the incident that Tobias had a newer video game in his room that she was sure he could not have afforded, and she never asked him where it came from — a fact she is ashamed of and scared to raise now [m08.fact.040]. After the incident, Dana overheard Tobias on the phone, sounding frightened, telling someone that he "didn't say anything," which makes her worry he is protecting the older boys and may be in danger [m08.fact.041]. Dana herself received a shoplifting citation as a teenager that was later dismissed; she has never told Tobias, and she is frightened by the echo of it [m08.fact.042]. Because she was not inside the store, Dana does not actually know what happened in the doorway [m08.fact.043]. {#b:dc466264}

## The school {#b:b7024e6a}

Tobias's school counselor at Larkmont High, Adaora Nwosu, regards him as a fundamentally solid student whose grades dipped in the last year, and considers him a strong candidate for diversion rather than formal adjudication [m08.fact.044]. Nwosu has known Tobias since he enrolled and is willing to speak to his character and to the supports the school can put around him [m08.fact.045]. {#b:600aaf92}
