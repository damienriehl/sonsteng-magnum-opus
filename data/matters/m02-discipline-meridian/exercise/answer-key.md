## 1. Model Case Analysis

### 1.1 The rules in play

This is a three-count Petition [m02.fact.031], and a strong student maps each count to the governing Meridian Rule of Professional Conduct (ABA-Model numbering; canon citations are welcome here because this file is instructor-only):

- **Rule 1.15 — Safekeeping of client property (Count One and Count Three).** The core of the case. Multiple distinct sub-duties are implicated, and the student should not collapse them:
  - *Segregation / no commingling.* Winterhalt deposited the entire $42,000 settlement draft into his **operating** account instead of his IOLTA trust account, mixing client money with firm money [m02.fact.007][m02.fact.008]. The IOLTA account existed on paper but was never used for these funds (Exhibit 3). This is a straight commingling violation and is essentially **conceded** in the Answer [m02.fact per Exhibit 2 ¶2].
  - *Keeping funds intact.* The client's $22,500 net share [m02.fact.009] had to remain intact from receipt until disbursement. It did not: the operating balance fell **below** $22,500 on at least two occasions — $19,410 on Apr 9 and $21,050 on Apr 22, 2024 (Exhibit 3) [m02.fact.011]. This is the fact that opens the door to the misappropriation framing.
  - *Prompt delivery and accounting.* Disbursement took ~four months (settled late Feb, paid June 18, 2024) [m02.fact.010], and Winterhalt **never provided a written settlement statement or accounting** at the time [m02.fact.048]; the Exhibit 4 statement was reconstructed after the fact. Both the delay and the missing accounting are independent Rule 1.15 breaches.
  - *Required books and records (Count Three).* He could not produce a contemporaneous reconciliation; the Feb 2024 software migration corrupted the trust ledger and destroyed the reconciliation history [m02.fact.025][m02.fact.026]. A recordkeeping violation does not require any missing money — it stands on its own.
- **Rule 1.4 (and 1.3) — Communication and diligence (Count Two).** Osei telephoned ~eleven times between March and June 2024 and got no return call [m02.fact.017]. That is a communication failure independent of any competence problem — the estate work was ultimately completed correctly with no loss [m02.fact.019]. The instructor notes correctly pin this to Rule 1.4 (and 1.3 diligence).
- **Rule 8.4 — Misconduct / dishonesty.** Not separately charged in the Petition, but it is the **shadow count**: the Director's characterization of the temporary fund use as "misappropriation" [m02.fact.032] is, in substance, an allegation that sounds in dishonesty/selfish motive. A strong student treats the mental-state fight as the real Rule 8.4 fight even though the Petition pleads it under Rule 1.15 safekeeping.
- **Rule 8.1(b) — Candor/cooperation in the disciplinary process.** Cuts **for** the respondent here: he cooperated, produced bank records, and admitted the commingling to the investigator without counsel present [m02.fact.029][m02.fact.050]. Note the flip side the Petition presses — he did **not** self-report; the matter surfaced only through client complaints [m02.fact.030]. Failure to self-report is not itself a charged violation but is argued as an aggravator.
- **Rule 3.3 / 4.2 — the student's OWN duties.** Two professional-responsibility duties run to the *student as respondent's counsel*, not to Winterhalt: candor to the tribunal (Rule 3.3) in how the brief characterizes the borrowed funds, and the no-contact rule (Rule 4.2) governing any approach to Marchetti, who is represented by Beckwith [m02.fact.027]. See §2.

### 1.2 The critical mental-state question (this drives the sanction)

The single most important issue — and the one that separates A from B work — is the **characterization of the shortfall**:

- **Negligent commingling + recordkeeping failure.** The account dipped below what was owed because Winterhalt lost track of what he was holding: his ledger was destroyed [m02.fact.025], his only bookkeeper had left [m02.fact.003], he was hospitalized [m02.fact.021], and he was in personal collapse [m02.fact.022][m02.fact.023]. On this framing the presumptive Meridian sanction is a reprimand or short suspension with probation — the outcome the defense wants [m02.fact.044].
- **Knowing / intentional misappropriation.** The concealed fact [m02.fact.012] is the pivot: in April 2024, short of cash, Winterhalt **used ~$3,100 of the funds in that account to cover office rent and Fenwick's pay**. That is affirmative use of money in an account he knew held un-disbursed client funds, for his own purposes. If a referee finds he *knew* he was reaching client money, the presumptive sanction in most disciplinary regimes is **suspension or disbarment** [m02.fact.045]. The Director's Petition already leans this way, expressly calling the temporary use "misappropriation" [m02.fact.032].

**Reasonable minds genuinely differ here, and the key should not pretend otherwise.** The strongest points on each side:

- *Toward knowing:* The $14,000 fee [m02.fact.009] was also sitting in that operating account, so at the Apr 9 low of $19,410 the balance had fallen **below even the client share alone** — the "cushion" of his own earned fee was gone, which a prosecutor argues he had to have noticed when he deliberately pulled $3,100 for rent/payroll. He *chose* to spend from that account when short [m02.fact.012].
- *Toward negligent:* He had no working ledger and had genuinely "lost track of what he was holding and for whom" [m02.fact.026]; he replaced the money within weeks [m02.fact.012][m02.fact.013], added $600 voluntary interest [m02.fact.013], paid the lien in full [m02.fact.014], and no client lost a dollar [m02.fact.015]. Restoration-plus-interest and the absence of any concealment scheme are classic markers of negligence, not conversion for gain.

A first-rate defense answer **confronts this head-on** rather than pretending fact 012 doesn't exist — because the student's own interview record will (if done well) contain it, and arguing "no intent, no harm" while sitting on an elicited fact 012 is both a losing litigation posture and a Rule 3.3 candor problem. The winning move is to concede the commingling and recordkeeping violations outright, characterize the April use as negligent misappropriation born of a destroyed ledger and personal collapse rather than knowing conversion, and argue proportionality from there.

### 1.3 Shortfall vs. bookkeeping error — read the arithmetic

The Exhibit 3 reconstruction shows a **real shortfall, not a mere bookkeeping artifact.** The bank balances actually fell below $22,500 twice (Apr 9: $19,410; Apr 22: $21,050) [m02.fact.011]. Students who wave this away as "just a records problem corrupted by the software failure" [m02.fact.025] miss the point: the software failure explains the **recordkeeping** count (Count Three) and supports the *negligence* framing, but it does not erase the **intact-funds** violation — the money physically was not there. The defense's honest position is: the shortfall happened; the fight is over mental state and consequence, not over whether it occurred.

### 1.4 The Marchetti settlement handling and the Beckwith demand

- The settlement math is clean and conceded: $42,000 gross, $14,000 fee (one-third), $5,500 medical lien, $22,500 net to client [m02.fact.006][m02.fact.009] (Exhibit 4). No dispute over the numbers themselves.
- The **Beckwith demand letter** (June 3, 2024) [m02.fact.027] (Exhibit 5) is important for two reasons: (a) it demanded disbursement, a written statement, and confirmation of the holding account within 14 days — and disbursement followed on June 18 — which the Director will frame as payment *only under pressure*, not voluntary remediation; and (b) it expressly directs that all further communication about Marchetti go through Beckwith, which is the **Rule 4.2 trigger** for the student's own conduct.

### 1.5 Strongest theory for each side

**For the Director (prosecution):** The most damaging package is Count One reframed as knowing misappropriation — client money used for the lawyer's rent and payroll [m02.fact.012] — aggravated by (i) a prior 2019 admonition [m02.fact.039], (ii) failure to self-report [m02.fact.030], and (iii) the four-month silence toward two clients showing a pattern, not a one-off. The Director argues the protective purpose of discipline requires a suspension to safeguard the public from a lawyer whose books collapsed and who spent client funds while it happened.

**For the respondent (defense/mitigation):** Concede the commingling, the delayed disbursement, the missing accounting, and the recordkeeping failure — everything in the Answer's admissions (Exhibit 2). Then build proportionality on: no client loss [m02.fact.015]; full restitution plus voluntary interest [m02.fact.013]; medical lien paid, no default [m02.fact.014]; a genuine health/personal crisis (cardiac event and stent [m02.fact.021], finalized divorce [m02.fact.022], depression and heavy drinking now in documented recovery since Oct 2024 [m02.fact.024]); loss of his only staff [m02.fact.003]; a destroyed ledger [m02.fact.025]; full cooperation and voluntary admission [m02.fact.029][m02.fact.050]; and prompt, thorough remediation — new bookkeeper [m02.fact.035], compliant IOLTA and monthly three-way reconciliation [m02.fact.036], trust-accounting CLE [m02.fact.037], and a reduced caseload [m02.fact.038]. The defense's proportionality target is **public reprimand + supervised probation** [m02.fact.044].

### 1.6 Likely sanction range (honest bottom line)

A defensible model answer lands on **public reprimand with a period of supervised probation** (with conditions: continued bookkeeper, monthly reconciliation, recovery-program compliance, practice-management supervision) as the argued-for outcome, while acknowledging that a **short suspension (e.g., 30–90 days), likely stayed on probation conditions,** is a realistic middle result a referee could reach — and that a finding of *knowing* misappropriation would push the presumptive sanction to suspension or disbarment [m02.fact.045]. The point to reward is not a "correct" number but a *proportionate, factor-driven* argument tied to the protective (not punitive) purpose of discipline, that honestly confronts the misappropriation framing instead of ignoring it. **Reasonable minds differ** on where in the reprimand-to-short-suspension band this lands; they differ far less on the two poles (pure recordkeeping = too light to ignore; knowing conversion = disbarment territory).

### 2.3 Interview strategy notes

- **Sequence gates deliberately.** Fact 023 (mail avoidance) needs a genuine wellbeing question and an acknowledged emotion by turn 4; fact 024 (drinking) needs an explicit **confidentiality/privilege reassurance** and a nonjudgmental response by turn 6; fact 012 (borrowed funds) is *concealed* — it comes only after sustained trust, never through pressure. The anti-sycophancy rule means flattery or insistence never unlocks these — only the emotional preconditions do.
- **Fact 012 is the whole ballgame.** A student who never earns it cannot honestly assess the misappropriation risk (§1.2) and will write a brief built on sand. A student who earns it and then must counsel Winterhalt about candor (disclosure vs. concealment) is doing exactly the assessed work (SK-LP-10, TSK-037; rubric c04.s02).
- **Fenwick's fact 049 cuts both ways.** Students should recognize that "he was warned twice" both explains the negligence *and* hands the Director a notice-based aggravator — good defense counsel elicits it, then frames it (he was hospitalized when warned [m02.fact.021][m02.fact.049]).
- **Osei only exists on paper.** Because there is no Osei persona, the Count Two facts must come from his written statement and from Winterhalt (fact 017); reward students who read the statement rather than assuming they can "interview" him.

## 3. Deliverable Model-Answer Outline

**Primary graded written deliverable:** the **Respondent's Pre-Hearing Brief** arguing for a public reprimand and supervised probation rather than a suspension (`exercise.json` activities 4; SK-LP-17 / TSK-032; ≤10 pages). The preliminary case-analysis memo (activity 2) shares the same analytic spine (§1); the outline below is the culminating brief and is mapped to every rubric criterion. A graded-A brief has this shape:

### 3.1 Graded-A brief structure

1. **Introduction / summary of position.** Concede the misconduct that must be conceded; frame the single contested issue as *characterization and proportionate sanction*, not guilt. State the ask up front: public reprimand + supervised probation. Signal that no client lost a dollar [m02.fact.015] without leading with it as an excuse.
2. **Statement of facts (persuasive but scrupulously accurate).** The catastrophic year, in order: divorce finalized Jan 2024 [m02.fact.022]; software failure destroys the ledger Feb 2024 [m02.fact.025]; cardiac event + stent March 2024 [m02.fact.021]; depression and (candidly stated) alcohol misuse now in documented recovery [m02.fact.024]; loss of his only staff [m02.fact.003]. Present the Marchetti timeline honestly, including the two sub-$22,500 dips [m02.fact.011] and the missing accounting [m02.fact.048]. **Do not omit the April use of ~$3,100** [m02.fact.012] — candor to the tribunal (Rule 3.3) and credibility both require confronting it.
3. **Argument I — The safekeeping violations (Counts One and Three), conceded and correctly characterized.** Admit commingling [m02.fact.008], delayed disbursement [m02.fact.010], absent accounting [m02.fact.048], and inadequate records [m02.fact.025][m02.fact.026]. Then draw the sanction-defining line: the April use was **negligent misappropriation** driven by a destroyed ledger and a lawyer who had lost track of what he held [m02.fact.026], **not knowing conversion** for personal gain — evidenced by prompt restoration [m02.fact.012], voluntary interest [m02.fact.013], full lien payment [m02.fact.014], and zero client loss [m02.fact.015]. Acknowledge the Director's contrary framing [m02.fact.032] and meet it, don't dodge it.
4. **Argument II — Diligence/communication (Count Two).** Admit the unreturned Osei calls [m02.fact.017]; contextualize (no missed deadline, estate completed correctly, no loss [m02.fact.018][m02.fact.019]) without minimizing the real disrespect the client felt.
5. **Argument III — Sanction and proportionality.** The protective (not punitive) purpose of discipline; weigh aggravating factors (2019 admonition [m02.fact.039] — noting it's unsettled whether it counts [m02.fact.040]; failure to self-report [m02.fact.030]) against the substantial mitigation (health/personal crisis; no loss; restitution + interest; cooperation and voluntary admission [m02.fact.029][m02.fact.050]; thorough remediation [m02.fact.035][m02.fact.036][m02.fact.037][m02.fact.038]; remorse and recovery [m02.fact.024]). Argue reprimand + probation with conditions; concede a short stayed suspension is within range if the panel weighs the priors and notice [m02.fact.049] more heavily.
6. **Proposed disposition / conditions.** Concrete probation terms: retained bookkeeper, monthly three-way reconciliation, recovery-program compliance, practice supervision, reduced caseload — turning remediation already done [m02.fact.035]–[m02.fact.038] into enforceable conditions.
7. **Conclusion.** Restate the proportionate ask.

## 4. Grading Guidance

### 4.1 A / B / C discriminators for THIS matter

1. **Does the student locate fact 012 on the commingling-vs-misappropriation line?** *A:* elicits fact 012 in the interview, then in the brief argues negligent (not knowing) misappropriation with record support and confronts the Director's framing. *B:* identifies the distinction abstractly but never grounds it in fact 012, or hedges. *C:* misses fact 012 entirely and argues "no harm, no foul" — a brief built on a fact the student's own interview should have surfaced.
2. **Does the analysis reconcile the arithmetic?** *A:* recognizes the Exhibit 3 balances actually fell below $22,500 [m02.fact.011] — a real shortfall, not just a records artifact — and separates the intact-funds violation from the recordkeeping count. *B:* notices the shortfall but blurs it into the software-failure narrative. *C:* treats the whole thing as a bookkeeping error erased by the ledger corruption.
3. **Rule 4.2 handling.** *A:* spots that Marchetti is represented [m02.fact.027] and routes through Beckwith. *B:* hesitates or asks about it late. *C:* contacts Marchetti directly — a negative-scoring ethics event.
4. **Proportionate, factor-driven sanction argument.** *A:* weighs aggravators (priors [m02.fact.039]; no self-report [m02.fact.030]; the twice-given warnings [m02.fact.049]) against mitigation and proposes concrete probation conditions, honestly conceding a short stayed suspension is in range. *B:* mostly one-sided mitigation. *C:* asks for a token outcome divorced from the record.

### 4.2 Common errors to watch for

- **Conflating negligent and knowing misappropriation** (the single most common and most consequential error). Grade down analysis that assumes "no client loss" answers the mental-state question — it does not.
- **Missing the trust-accounting arithmetic** — not seeing that the balance truly dipped below the client share, or double-counting the $14,000 fee as a permanent cushion when it too was exhausted at the Apr 9 low of $19,410.
- **Ignoring or burying fact 012** while arguing "no intent." Per the instructor notes: **do not award full analysis credit to a brief that argues 'no intent' when the student's own interview record shows fact 012 was elicited and ignored** — that is both a merits failure and a Rule 3.3 candor failure.
- **Treating restitution as a defense to the violation** rather than as sanction mitigation.
- **Over-claiming self-report credit** — he cooperated [m02.fact.029] but did **not** self-report [m02.fact.030]; those are different, and the difference matters to c03.
- **Forgetting Osei is paper-only** and inventing an "interview" of him.
- **Rule 4.2 barrel-through** on Marchetti.

### 4.3 Re-write-loop guidance

- **First loop — did they earn fact 012?** If the interview record lacks it, the highest-value coaching is on rapport/confidentiality sequencing (the gates for facts 023/024/012), not on brief polish. Send them back to the persona engine before the brief.
- **Second loop — characterization discipline.** If the brief has fact 012 but argues "no intent" flatly, coach the student to *confront and characterize* it (negligent misappropriation, with the restoration/interest/no-loss record) rather than omit it — and connect this explicitly to their Rule 3.3 candor duty.
- **Third loop — proportionality balance.** If the sanction argument is all mitigation, require the student to state the Director's best aggravating case (priors, no self-report, notice via Fenwick's warnings) and answer it; a brief that cannot survive the misappropriation question at oral argument (c06) is not yet an A.
- **Reasonable-minds calibration.** Do not penalize a student for landing on a short stayed suspension instead of a pure reprimand, or vice versa, *if* the argument is proportionate and factor-driven. The graded skill is the reasoning, not the number.
