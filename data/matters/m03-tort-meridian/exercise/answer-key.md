## 1. Model Case Analysis

This is a contested rear-end auto-negligence case set in Meridian District Court, Marrick County, headed to a jury [m03.fact.051]. It is deliberately built so that liability, the icy-conditions defense, and injury causation are all genuinely disputable. A first-rate student produces a *defensible theory that is candid about its own soft spots*, not a claim of certainty. The primary written deliverable is the preliminary case-and-strategic-analysis memorandum (see §3).

### 1.1 The negligence spine (duty / breach / causation / damages)

**Duty.** Uncontested. Every motorist owes other road users a duty of reasonable care, heightened — not lowered — by known adverse conditions. The freezing drizzle, the below-freezing temperatures, the visible glaze of ice, and the untreated pavement [m03.fact.007] were all *apparent* that morning; a reasonable driver adjusts speed and following distance to conditions he can see. The downhill grade on the approach [m03.fact.008] is part of the duty picture: a careful driver descending an icy grade toward a signaled intersection leaves extra room. Students should research the Meridian formulation and the pattern jury instruction, but the duty element is not where the case is won or lost.

**Breach — the heart of liability.** The strongest breach facts for the plaintiff:
- Ashcombe struck a car that had been *fully stopped for several seconds* at a red light, behind another stopped car [m03.fact.010], [m03.fact.011]. A stopped-and-waiting plaintiff is the paradigm faultless rear-end victim.
- The physical geometry corroborates her: the police diagram shows V1 stopped short of the crosswalk with V2 mid-slide behind it in the same lane [m03.exh.002 / m03.fact.026], and the officer's damage assessment is "consistent with a low-to-moderate-speed rear impact" [m03.fact.024].
- Meridian law (like most) treats following distance and control as the driver's responsibility; the general rule that a following driver must keep enough distance to stop for hazards ahead, *including* on ice he can see, is the plaintiff's central legal lever. The best framing: the ice did not appear from nowhere — it was the observable condition the whole intersection was coping with, and Quesada, in the very next lane, kept control by braking early [m03.fact.021]. If one driver could stop safely and the other could not, the difference is the second driver's speed/spacing, not the weather.
- The distraction thread reinforces breach if the student can develop it (see 1.2).

Honest counter (defense breach theory): Ashcombe says he was below the 30-mph limit at ~20–25 mph [m03.fact.012], [m03.fact.013], hit black ice, lost traction, and could not stop [m03.fact.013], [m03.fact.014]. He frames this as a *sudden emergency / unavoidable accident* — an act of the conditions, not of the driver. The absence of a citation [m03.fact.025] and the officer's note that there was no independent proof of speed [m03.fact.025] are the defense's best objective support. Students must research how Meridian treats the sudden-emergency doctrine; the winning plaintiff answer is that a hazard the driver could and did perceive (visible ice, a downgrade, stopped cars ahead) is *not* a sudden emergency — the emergency doctrine does not excuse a driver who created the predicament by driving too fast for known conditions. "Reasonable minds could differ" on whether a jury credits the ice story, and the memo should say so.

**Causation.** Two layers. *Collision causation* is strong: the impact indisputably came from Ashcombe's truck striking a stopped car. *Injury causation* is the real contest — the pre-existing-condition and treatment-gap fight in 1.3.

**Damages.** Built up from the exhibits (see 1.3): ~$11,600 documented medical charges [m03.fact.042 / m03.exh.004], ~$3,200 lost wages [m03.fact.048 / m03.exh.007], ~$4,820 property repair [m03.fact.033 / m03.exh.006], plus non-economic pain-and-suffering for a five-month treatment course [m03.fact.041] and documented residual limitations [m03.fact.043].

### 1.2 Comparative fault, the ice, and the distraction thread

**Comparative fault — research flag.** The Meridian canon supplies the statutes of limitation (auto negligence 3 yrs, Mer. Stat. § 210.05; property injury 6 yrs, § 210.07) but *does not* supply a comparative-fault percentage-bar rule. The student must research Meridian's comparative-fault regime (is it pure, modified-50%, modified-51%, or contributory?) — do not credit a student who simply assumes a number. Instructor note: treat any specific bar the student cites as researched-by-them; the key is that they *identify* comparative fault as a live issue and know the plaintiff carries essentially none of it.

**Whose fault is actually being compared?** A subtle but important point strong students catch: the plaintiff was stopped at a red light and did nothing wrong [m03.fact.010], [m03.fact.016] — there is no meaningful *plaintiff* comparative negligence to allocate. So the "comparative fault" contest here is not plaintiff-vs-defendant apportionment; it is really the binary question of whether Ashcombe breached at all or whether the ice was a superseding, no-fault cause (unavoidable accident). Reasonable minds can differ on *that*. A student who spends pages allocating a percentage of fault to Ms. Petimeyer has misread the record.

**The distraction / cell-phone thread.** This is the plaintiff's best shot at converting "unavoidable ice" into "distracted driver who couldn't react":
- Quesada, the neutral eyewitness, saw Ashcombe's head down, glancing toward his lap rather than the road, in the seconds before the slide [m03.fact.019 / m03.exh(witness-quesada)].
- Ashcombe's carrier records show an outgoing SMS at 7:42 a.m., within ~one minute of the 7:43 a.m. impact [m03.fact.028 / m03.exh.005], [m03.crash report 7:43].

But the student must be candid about the ceiling on this evidence, or lose credibility:
- Quesada expressly did **not** see a phone and cannot say what Ashcombe was looking at [m03.fact.020]. He is a careful, neutral witness who will not be pushed past what he saw — do not oversell him.
- The carrier record shows a message was *sent*; it does not show whether it was hand-typed or hands-free voice-dictated [m03.fact.030 / m03.exh.005]. Ashcombe claims hands-free dictation [m03.fact.029], and no independent record confirms or refutes him [m03.fact.030], [m03.fact.031].
- Petimeyer's own belief that he was distracted is an inference, not an observation — she was facing forward and saw nothing [m03.fact.016], [m03.fact.017]. A student who presents her as an eyewitness to distraction has misused the record.

The disciplined move: use the head-down observation + the one-minute-before text as *circumstantial* support for inattention, and pursue targeted discovery (phone forensics, the vehicle's infotainment/Bluetooth pairing logs, carrier metadata, dispatch records for the 7:38 and 7:51 calls [m03.exh.005]) to try to prove or disprove hands-free. "Whether Ashcombe was manually texting at the moment of the crash is genuinely disputed and unproven" [m03.fact.031] — the memo should say exactly that and explain what discovery could move it.

### 1.3 Damages build-up and the causation/IME fight

**Economic damages (documented):**
- Medical: ~$11,600 in billed charges — urgent care, physiatry evaluations, cervical/shoulder imaging, ~20 PT sessions, discharge eval [m03.exh.004 / m03.fact.042]. Note these are *charges*, not paid/adjusted amounts [m03.exh.004 footnote]; a careful student flags that the recoverable figure may turn on Meridian's collateral-source and reasonable-value rules (research item).
- Wage loss: ~$3,200 from ~three weeks of missed shifts plus a period of reduced hours [m03.exh.007 / m03.fact.048], [m03.fact.049].
- Property: ~$4,820 independent repair appraisal; not a total loss, repaired [m03.exh.006 / m03.fact.033]. Note the property claim carries a longer 6-year SOL (§ 210.07) than the injury claim (§ 210.05, 3 yrs) — usually academic here since both are timely, but a precise student notes it.

**Non-economic damages:** cervical strain and right-shoulder impingement [m03.fact.040], a five-month treatment course [m03.fact.041], and credible residual limitations that bite on her actual job — turning her head for the route and securing the wheelchair ramp [m03.fact.043], [m03.fact.049]. The human, work-specific character of the residual limitation is the plaintiff's most persuasive non-economic material.

**The IME (Kolstad) and the causation contest.** The defense's Dr. Kolstad opines the current symptoms are largely pre-existing/degenerative, not crash-caused: mild exam findings, degenerative imaging "consistent with age and repetitive lifting," modest vehicle damage, and the two-day symptom/treatment delay as atypical of significant acute injury — he concedes "at most a short-lived cervical strain" [ime-report-kolstad / m03.fact.046]. The treating physiatrist Dr. Ekwueme opines the crash *aggravated a previously quiescent* condition and produced new symptomatic soft-tissue injury, with the degenerative imaging asymptomatic before the crash [witness-ekwueme / m03.fact.047].

The plaintiff's causation answer is the **eggshell / aggravation ("take the plaintiff as you find her") principle** — a research item, but the doctrinal pivot. A defendant is liable for aggravating a latent/pre-existing condition even if a healthier plaintiff would have been hurt less. Strong students:
- Lean on the treating physician's longitudinal advantage (she directed five months of care) over the one-time defense examiner.
- Reframe the 2019 rotator-cuff tendinitis [m03.fact.044] as *resolved and asymptomatic* before the crash — the very predicate for an aggravation theory — rather than hiding it.
- Meet the two "bad facts" candidly: the two-day treatment gap [m03.fact.038], [m03.fact.039] (she declined transport, drove home, worked a partial shift because she "didn't think it was that bad," and symptoms characteristically set in overnight [m03.fact.036], [m03.fact.037]) and the modest vehicle damage [m03.fact.034], [m03.fact.035] (bumper-height differential [m03.exh.003 photo 4, m03.exh.006 note] and soft-tissue injuries do not track sheet-metal cost).

**Watch-for (instructor):** a student who *concedes* causation because of the prior condition, or who fails to name the aggravation principle, has surrendered the defense's best-and-only real damages argument for free. That is the single most common serious error in this matter.

### 1.4 Strongest theory each way; what the A memo concludes

**Strongest plaintiff theory:** A driver descending a visibly icy downgrade toward stopped cars at a red light, with his head down and a text leaving his phone a minute before impact, failed to keep the control and following distance the known conditions demanded, and rear-ended a motorist who was doing everything right — sitting stopped at the light. The ice was a *condition* every driver at that intersection had to manage; the neutral witness managed it; Ashcombe did not. On damages, the crash aggravated a quiet prior shoulder condition and caused a new neck/shoulder injury, documented by five months of treatment and real limits on her work.

**Strongest defense theory:** A careful driver going under the limit hit unforeseeable black ice and slid — a genuine sudden emergency the officer effectively recognized by declining to cite anyone [m03.fact.025]. The damages are inflated: trivial vehicle damage, a two-day delay before any complaint, degenerative imaging, and a documented prior shoulder problem all point to a minor strain superimposed on age and pre-existing wear, worth a fraction of the demand.

**What the A memo concludes:** liability is *probable but not certain* — the plaintiff should win the breach fight because the emergency doctrine does not cover a self-created, foreseeable hazard, but a jury could credit the ice, so the case carries real trial risk. Causation is genuinely contested and turns on the aggravation principle and a treater-vs-examiner credibility battle. Damages are solid on economics (~$19.6K hard: $11.6K medical + $3.2K wage + $4.8K property) with meaningful non-economic value, but exposed on the modest-impact and delay narratives. The $12,000 offer [m03.fact.052] is below even the documented hard damages and should be rejected, with the client counseled on the realistic range and the downside (see §3, criterion c05). The memo should *not* overclaim certainty on distraction or on the full non-economic ask.

### 2.3 Douglas Ashcombe — adverse driver, disposition: **guarded**, **REPRESENTED (Rule 4.2 checkpoint)**

**Rule 4.2 teaching point (mandatory).** Ashcombe is an adverse party **represented by counsel** through his liability insurer [m03.fact.054 / persona `represented_by_counsel: true`, `rule_4_2.applies: true`]. Under Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2 (mirrors the Model Rule) *(instructor-verified-at-publish)*, plaintiff's counsel may **not** communicate with him about the subject of the representation without his lawyer's consent. Any student who tries to interview Ashcombe directly should hit the in-app checkpoint — the correct move is to route contact through his counsel or use **formal discovery** (interrogatories, requests for production, and, above all, his **deposition** with counsel present). The persona is configured to surface this as a teaching moment, not a silent block. Grading: a student who barrels ahead and "interviews" him directly takes a serious professional-responsibility hit (c06; per interview-pedagogy, ethics is scored on a signed −2…+2 scale and a 4.2 violation can go negative). The facts below are therefore what a student would properly develop **through discovery/deposition**, not through a direct interview — included so the instructor can see what that discovery should target.

- **c05 — Client counseling & settlement judgment (35).** **Earn:** candidly advises on strengths/weaknesses and on the $12,000 offer against a realistic range (note it undershoots even the ~$19.6K documented hard damages), while respecting that the decision is the client's [m03.fact.052]; folds in the candor obligation if the concealed side-work surfaced [m03.fact.050]. **Lose:** guaranteeing an outcome; deciding *for* her; ignoring downside/trial risk.
- **c06 — Professional responsibility (25).** **Earn:** identifies Ashcombe's represented status and treats the Rule 4.2 contact question as a decision point — routes through counsel or formal discovery [m03.fact.054]; handles the contingency engagement properly (writing, reasonableness). **Lose:** attempting to contact Ashcombe directly (signed-scale ethics hit); mishandling the fee arrangement.

## 4. Grading Guidance

### 4.1 A / B / C discriminators for THIS matter

1. **Handling of the aggravation/eggshell causation fight.** *A:* names the "take the plaintiff as you find her" principle, reframes the 2019 condition [m03.fact.044] as resolved-and-asymptomatic (the predicate for aggravation), and leverages the treater's five months over Kolstad's single exam [m03.fact.047], [m03.fact.046]. *B:* identifies the causation dispute but is thin on the doctrine or the treater/examiner contrast. *C:* concedes causation because of the prior injury — surrendering the defense's only real damages argument.
2. **Calibration of the distraction evidence.** *A:* uses head-down + one-minute text as circumstantial inattention and plans discovery to resolve hand-typed vs. hands-free [m03.fact.019], [m03.fact.028]–[m03.fact.031], while conceding neither Quesada nor the carrier record proves texting [m03.fact.020], [m03.fact.030]. *B:* leans on the phone but overstates certainty. *C:* asserts Ashcombe was texting as fact, or treats the client as an eyewitness to distraction [m03.fact.016].
3. **Meeting the sudden-emergency / no-citation defense.** *A:* argues a visible, foreseeable hazard on a downgrade toward stopped cars is not a sudden emergency, and that no-citation reflected lack of speed proof, not exoneration [m03.fact.025], using Quesada's controlled stop as proof a careful driver could stop [m03.fact.021]. *B:* addresses it generically. *C:* ignores it or accepts the no-citation as dispositive.
4. **Client interview relational axis + Rule 4.2.** *A:* earns the rapport-gated facts from a distressed client without pressure and correctly refuses direct contact with the represented Ashcombe [m03.fact.054]. *C:* extracts sensitive facts by pressure (anti-sycophancy fail) and/or tries to interview Ashcombe directly (signed-scale ethics penalty).

### 4.2 Common errors to penalize

- **Conceding causation** to the prior shoulder condition instead of running aggravation (the #1 serious error).
- **Over-claiming the phone evidence** as proof of texting; misusing Petimeyer or Quesada as eyewitnesses to distraction beyond what they saw [m03.fact.016], [m03.fact.020].
- **Over-claiming damages** the record won't support — treating billed charges as automatically recoverable, or inflating non-economic value past what a modest-impact, five-month-recovery soft-tissue case will bear [m03.fact.035], [m03.fact.041].
- **Assuming a comparative-fault number** instead of flagging it as a research item, or wrongly allocating fault to a plaintiff who was stopped at a red light [m03.fact.010].
- **Rule 4.2 breach** — contacting Ashcombe directly rather than through counsel/discovery [m03.fact.054].
- **Missing the two-day gap and modest-impact defenses** entirely, then having no answer at trial [m03.fact.035], [m03.fact.039].
- **Guaranteeing a result** in client counseling or deciding the settlement for her [m03.fact.052].

### 4.3 Re-write-loop guidance

If the work misses the floor, route it back with a *targeted* prompt, not a global "redo":
- Missed causation doctrine → "Research and apply Meridian's rule for aggravation of a pre-existing condition; rebuild §IV around it and the treater-vs-IME contrast."
- Over-claimed distraction → "Rewrite the phone paragraph to state exactly what the record proves and what discovery is needed; separate inference from observation."
- Weak interview → "Re-run the client interview; open-ended funnel-top, earn the sensitive facts through rapport (no pressure), and surface the non-legal concerns." Reward genuine improvement in the relational axis, not just added facts.
- Rule 4.2 slip → non-negotiable teaching moment; require the student to articulate the correct route (counsel/discovery) before credit is restored.

*Grade to defensible reasoning, not to a single "right answer." The matter is built so that a candid B+/A- student who honestly weighs the ice, the gap, and the IME can outscore a confident student who overclaims — that calibration is the point of the capstone.*
