# Instructor Notes — Petimeyer v. Ashcombe (m03) {#b:e8d4178c}

*Teaching notes for the instructor only. NOT part of the student packet. Citations are to the Meridian canon and are marked instructor-verified-at-publish. No answer key or intentional-error layer is included here.* {#b:4cf97377}

## What this matter teaches {#b:b9f4a552}

This is the tort/personal-injury capstone: a contested rear-end auto-negligence case that runs the full civil-litigation arc (intake and interviewing, fact investigation, pleadings, discovery, medical-expert handling, settlement counseling, and jury trial). It is deliberately built so that liability, the weather defense, and injury causation are all genuinely disputable — students should not find a "right answer" so much as a defensible theory they can build and stress-test. {#b:ec5914f5}

## Governing Meridian law (instructor-verified) {#b:9f766ebd}

- **Limitations.** Personal injury / general negligence, including auto negligence, is a three-year period — Mer. Stat. § 210.05. The February 7, 2025 collision is comfortably within the period; a filing in 2025 is timely. Good spot to check whether students calendar the deadline (TSK-055 habits) even though it is not close. {#b:063d5365}
- **Forum.** Meridian District Court, Marrick County (seat: Elleston). Assign Hon. Priya Nandakumar (District Court, Marrick County, civil) from the judge pool for any oral-argument or trial event. {#b:b21c0db9}
- **Negligence framework.** Standard duty/breach/causation/damages negligence; students research the Meridian formulation and the pattern jury instructions. The "sudden emergency" / adverse-conditions argument and comparative-fault allocation are the doctrinal pivot points. {#b:4f9a0cc5}
- **Eggshell/aggravation.** The pre-existing 2019 shoulder condition puts the "take the plaintiff as you find her" aggravation principle squarely in play; watch for students who concede too much on causation. {#b:f3654316}
- **Professional conduct.** Rule 4.2 (communication with a represented person) — the defendant driver is represented through his insurer's counsel; contacting him directly is off-limits. Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2 mirrors the Model Rule. Contingency-fee handling implicates Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 1.5 (reasonableness, writing). {#b:089373ee}

## Interview / persona coaching points {#b:c97707d2}

- **Lorraine Petimeyer (client, distressed).** Three rapport-gated disclosures reward genuine rapport work: the prior 2019 shoulder condition (needs confidentiality reassurance + a non-judgmental response), the anxiety about the two-day treatment gap (needs acknowledged emotion), and the reduced work hours she is proud to hide (needs a wellbeing question + sustained listening). One concealed fact — continued cash weekend work — should only surface under strong preconditions and is a candid-counseling teaching moment. Anti-sycophancy is in force: pressure or flattery must not unlock these. {#b:3a7b8338}
- **Rafael Quesada (neutral eyewitness).** Teaches the limits of eyewitness testimony — he saw the head down but no phone, and will not be pushed past what he saw. {#b:caa2bc06}
- **Douglas Ashcombe (represented adverse party).** The Rule 4.2 checkpoint. Students who try to interview him directly should hit the teaching moment; the correct move is to route through counsel or use formal discovery. {#b:504fa3c8}

## Suggested assessment emphasis {#b:a53cc500}

Weight the client-interview relational axis and the causation/damages analysis heavily; these are where students most often go shallow. See rubric.json for the point allocation (declared total 325). {#b:5ba30b66}

## Teaching arc & timing {#b:94ffe5ab}

A workable sequence across roughly four class blocks plus asynchronous drafting (adjust to your calendar): {#b:73a3786d}

- **Block 1 — Intake, interviewing, and the relational axis (~2 hrs).** Frame the two-axis debrief (fact coverage + standardized-client relational rating; see docs/research/interview-pedagogy.md). Students run the simulated Petimeyer interview through the persona engine, then the Quesada interview. Debrief live on whether they *earned* the three rapport-gated facts (2019 shoulder, gap anxiety, reduced hours) and, rarely, the concealed side-work — and whether anyone tried to pressure the facts loose (anti-sycophancy fail). Surface the Rule 4.2 moment the first time a student tries to "interview" Ashcombe. {#b:c0380667}
- **Block 2 — Case theory & the conditions defense (~1.5 hrs).** Workshop the negligence spine and the sudden-emergency / no-citation rebuttal. Use the police diagram and Quesada's own controlled stop as the anchor for "a careful driver could stop on this ice." {#b:fce7906c}
- **Block 3 — Causation, the IME, and damages (~1.5 hrs).** Center the aggravation / "take the plaintiff as you find her" principle and the treater-vs-examiner credibility contest (Ekwueme vs. Kolstad). Build the damages spreadsheet from the exhibits; stress-test the modest-impact and two-day-gap defenses. {#b:29a3397c}
- **Block 4 — Pleadings, discovery, counseling, and trial prep (~2 hrs).** Draft-review the complaint and one-page discovery plan; run the settlement-counseling role-play against the $12,000 offer; deliver short openings and one direct + one cross. Trial garb required for the oral events. {#b:1645b84d}
- **Async:** the case-and-strategic-analysis memorandum, the draft complaint, and the discovery plan are drafted between blocks; the answer-key mapping (exercise/answer-key.md) gives the criterion-by-criterion target. {#b:6e93afad}

**Pacing note.** The two failure modes that eat time are (a) students who never earn the prior-shoulder fact and then have no causation theory, and (b) students who overclaim the phone evidence. Budget debrief time to catch both early rather than at the memo stage. {#b:e8676e60}

## Discussion questions {#b:290f8e79}

1. Ms. Petimeyer was stopped at a red light and did nothing wrong — so what, exactly, is the "comparative fault" question in this case really comparing? (Draw out that the contest is breach-vs-unavoidable-accident between the drivers, not plaintiff apportionment.) {#b:75d17d33}
2. The eyewitness saw the driver's head down but no phone, and the carrier record shows a text but not whether it was hand-typed or dictated. How far can that evidence carry the plaintiff, and what discovery would you serve to resolve it? {#b:f83e725f}
3. Officer Harmon issued no citation and cited the icy conditions. Is that a problem for the plaintiff, evidence for the defense, or neither — and how do you handle it at trial? {#b:cf13e5b0}
4. The defense will argue the two-day treatment delay and the modest vehicle damage prove the injury is minor. What is the honest, persuasive answer that does not overclaim? {#b:b49d25b7}
5. How do you counsel a distressed client who was "just sitting at a red light" that a $12,000 offer may be rational to reject but that trial carries real risk — without deciding for her? {#b:bb57f1a0}
6. A student wants to call Douglas Ashcombe to "just get his side." Walk through why Rule 4.2 forecloses that and what the proper route is. What would change if he were unrepresented? {#b:a8efc1ab}
