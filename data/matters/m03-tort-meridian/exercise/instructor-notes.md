# Instructor Notes — Petimeyer v. Ashcombe (m03)

*Teaching notes for the instructor only. NOT part of the student packet. Citations are to the Meridian canon and are marked instructor-verified-at-publish. No answer key or intentional-error layer is included here.*

## What this matter teaches

This is the tort/personal-injury capstone: a contested rear-end auto-negligence case that runs the full civil-litigation arc (intake and interviewing, fact investigation, pleadings, discovery, medical-expert handling, settlement counseling, and jury trial). It is deliberately built so that liability, the weather defense, and injury causation are all genuinely disputable — students should not find a "right answer" so much as a defensible theory they can build and stress-test.

## Governing Meridian law (instructor-verified)

- **Limitations.** Personal injury / general negligence, including auto negligence, is a three-year period — Mer. Stat. § 210.05. The February 7, 2025 collision is comfortably within the period; a filing in 2025 is timely. Good spot to check whether students calendar the deadline (TSK-055 habits) even though it is not close.
- **Forum.** Meridian District Court, Marrick County (seat: Elleston). Assign Hon. Priya Nandakumar (District Court, Marrick County, civil) from the judge pool for any oral-argument or trial event.
- **Negligence framework.** Standard duty/breach/causation/damages negligence; students research the Meridian formulation and the pattern jury instructions. The "sudden emergency" / adverse-conditions argument and comparative-fault allocation are the doctrinal pivot points.
- **Eggshell/aggravation.** The pre-existing 2019 shoulder condition puts the "take the plaintiff as you find her" aggravation principle squarely in play; watch for students who concede too much on causation.
- **Professional conduct.** Rule 4.2 (communication with a represented person) — the defendant driver is represented through his insurer's counsel; contacting him directly is off-limits. Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2 mirrors the Model Rule. Contingency-fee handling implicates Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 1.5 (reasonableness, writing).

## Interview / persona coaching points

- **Lorraine Petimeyer (client, distressed).** Three rapport-gated disclosures reward genuine rapport work: the prior 2019 shoulder condition (needs confidentiality reassurance + a non-judgmental response), the anxiety about the two-day treatment gap (needs acknowledged emotion), and the reduced work hours she is proud to hide (needs a wellbeing question + sustained listening). One concealed fact — continued cash weekend work — should only surface under strong preconditions and is a candid-counseling teaching moment. Anti-sycophancy is in force: pressure or flattery must not unlock these.
- **Rafael Quesada (neutral eyewitness).** Teaches the limits of eyewitness testimony — he saw the head down but no phone, and will not be pushed past what he saw.
- **Douglas Ashcombe (represented adverse party).** The Rule 4.2 checkpoint. Students who try to interview him directly should hit the teaching moment; the correct move is to route through counsel or use formal discovery.
