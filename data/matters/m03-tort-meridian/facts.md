# Petimeyer v. Ashcombe — Ground-Truth Facts

*Master narrative for matter m03. This is the authoritative record from which the case file, personas, exercise, and rubric are drawn. Every material fact carries a sequential anchor. Student-facing packets remain facts-only; no legal citations appear here.*

## The parties

Lorraine Petimeyer is a fifty-two-year-old part-time school-bus driver who has lived in Elleston, in Marrick County, Meridian, for most of her adult life [m03.fact.001]. She drives a morning and an afternoon route for the Elleston area schools and picks up cleaning and light home-care work on weekends for extra income [m03.fact.002]. She is widowed, lives alone, and helps look after two grandchildren several afternoons a week [m03.fact.003].

Douglas Ashcombe is a forty-six-year-old heating-and-cooling service technician who lives one town over and drives a full-size pickup truck for both work and personal use [m03.fact.004]. On the morning of the collision he was driving to a service call and running a few minutes behind schedule [m03.fact.005].

## The collision

The collision happened on the morning of Friday, February 7, 2025, at the intersection of Elleston Road and Birchmont Avenue in Elleston [m03.fact.006]. A freezing drizzle had fallen overnight and the temperature had stayed below freezing, leaving a glaze of ice on the pavement in patches; the roads had not yet been sanded on that stretch [m03.fact.007]. Elleston Road slopes gently downhill on the approach to the Birchmont Avenue traffic signal [m03.fact.008].

Petimeyer was driving her four-door sedan, alone, on her way to the bus depot [m03.fact.009]. She came to a complete stop in the right-hand lane at the red light at Birchmont Avenue, behind one other stopped car [m03.fact.010]. She had been fully stopped for several seconds when Ashcombe's pickup, coming down the hill behind her in the same lane, struck the rear of her sedan [m03.fact.011].

The posted speed limit on that stretch of Elleston Road is thirty miles per hour [m03.fact.012]. Ashcombe says he was traveling below the limit, at roughly twenty to twenty-five miles per hour, when his truck hit a patch of black ice, lost traction, and slid into Petimeyer's car with no time to stop [m03.fact.013]. He maintains he was watching the road and that the ice, not any inattention, caused the crash [m03.fact.014].

Petimeyer says Ashcombe was following too closely for the icy conditions and was not paying attention when he came down the hill [m03.fact.015]. She did not actually see what Ashcombe was doing before the impact, because she was facing forward and stopped [m03.fact.016]. Her belief that he was distracted is an inference she drew afterward, not something she personally observed [m03.fact.017].

## The independent witness

Rafael Quesada was stopped in the left-hand lane at the same red light, one car length back, in his own vehicle [m03.fact.018]. Through his passenger-side window he saw Ashcombe's pickup approaching in the next lane and noticed that Ashcombe appeared to be glancing down toward his lap rather than at the road in the seconds before the truck began to slide [m03.fact.019]. Quesada did not see a phone in Ashcombe's hands and cannot say for certain what Ashcombe was looking at [m03.fact.020]. Quesada also confirms that the pavement was visibly icy and that his own car had slid slightly when he braked for the light [m03.fact.021]. Quesada stayed at the scene, gave his name to the responding officer, and later provided a written statement [m03.fact.022].

## The police response and the citation question

Officer Denise Harmon of the Elleston Police Department responded to the scene about fifteen minutes after the crash [m03.fact.023]. Her report records both drivers' accounts, notes that the roadway was coated with a sheet of ice, and describes the damage as consistent with a low-to-moderate-speed rear impact [m03.fact.024]. Harmon did not issue a citation to either driver, noting the hazardous road conditions and the absence of any independent proof of speed [m03.fact.025]. The report includes a hand-drawn diagram showing the two lanes, the signal, the downhill grade, and the resting positions of the vehicles [m03.fact.026]. The absence of a citation is a fact the plaintiff must work around, because the defense treats it as support for the icy-conditions account [m03.fact.027].

## The phone question

Ashcombe's cellular records show that a text message was sent from his phone at 7:42 a.m., within roughly a minute of the estimated time of impact [m03.fact.028]. Ashcombe says the message was composed by hands-free voice dictation through his truck's system and that he never took his eyes off the road or handled the phone [m03.fact.029]. There is no independent record confirming whether the message was typed by hand or dictated [m03.fact.030]. Whether Ashcombe was manually texting at the moment of the crash is therefore genuinely disputed and unproven [m03.fact.031].

## The damage

The rear of Petimeyer's sedan was crushed inward at the trunk and bumper; the airbags did not deploy [m03.fact.032]. An independent appraisal put the repair cost at roughly four thousand eight hundred dollars, and the car was repaired rather than totaled [m03.fact.033]. Ashcombe's pickup sustained only minor front-bumper damage [m03.fact.034]. The relatively modest vehicle damage is a fact the defense uses to argue that the forces involved were too small to cause a lasting injury [m03.fact.035].

## The injuries and treatment

Petimeyer felt shaken but declined ambulance transport at the scene, drove herself home, and worked a partial shift that afternoon [m03.fact.036]. By the next morning her neck and right shoulder were stiff and painful, and the pain worsened over the weekend [m03.fact.037]. She first sought care two days after the crash, on Sunday, February 9, 2025, at an urgent-care clinic, and was referred onward [m03.fact.038]. The two-day gap before treatment is another fact the defense emphasizes [m03.fact.039].

Dr. Ifeoma Ekwueme, a physiatrist, examined Petimeyer and diagnosed a cervical strain and a right-shoulder impingement, and prescribed a course of physical therapy [m03.fact.040]. Petimeyer completed roughly twenty physical-therapy sessions over about five months, from late February through July 2025 [m03.fact.041]. Her documented medical bills for the examinations, imaging, and therapy total approximately eleven thousand six hundred dollars [m03.fact.042]. She improved substantially but reports residual neck stiffness and shoulder pain that flare when she turns her head sharply or lifts overhead [m03.fact.043].

## The prior-condition dispute

Petimeyer had a documented right-shoulder problem years before the crash: she was treated for rotator-cuff tendinitis in 2019 after years of repetitive lifting, and it had largely resolved with therapy at the time [m03.fact.044]. She is embarrassed about the old injury and worried that mentioning it will let the other side blame her pain on something other than the crash, so she is reluctant to bring it up [m03.fact.045]. Dr. Alan Bexley, the physician retained by the defense to perform an independent medical examination, opines that Petimeyer's current shoulder symptoms are largely degenerative and pre-existing rather than caused by the collision [m03.fact.046]. Dr. Ekwueme's view is that the crash aggravated a previously quiet condition and produced new, symptomatic injury [m03.fact.047].

## Work impact and lost income

Petimeyer missed about three weeks of bus-route shifts in the weeks after the crash and lost roughly three thousand two hundred dollars in wages [m03.fact.048]. She struggled with the parts of her route that require turning her head fully and helping secure a wheelchair ramp, and she reduced her hours for a period [m03.fact.049]. She also quietly continued some of her weekend cleaning work during her recovery, more than she has told anyone, because she needed the cash — a fact she has kept to herself and worries could be used to say she was not really hurt [m03.fact.050].

## Posture and settlement

Petimeyer retained the firm on a contingency basis and a complaint was filed on her behalf in Meridian District Court, Marrick County; the case is set for a jury trial [m03.fact.051]. The defense, through the driver's liability insurer's counsel, has offered twelve thousand dollars to settle, which Petimeyer considers far too low given her bills and lost wages [m03.fact.052]. Liability, the icy-conditions defense, and the extent and cause of Petimeyer's injuries all remain genuinely contested as the matter heads toward trial [m03.fact.053]. Douglas Ashcombe is represented by counsel retained through his insurer, which governs whether and how he may be contacted directly [m03.fact.054].
