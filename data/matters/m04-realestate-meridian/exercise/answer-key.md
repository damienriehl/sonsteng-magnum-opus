## 1. Model Case Analysis {#b:06b8acfe}

### Issue 1 — Is the 1978 instrument an easement appurtenant or a revocable license? {#b:66480539}

This is the load-bearing question and it is **genuinely arguable — reasonable minds differ** [m04.fact.006][m04.fact.007]. A first-rate student argues both sides and then refuses to bet the deal on the outcome. {#b:b1253519}

**Facts cutting toward an easement appurtenant (runs with the land, would pass to Meskin):** {#b:1e118883}
- It is a **written, recorded, notarized grant** — recorded with the Sable County Recorder in 1978, with a notarial acknowledgment on the instrument [m04.fact.006][exhibit-002]. Recording gives **constructive notice** to successors under Meridian's race-notice recording act (**Mer. Stat. § 507.34**); Delahaye, who took 4380 in 2016, took with record notice of it [m04.fact.010]. Acknowledgment before a notary (**Mer. Stat. ch. 358**) made it recordable in the first place. {#b:3b75da4c}
- It grants **"a right of way for ingress and egress ... for the purpose of reaching the neighboring parcel to the south"** [exhibit-002]. Tying the right to *reaching a specific parcel* is the hallmark of an **appurtenant** access easement — it benefits an identifiable dominant estate (4400), not merely a person. Access easements are presumptively appurtenant, not in gross. {#b:aad089c7}
- It recites **consideration** ("One Dollar and other good and valuable consideration") [exhibit-002] — a bargained grant, which cuts against a gratuitous, freely revocable license. {#b:0e10e2c6}
- **Forty-plus years of open, continuous use** [m04.fact.005][exhibit-008] corroborate a permanent right rather than a permission that was ever pulled. {#b:22141ac6}

**Facts cutting toward a revocable license / weakness in the buyer's position:** {#b:d5b0f3d1}
- The grant is made **"in consideration of the neighborly relations between the parties"** [exhibit-002] — personal, favor-between-friends framing, echoed by Delahaye's account that his father "only ever gave Harold permission" [m04.fact.035][witness-delahaye]. {#b:3476981c}
- The instrument **names the grantees** — "Harold and Ruth Renard ... and their household" — and contains **no "heirs and assigns" or "successors" language** [m04.fact.008][exhibit-002]. (Under modern law the absence of "heirs and assigns" is not by itself fatal — a conveyance is presumed to pass the grantor's whole interest unless limited — but combined with the personal recitals it gives Delahaye a real argument.) {#b:aa885712}
- It is **silent on permanence, silent on revocability, and silent on whether it is appurtenant or personal** [exhibit-002 recorder's note]. {#b:3abfdd4f}
- The grantor's successor now **disputes permanence** and points to his own upkeep as evidence of control [m04.fact.010][m04.fact.035][m04.fact.036]. {#b:20682fb9}

**Honest resolution.** The better-reasoned reading, on balance, is an **easement appurtenant** — a recorded, notarized grant, for consideration, of ingress/egress to reach an identified parcel, ripened by four decades of open use. But the "neighborly relations" recital, named grantees, and absence of successor language leave enough doubt that **the buyer must not stake the purchase on winning that construction.** The correct move is to **cure**, not to litigate the 1978 paper. {#b:e6300f59}

### Issue 2 — Indefinite location and the mislocated lane {#b:93edfa48}

Even a valid easement is troubled: the instrument has **no metes-and-bounds description, no width, no monument — only "the existing cartway"** [m04.fact.011][exhibit-002], and surveyor Brummond found the **traveled lane sits ~15 feet off** the record location, *toward* the Delahaye cabin [m04.fact.012][m04.fact.013][exhibit-003][witness-brummond]. Two doctrines pull in opposite directions: where a right of way is granted without a fixed location, the law can **fix location by the parties' practical construction / long use of the traveled way** (which 40+ years of use might supply), *or* the indefiniteness can leave the right unenforceably vague. The surveyor's own recommendation — and the right answer — is a **new recorded easement fixing centerline and width** [exhibit-003 note][m04.fact.041]. That is exactly what Delahaye has offered to sell for $25,000 [m04.fact.037]. {#b:8409dde3}

### Issue 3 — Race-notice, constructive notice, and the title commitment {#b:817e14ff}

Meridian is a **race-notice** jurisdiction (**§ 507.34**); the recorded 1978 instrument charges successors with constructive notice, which helps the *validity* argument but does nothing to cure *indefiniteness*. The title commitment lists the 1978 instrument as a **Schedule B, Part II exception (item 3)** and expressly **"will not insure legally sufficient permanent access ... absent a corrective recorded instrument satisfactory to the Company"** [m04.fact.040][exhibit-004]. Item 2 separately excepts the Verdant Shore Road rights, *subject to the pending vacation* [exhibit-004]. **Practical upshot:** whatever the easement-vs-license merits, the buyer **cannot get an access-insured policy without a recorded cure.** This is the buyer's strongest, most *objective* piece of negotiating leverage — it comes from the title company, not from the client's private urgency. {#b:8471d545}

### Issue 4 — The Verdant Shore Road vacation {#b:6a99fc36}

The 1971 plat shows a platted-but-never-built shoreline road, **Verdant Shore Road**, along the **rear (lake side)** of 4400 [m04.fact.014][exhibit-003]. The City proposes to vacate it to shed maintenance liability — not to help or hurt any owner [m04.fact.015][m04.fact.018][witness-vestergaard], with a public hearing set for April 2026 [m04.fact.017]. On vacation, the ground **reverts to abutting owners, typically to the centerline** [m04.fact.016][witness-vestergaard], so 4400 could pick up a lake-side strip. **But** the needed access is from the **county road (north) side**, and the vacated strip is on the **lake (rear) side**; whether it yields usable drivable access "depends on topography, the county's shoreland rules, and whether it connects to anything drivable" — Vestergaard's own caution is "it might help, it might do nothing" [m04.fact.016][witness-vestergaard]. **Treat the vacation as a contingency/backstop, never as the primary cure.** {#b:ebc357bf}

### Issue 5 — Marketable title, contingencies, and buyer's options {#b:d929a93b}

The draft PA requires **marketable title by warranty deed** [exhibit-001 ¶5]; unlocated, uninsurable access is a reasonable doubt on the practical title and a legitimate **title objection**. Buyer's realistic options [m04.fact.041]: {#b:18c6994f}
1. **Recorded-easement cure from Delahaye** (best) — pay ~$25k, fix centerline/width via Brummond, make it a **condition of closing** [m04.fact.037][m04.fact.041][m04.fact.012]. {#b:c2e836b8}
2. **Road-vacation contingency** — structure as a secondary backstop only [m04.fact.041][m04.fact.017]. {#b:c49b9599}
3. **Price abatement** to reflect the access risk (and the undisclosed $60k septic and density uncertainty) [m04.fact.041][m04.fact.028][m04.fact.023]. {#b:50ebf21c}
4. **Walk** — the BATNA, weak given the buyer's (confidential) LOI and financing deadline [m04.fact.024][m04.fact.025]. {#b:d336c614}

### Strongest theory — each side {#b:ce5ab9bd}

- **For the buyer (client):** Don't litigate the 1978 paper — *cure* it. Condition closing on a **new recorded easement from Delahaye fixing location and permanence**, backstopped by an access contingency with a **firm outer date comfortably before August 15** [m04.fact.025]; price in the **high-$370s–low-$380s** reflecting the ~$25k easement cost, the $60k septic surprise, and the shoreland-density risk [m04.fact.023][m04.fact.028]; add a **zoning/density contingency** [exhibit-001 ¶6]; keep the vacation as a secondary backstop; lead all leverage with the **objective title-insurance refusal**, never the client's urgency [m04.fact.040]. {#b:7658b24e}
- **For the seller (Renard):** The recorded, for-consideration grant of access *to the parcel*, ripened by 40+ years of open use, is a valid **easement appurtenant** that passed to Ruth and will pass to the buyer; long use fixes location by practical construction; the buyer is manufacturing risk to chisel price. Seller won't *warrant* access but will **cooperate with a Delahaye cure** and wants a defined outer date [exhibit-008]; her BATNA is the **$348k as-is backup offer** [m04.fact.032]; she is motivated to close this year [m04.fact.030]. {#b:9821a549}

### What a first-rate deliverable concludes {#b:be88077c}

The graded deliverables are an SSNP, purchase-agreement language, and a client memo (plus the interview record). A first-rate student **concludes**: pursue the **Delahaye recorded-easement cure as the condition of closing** — the only path the title company will insure [m04.fact.040] — via an **access contingency with a firm outer date before the (undisclosed) Aug 15 financing cliff**; **price in the high-$370s–low-$380s** to absorb the ~$25k easement + $60k septic + density risk while staying well under the $395k ceiling [m04.fact.026][m04.fact.028][m04.fact.023]; add a **shoreland-density contingency**; hold the **road vacation as a secondary backstop only**; and **protect the four confidential facts** at the table [m04.fact.024][m04.fact.025][m04.fact.026][m04.fact.028], leading leverage with the title company's objective refusal. The memo should **honestly tell the client that reasonable minds differ** on easement-vs-license — which is precisely why curing beats betting. {#b:cf502a03}

## 4. Grading Guidance {#b:50abb3ac}

### A / B / C discriminators for this matter {#b:1d795d00}

1. **Cure-not-bet.** *A:* argues easement **and** license from the instrument's own words, then makes a **recorded Delahaye easement the closing condition** and structures a contingency with a firm outer date [m04.fact.037][m04.fact.041]. *B:* correct issue-frame and a workable contingency but bets on one characterization or gives thin cure mechanics. *C:* calls it "an easement," stops, and drafts around a right that isn't insurable. {#b:795e933a}
2. **Objective leverage vs. leaked urgency.** *A:* leads with the title company's refusal to insure [m04.fact.040] and **protects all four confidential facts.** *B:* protects most but leaks one (often the LOI or the septic) or hints at urgency. *C:* argues from the client's need to close — or reveals the Aug 15 deadline [m04.fact.025]. {#b:8d681d60}
3. **The vacation, sized right.** *A:* treats Verdant Shore Road as a **secondary backstop**, notes it's on the wrong (lake) side and "may do nothing" [m04.fact.016][witness-vestergaard]. *B:* over-relies on it as a co-equal cure. *C:* **banks the deal** on the vacation. {#b:53e06094}
4. **Rule 4.2 line.** *A:* routes Renard through counsel and deals with Delahaye directly under **Rule 4.3**. *B:* gets the conclusion right but shaky on *why*. *C:* interviews Renard directly or muddles the represented/unrepresented distinction. {#b:01068bbc}

### Common errors to dock {#b:4d784af6}

- Calling the 1978 instrument an **easement without analyzing its language** ("neighborly relations," named grantees, no "heirs and assigns"), its recording, or its consideration [exhibit-002]. {#b:115535c4}
- Missing that **even a valid easement is unlocated** (no description; 15-ft deviation) and **uninsurable without cure** [m04.fact.011][m04.fact.012][m04.fact.040]. {#b:9d60ee70}
- **Ignoring the title-commitment Schedule B exceptions** (items 2 and 3) and the insurer's cure condition [exhibit-004]. {#b:68e712aa}
- **Banking on the road vacation** as the access solution [m04.fact.016]. {#b:81eb81d6}
- A contingency with **no firm outer date** (or one past Aug 15), or an open-ended "sole discretion" clause the seller would never sign [exhibit-001 ¶4]. {#b:c677fa4c}
- **Leaking a confidential fact** at the table — ceiling, deadline, LOI, or septic [m04.fact.024][m04.fact.025][m04.fact.026][m04.fact.028]. {#b:5c88bfe9}
- **Rule 4.2 / 4.3 slips** — contacting Renard directly; advising Delahaye; posing as neutral. {#b:343af7a9}
- Forgetting the **shoreland-density contingency** and the **$60k septic** in valuation [m04.fact.023][m04.fact.028]. {#b:f4f80812}
- Overstating certainty where the record is genuinely ambiguous — pretending the easement-vs-license question has one clean answer. It does not: **reasonable minds differ** [m04.fact.007]. {#b:2fa7bd5d}

### Re-write-loop guidance {#b:a88c541e}
