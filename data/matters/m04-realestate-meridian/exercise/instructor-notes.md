# Instructor Notes — m04 Real Estate (Renard–Meskin)

*Teaching notes for faculty only. NOT part of the student packet. No answer key or intentional-error catalog here — this is a teaching guide with a few Meridian-canon pointers, marked instructor-verified-at-publish. Keep out of student-facing output.*

## What this exercise teaches

This is the transactional-negotiation shape: buyer's counsel must convert a messy access problem into a purchase agreement, not a lawsuit. It is the corpus's per-side confidential-facts exercise — the pedagogy lives in the tension between what the client will pay/needs and what the student may reveal. The three graded muscles are (1) an SSNP built on real interests and BATNA, (2) a correct issue-frame on easement-vs-license and the road vacation, and (3) drafting that actually protects the buyer. Rule 4.2 is the professional-responsibility spine.

## Discussion spine (the access question)

- **Easement appurtenant vs. license.** The central research question is whether the 1978 instrument created an easement that runs with the land (and thus passes to Meskin) or a revocable license personal to the Renards. Facts cutting toward an easement: it is a recorded, notarized written grant, "for ingress and egress," supported by forty-plus years of open use. Facts cutting toward a license / weakness: "in consideration of neighborly relations," named grantees, no "heirs and assigns," no legal description, and the grantor's successor (Delahaye) disputing permanence. Good students argue both and then de-risk by *curing* rather than betting on a favorable construction.
- **Indefinite location.** Even a valid easement is troubled by the missing legal description and the ~15-foot deviation of the traveled lane. The cleanest cure is a new recorded easement fixing centerline and width — which is exactly what Delahaye has offered to sell.
- **City vacation.** The Verdant Shore Road vacation is a contingency, not a solution: reversion to abutting owners may or may not yield usable access. Do not let students bank the deal on it.

## Meridian-canon pointers *(instructor-verified-at-publish; do not put in student materials)*

- Recording / race-notice framework: **Mer. Stat. § 507.34**; the dual abstract/Torrens system and County Recorder conventions are in `meridian.json` (`real_estate_conventions`). 4400 is abstract property.
- Statute of limitations touchpoints if a dispute were to arise (not the posture here): injury to property / trespass 6 years, **Mer. Stat. § 210.07**; recovery of real property / adverse-possession backdrop 15 years, **Mer. Stat. § 541.02**.
- Professional conduct: no-contact rule is **Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2** (represented seller); dealing with an unrepresented person is **Rule 4.3**; conflicts **Rule 1.7**. Numbering tracks the ABA Model Rules.
- Conveyancing documents expected (Purchase Agreement, Title Commitment, Warranty/Quitclaim Deed, CRV for transfers over $3,000, closing statement) are listed in `meridian.json`.

## Rule 4.2 teaching moment

Ruth Renard is a represented opposing party. A student playing buyer's counsel who tries to interview her directly should trigger the in-app teaching moment: communication about the subject of the representation must go through her counsel. Contrast with Corwin Delahaye, who is unrepresented — direct contact is permissible, subject to Rule 4.3 (no legal advice, correct any misunderstanding of your role). This contrast is worth surfacing in debrief.

## Facilitation tips

- The strongest deals pair a price near the low-to-mid $370s–$380s with a Delahaye recorded-easement cure and a backstop road-vacation contingency, closing before Meskin's (undisclosed) financing deadline.
