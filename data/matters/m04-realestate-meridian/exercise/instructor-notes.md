# Instructor Notes — m04 Real Estate (Renard–Meskin) {#b:405dc1f7}

*Teaching notes for faculty only. NOT part of the student packet. No answer key or intentional-error catalog here — this is a teaching guide with a few Meridian-canon pointers, marked instructor-verified-at-publish. Keep out of student-facing output.* {#b:7266b716}

## What this exercise teaches {#b:46dde98e}

This is the transactional-negotiation shape: buyer's counsel must convert a messy access problem into a purchase agreement, not a lawsuit. It is the corpus's per-side confidential-facts exercise — the pedagogy lives in the tension between what the client will pay/needs and what the student may reveal. The three graded muscles are (1) an SSNP built on real interests and BATNA, (2) a correct issue-frame on easement-vs-license and the road vacation, and (3) drafting that actually protects the buyer. Rule 4.2 is the professional-responsibility spine. {#b:21ef5d2c}

## Discussion spine (the access question) {#b:ffca8d0e}

- **Easement appurtenant vs. license.** The central research question is whether the 1978 instrument created an easement that runs with the land (and thus passes to Meskin) or a revocable license personal to the Renards. Facts cutting toward an easement: it is a recorded, notarized written grant, "for ingress and egress," supported by forty-plus years of open use. Facts cutting toward a license / weakness: "in consideration of neighborly relations," named grantees, no "heirs and assigns," no legal description, and the grantor's successor (Delahaye) disputing permanence. Good students argue both and then de-risk by *curing* rather than betting on a favorable construction. {#b:8a865407}
- **Indefinite location.** Even a valid easement is troubled by the missing legal description and the ~15-foot deviation of the traveled lane. The cleanest cure is a new recorded easement fixing centerline and width — which is exactly what Delahaye has offered to sell. {#b:c8d3b299}
- **City vacation.** The Verdant Shore Road vacation is a contingency, not a solution: reversion to abutting owners may or may not yield usable access. Do not let students bank the deal on it. {#b:aa4bcc67}

## Meridian-canon pointers *(instructor-verified-at-publish; do not put in student materials)* {#b:e897e877}

- Recording / race-notice framework: **Mer. Stat. § 507.34**; the dual abstract/Torrens system and County Recorder conventions are in `meridian.json` (`real_estate_conventions`). 4400 is abstract property. {#b:2b68a926}
- Statute of limitations touchpoints if a dispute were to arise (not the posture here): injury to property / trespass 6 years, **Mer. Stat. § 210.07**; recovery of real property / adverse-possession backdrop 15 years, **Mer. Stat. § 541.02**. {#b:17d8a440}
- Professional conduct: no-contact rule is **Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2** (represented seller); dealing with an unrepresented person is **Rule 4.3**; conflicts **Rule 1.7**. Numbering tracks the ABA Model Rules. {#b:64a0f263}
- Conveyancing documents expected (Purchase Agreement, Title Commitment, Warranty/Quitclaim Deed, CRV for transfers over $3,000, closing statement) are listed in `meridian.json`. {#b:9911701a}

## Rule 4.2 teaching moment {#b:f2fa3c82}

Ruth Renard is a represented opposing party. A student playing buyer's counsel who tries to interview her directly should trigger the in-app teaching moment: communication about the subject of the representation must go through her counsel. Contrast with Corwin Delahaye, who is unrepresented — direct contact is permissible, subject to Rule 4.3 (no legal advice, correct any misunderstanding of your role). This contrast is worth surfacing in debrief. {#b:638cd77d}

## Suggested teaching arc & timing {#b:2226d439}

A two-session (or one long-block) arc. The answer key (`answer-key.md`) holds the model answers; this arc is only *how to run the room*. {#b:b291d76b}

- **Session 1 — interview & diagnosis (~90 min).** {#b:c5dc38e7}
  - *0–15:* Frame the transactional posture (deal, not lawsuit) and the two access wrinkles. Do **not** pre-answer easement-vs-license. {#b:9cfd14dc}
  - *15–55:* Students run the Meskin client interview on the chat engine; a few also try Delahaye. Circulate and note who earns the rapport-gated facts vs. who bullies (fails by design). {#b:25bf211f}
  - *55–90:* Live debrief of the interview: what did you surface, and *how*? Surface the Rule 4.2 trap for anyone who tried to interview Renard. {#b:7e84ed42}
- **Between sessions:** students draft the SSNP and the access contingency; you spot-check for the two failure modes (flat "it's an easement"; banking on the vacation). {#b:92af0152}
- **Session 2 — negotiation & drafting (~90 min).** {#b:6c8565de}
  - *0–20:* Cold-call the easement-vs-license split; force both sides on the board before anyone concludes. {#b:6c27a566}
  - *20–60:* Negotiation round with seller's side; watch for confidential-fact leaks and whether leverage is led by the title refusal or the client's urgency. {#b:bd82f5ac}
  - *60–90:* Redline the access contingency together; stress-test the outer date against the (undisclosed) Aug 15 cliff; close on the counseling memo's "reasonable minds differ" honesty. {#b:2fde028d}

## Discussion questions {#b:5d64cbf7}

1. The 1978 instrument is recorded and notarized but says "neighborly relations" and names the Renards with no "heirs and assigns." Which way does each feature cut — and why is *curing* smarter than litigating the answer? {#b:cab1b7a4}
2. If long, open use can fix the location of an indefinitely described right of way, does the 15-foot deviation *toward* Delahaye's cabin help or hurt the buyer? Who bears that uncertainty at closing? {#b:b727b190}
3. The city planner says the vacation "might help, might do nothing." What has to be true — geographically and under shoreland rules — for Verdant Shore Road to become real access, and how do you draft for a contingency you can't predict? {#b:bc11deef}
4. Your client's best leverage is objective (the title company won't insure) and his worst exposure is confidential (the Aug 15 deadline). How do you run the negotiation so the first does the work and the second never shows? {#b:97b67fa7}
5. Ruth is represented; Corwin is not. Walk the line between Rule 4.2 and Rule 4.3 — what may you say to Corwin, and what must you not? {#b:0044ebcb}
6. Delahaye quietly wanted to buy 4400 himself. How does a latent rival-buyer change the way you negotiate a $25k easement from him? {#b:a6beba64}

## Facilitation tips {#b:d943474d}

- In the client interview, watch whether students earn the rapport-gated facts (ceiling price $395k, the management LOI, the septic surprise) through confidentiality reassurance and process explanation — or try to bully them out, which should fail by design. {#b:325eb635}
- The strongest deals pair a price near the low-to-mid $370s–$380s with a Delahaye recorded-easement cure and a backstop road-vacation contingency, closing before Meskin's (undisclosed) financing deadline. {#b:6755efd8}
