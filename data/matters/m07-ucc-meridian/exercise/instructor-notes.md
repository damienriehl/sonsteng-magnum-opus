## What this exercise is teaching {#b:6d318d1f}

A UCC Article 2 sale-of-goods, breach-of-warranty and remedies problem, headed for mediation then binding arbitration (per the agreement's clause). The pedagogical spine is: (1) a real client interview with a guarded manager who is sitting on facts that hurt his own case; (2) the classic **limited-remedy / failure-of-essential-purpose / consequential-damages-exclusion** analysis; and (3) counseling under mixed causation. It also stages a **Rule 4.2** no-contact moment because Brandt is represented. {#b:ddabf639}

## Governing law orientation (Meridian canon) {#b:64d15740}

- **Sale of goods / warranty limitations period:** Mer. Stat. § 305.20 (four years; mirrors UCC § 2-725; accrues on tender of delivery). The 2025 tender is comfortably within the period as of the 2026 as-of date — no limitations issue. {#b:a8b7398b}
- The Meridian UCC tracks the uniform text; instructors can map the analysis to the standard uniform sections: {#b:6545075e}
  - Express warranty analog to **UCC § 2-313** (the written warranty and the express performance spec at ≤40°F / 38°F pull-down). {#b:5749b3e3}
  - Implied warranty of merchantability analog to **UCC § 2-314**; fitness for particular purpose analog to **§ 2-315**. {#b:82610eaa}
  - Disclaimer/limitation analog to **§ 2-316**. {#b:7f965a71}
  - Contractual limitation of remedy and **failure of essential purpose** analog to **§ 2-719** — the core issue. Note the live split of authority on whether a failed limited remedy also knocks out a separately stated consequential-damages exclusion, versus treating the two clauses independently in a commercial deal. Reward students who see the two-clause structure. {#b:c3991297}
  - Buyer's damages / consequential damages analog to **§§ 2-714, 2-715**, including foreseeability and the duty to mitigate (cover via the rented cooler). {#b:2353bdf4}
- **Forum:** the agreement's clause sends this to mediation then binding arbitration seated in Meridian; there is no District Court trial. Good students adjust leverage, discovery, cost, and confidentiality expectations accordingly (and recognize the narrow judicial review of an award). {#b:a1814ddc}

## Facts that cut against the client (surface in debrief) {#b:0cfea0cd}

- Deferred condenser maintenance in November ([m07.fact.037]) and the pre-purchase panel warning the board deferred ([m07.fact.038]) are **rapport-gated** — students who interview well and without judgment should get them. A student who never surfaces them has a blind spot to flag. {#b:3bda5837}
- The skipped 48-hour commissioning test ([m07.fact.039]) is **concealed** — the client volunteers it to essentially no one. It is legitimate for most students not to reach it; use it to discuss how undisclosed bad facts detonate later. {#b:c4fc3de5}
- The mixed-causation engineering picture ([m07.fact.032]–[m07.fact.034]) is the honest core: this is not a clean win. Reward candor over advocacy theater. {#b:d882e85e}

## Rule 4.2 moment {#b:eecf6bbd}

Brandt is represented by Attorney Bernice Skoglund (Skoglund & Pratt LLP). A student who tries to interview Sylvia Brandt or Brandt's technician directly should trigger the no-contact teaching moment (Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2, ABA-model numbering per the canon). Contact must route through opposing counsel. This is a fictional respondent-lawyer name for a firm, not a real attorney. {#b:9453fdc5}

## Money / business layer {#b:8cd9295c}

Hourly engagement at the firm's standard rates (senior $250, associate $225). The matter is a real accounts-receivable file: the co-op is a slow-paying, aggrieved client, and the ledger carries roughly $17,900 outstanding across the spring invoices, aged into the 31–60 and 61–90 buckets — a good hook for a practice-management discussion about carrying AR for a sympathetic small client. {#b:2c75f62f}

## Common student errors to watch for {#b:fc544c5b}

- Conflating the limited-remedy failure with the consequential-damages exclusion (they are separate provisions). {#b:c471a4e8}
- Overpromising the client a full consequential-damages recovery without grappling with the exclusion or the mixed causation. {#b:c26b0c27}
- Missing that this is arbitration, not a jury trial, and mis-sizing the settlement value. {#b:7dda20ec}
- Contacting the represented opposing party. {#b:84505ba3}

## Teaching arc (how to run the session) {#b:551b2ba7}

The arc moves the student from *listening* to *judgment*, and the design intent is that the hard parts are earned, not handed over: {#b:ab87af11}

1. **Warm open, then the guarded interview.** Start on relationship and orientation. The pedagogical payload is that Aaberg is guarded and sits on two rapport-gated facts (deferred maintenance, the deferred panel upgrade) and one concealed fact (the skipped 48-hour commissioning test). Students who lead with judgment or verification pressure get the volunteered layer and stall. See the Aaberg tier table in the key (§ 2.1). {#b:8fd5a714}
2. **Records into a chronology.** The student turns the case file — purchase agreement/warranty, the blank commissioning fields, the operator log, service reports, Larkspur tickets, demand/denial, and the Rask report — into a clean three-failure timeline and names the gaps (unknown controller logs; disputed startup). {#b:4b5980cb}
3. **The two-clause pivot.** The intellectual center: separate the limited repair-or-replace remedy from the consequential-damages exclusion, run failure-of-essential-purpose, then independently test whether the exclusion survives in a commercial deal. This is where A-work separates from B-work (key § 1.4–1.5, rubric c02). {#b:0bce08a4}
4. **Candor under mixed causation.** The engineer's honest "no safety margin even if perfectly maintained" finding coexists with the co-op's panel and maintenance problems. Reward students who counsel honestly over those who advocate past the bad facts. {#b:4a94ece2}
5. **ADR and the 4.2 boundary.** Close on realistic settlement sizing for binding arbitration (not a jury) and the no-contact rule. If the student reaches for Brandt's people, the in-app 4.2 moment should fire; debrief it as a real ethics limit, not a tactic. {#b:be8ae851}

## Timing (a 2-hour block; scale pro rata for a 90-minute or two-session build) {#b:7f684839}

- **Framing & goals (10 min).** Orient to posture (pre-arbitration, demand-and-investigation) and the buyer's-counsel role. Do **not** pre-teach the two-clause structure — let them find it. {#b:55e3c3bc}
- **Client interview with Aaberg (30 min).** The core relational exercise. Watch live or review transcripts for whether the guarded tiers were earned. {#b:f49d50e9}
- **Case-file + witness synthesis (25 min).** Chronology building and gap-spotting; optional short Tuvang interview for the log and discovery-of-failure detail. {#b:6c27ed0c}
- **Warranty/remedies analysis worktime (25 min).** The § 7 vs. § 8 split, warranties, and revocation-vs-rejection. Circulate; ask "which clause are you in right now?" {#b:84cdb97a}
- **ADR + strategy (15 min).** Mediation vs. arbitration, BATNA, settlement range, Larkspur relationship. {#b:af391a31}
- **Debrief (15 min).** Use the questions below; anchor on candor and the two-clause move. {#b:c8dafc58}

## Debrief discussion questions (pick 4–6) {#b:1ffeffb1}

1. **The two clauses.** If the repair-or-replace remedy failed of its essential purpose, does the consequential-damages exclusion fall with it — or stand on its own? What turns on this being a commercial, merchant-to-merchant deal? (Instructor orientation: § 2-719(2) vs. § 2-719(3); commercial-loss exclusions are not prima facie unconscionable — key § 1.5.) {#b:a0503590}
2. **Earning the hard facts.** Which facts only surfaced because the interview built trust, and which never came out? What did *not* asking about maintenance or the panel cost the analysis? (Key § 2.1: [m07.fact.037], [m07.fact.038] rapport-gated; [m07.fact.039] concealed.) {#b:96aaa16f}
3. **Candor vs. advocacy.** The co-op's own engineer will not hand you a clean win. How do you counsel a client whose case is genuinely mixed without either sugar-coating or defeatism? (Key § 1.8.) {#b:8c3d867b}
4. **Reject or revoke?** The co-op accepted and kept using the unit. Is this a rejection case, a revocation case, or a breach-of-warranty-of-accepted-goods case — and why does the answer change the size of the recovery? (Key § 1.6.) {#b:8503ee6c}
5. **Sizing the deal.** Knowing the exclusion may survive and causation is mixed, what is a realistic settlement — and how does binding arbitration (narrow review, confidentiality, cost) shape your number and your leverage? (Key § 1.10, rubric c04.) {#b:a5a28f7d}
6. **The 4.2 line.** Where exactly is the no-contact boundary — does it reach only Sylvia Brandt, or also technician Pelletier? What do you do when the fastest way to a fact runs through the other side's people? (Key § 2.3; Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2.) {#b:7a7387af}
7. **The undisclosed detonator.** Suppose the skipped commissioning test ([m07.fact.039]) never surfaces in your interview but Brandt proves it in arbitration. How should that change how you counsel clients about their own bad facts? {#b:cf0d49c8}
