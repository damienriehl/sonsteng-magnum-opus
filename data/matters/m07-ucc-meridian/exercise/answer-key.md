## (1) Model Case Analysis — keyed to fact anchors {#b:1aa62bfb}

### 1.1 The transaction and governing frame {#b:23269d6d}

Cloverdyke Dairy Cooperative (buyer) is a farmer-owned co-op operating a shared bulk-milk receiving and cooling station [m07.fact.001]. On September 10, 2025 it bought a bulk milk-cooling and refrigeration unit, installed, from Brandt Refrigeration Systems, LLC (seller), a regional commercial-refrigeration dealer-installer, for $96,000 [m07.fact.004][m07.fact.005]. Both parties are merchants dealing in this kind of transaction; this is a **sale of goods governed by UCC Article 2 as adopted in Meridian**. The four-year warranty limitations period (**Mer. Stat. § 305.20**, mirroring UCC § 2-725, accruing on tender of delivery) runs from the October 15, 2025 install/tender [m07.fact.010]; as of the June 30, 2026 as-of date this is comfortably in-period — **no limitations issue**, and an A-answer says so briefly and moves on. (A student who agonizes over the statute of limitations is misreading the problem.) {#b:1bea15ab}

### 1.2 Which warranties apply {#b:3c7ae9a0}

**Express warranties (§ 2-313 analog) — strong.** Two independent express warranties: {#b:2ddcfd79}
- The **written limited warranty**: the unit is free from defects in material and workmanship for 24 months [m07.fact.006] (Exhibit m07.exh.001 § 7). {#b:d9cd9cf5}
- The **express performance specification**: the unit "shall maintain the bulk tank contents at or below 40°F" and "reduce fresh incoming milk to 38°F" within the pull-down time [m07.fact.007] (§ 3). This is an express warranty of a bargained-for performance level, and the three failures — tank reaching ~55°F on Nov 3 [m07.fact.015]; compressor trip Nov 24 [m07.fact.017]; weekend drift Dec 12 [m07.fact.018] — are direct evidence the unit did not meet the promised spec. This is the buyer's cleanest breach theory. {#b:3ff762c4}

**Implied warranty of merchantability (§ 2-314 analog) — strong, and watch the disclaimer gap.** Brandt is a merchant with respect to refrigeration equipment [m07.fact.004]; the unit must be fit for the ordinary purpose of cooling and holding bulk milk. Repeated failure to hold temperature is prima facie unmerchantable. **Key teaching point:** the agreement limits the *remedy* (§ 7) and excludes *consequential damages* (§ 8), but on the face of Exhibit m07.exh.001 it contains **no § 2-316 disclaimer of the implied warranties** — no conspicuous "AS IS," no language mentioning "merchantability." An A-answer notices that Brandt limited remedies but never effectively disclaimed the implied warranty of merchantability, so that warranty is very likely still alive. {#b:248638cc}

**Implied warranty of fitness for a particular purpose (§ 2-315 analog) — viable, and unusually well-supported here.** Brandt, as dealer-installer, had reason to know the co-op's particular purpose (cooling this station's peak incoming milk volume to the contract spec) and the co-op relied on Brandt's skill to select and size the unit. The engineer found the unit "marginally undersized for the co-op's peak cooling load" [m07.fact.032], and — the confidential kicker on Brandt's side — Brandt's own installer flagged **before shipment** that the condensing unit was "at the very edge of its rated capacity for the co-op's stated load," and Brandt shipped it anyway rather than upsize [m07.fact.040]. That fact (if it ever surfaces in discovery/arbitration) is powerful fitness-warranty and defect evidence. Note the student cannot get [m07.fact.040] from any interview available to buyer's counsel (see § 2); it is instructor ground truth. {#b:04c5e751}

### 1.3 The limited remedy, disclaimer, and the two-clause structure {#b:0f600cbc}

The agreement contains **two distinct provisions**, and the single most important analytical move in this matter is to keep them separate: {#b:aaaddd70}
- **§ 7 — Limited remedy:** repair or replacement of defective parts, at Seller's option, as the buyer's "sole and exclusive remedy" [m07.fact.006]. {#b:3d1491d2}
- **§ 8 — Consequential-damages exclusion:** "IN NO EVENT" liable for incidental or consequential damages, expressly listing spoiled product, lost production, lost profits, and the cost of substitute cooling [m07.fact.006]. {#b:b4528c8f}

Conflating these is the signature error (see § 4 and instructor-notes common errors). They rise and fall under different rules (§ 2-719(2) vs. § 2-719(3)). {#b:0acbaa29}

### 1.4 Failure of essential purpose (§ 2-719(2) analog) — the core issue {#b:f9d541c5}

A repair-or-replace remedy fails of its essential purpose when repeated repair attempts do not restore a conforming product (the "shaken faith"/recurring-failure line of cases). The facts build this argument: {#b:f9041c4a}
- The **same essential function** (holding temperature) failed **three times in the first two months** [m07.fact.014]. {#b:2f972307}
- Brandt's technician's interventions — reset controls, replaced a contactor, cleaned coils [m07.fact.024] — treated symptoms; the engineer's root findings were an **improper refrigerant charge and expansion-valve setting at installation** plus **marginal undersizing** [m07.fact.032], neither of which a contactor swap or coil cleaning cures. {#b:45676210}
- The engineer's honest bottom line: even a **perfectly maintained** unit, charged/adjusted and sized as this one was found, "would operate without a safety margin" [m07.fact.032] — i.e., the remedy as delivered could not make the unit conform. {#b:8c1cbbc9}

An A-answer concludes the limited remedy **likely failed of its essential purpose**, while candidly flagging Brandt's counter: several failures trace to conditions Brandt says are the buyer's — voltage imbalance on the co-op's panel [m07.fact.033] and fouled coils from deferred maintenance [m07.fact.037] — so Brandt will argue the *unit's* repair remedy never "failed" because the unit was not the (sole) problem. Genuinely disputed; reward the student who holds both. {#b:96e57250}

### 1.5 Does the consequential-damages exclusion survive? (§ 2-719(3) analog) — the money question {#b:5287fac5}

This is where students separate. There is a **live split of authority**: {#b:94a6e3ac}
- **Dependent view:** when the limited remedy fails of its essential purpose, the consequential-damages exclusion falls with it, and the buyer may recover consequentials. {#b:918c6e22}
- **Independent view (majority in commercial deals):** the two clauses are separate; a failed limited remedy does not automatically void a **separately stated** consequential-damages exclusion, which survives unless unconscionable. Under § 2-719(3), a limitation of consequential damages **for commercial loss is not prima facie unconscionable** (unlike personal-injury loss). {#b:bf6a8bd2}

Because this is a **commercial, merchant-to-merchant** deal [m07.fact.004][m07.fact.005], the honest assessment is that the § 8 exclusion **may well survive** even if the § 7 remedy fails — putting the spoiled-milk, lost-premium, and rental-cooler recovery **at real risk**. The unconscionability challenge (§ 2-302 analog) is weak here: two sophisticated commercial parties, a negotiated equipment purchase, and the § 2-719(3) presumption all cut against the buyer. The strongest unconscionability hook is narrow — if a failed limited remedy plus an enforced exclusion would leave the buyer with **no meaningful remedy at all**, some courts recoil — but an A-answer treats that as an argument to make, not a win to promise. {#b:60d4fd0d}

**A-grade takeaway:** do not promise full consequential recovery. The realistic play is to lead with the **more reliable remedies** (revocation/replacement of the unit — § 1.6) and treat the ~$28,000 in consequentials as contested leverage, not a sure thing. {#b:53466455}

### 1.6 Rejection vs. revocation of acceptance {#b:dc17b8c3}

The student must classify the posture correctly. The co-op **accepted** the goods — the unit was installed and placed into production service in October [m07.fact.010], and used through the fall. So this is **not a rejection** case (§ 2-602 rejection is a pre-acceptance right; that window closed at acceptance). The live question is **revocation of acceptance under § 2-608**: {#b:625ad8bb}
- **Substantial impairment:** three failures of the core cooling function, spoiling ~19,000 lb of milk [m07.fact.015][m07.fact.017][m07.fact.018][m07.fact.020], plausibly impair the unit's value to the co-op substantially. {#b:91853327}
- **Reasonable assumption of cure / difficulty of discovery:** acceptance at install was reasonable on the assumption defects would be seasonably cured; the defect was latent (install-configuration and sizing) [m07.fact.032]. {#b:c182fd68}
- **Timeliness / seasonableness:** the Jan 20, 2026 demand expressly sought **replacement of the unit** [m07.fact.029] — functionally a revocation — within roughly six weeks of the third failure. Defensible as timely. {#b:203f236e}
- **Complication — continued use:** the co-op kept operating (supplementing with the rented portable cooler [m07.fact.022]) rather than tendering the unit back, which Brandt will argue is inconsistent with revocation. An A-answer flags this tension and notes revocation may need to be pleaded in the alternative to a straight breach-of-warranty-of-accepted-goods claim (§ 2-714). {#b:7420259c}

Why revocation matters strategically: if the exclusion (§ 8) survives, revocation is the buyer's route to the **big number** — return of the $96,000 price [m07.fact.005] (or a conforming replacement) — because that recovery is **not** a "consequential" the exclusion reaches. {#b:e576d818}

### 1.7 Notice of breach (§ 2-607(3)(a) analog) — satisfied {#b:c76d774d}

A buyer who accepts must notify the seller of breach within a reasonable time or be barred. Here notice is **clearly satisfied**: the co-op called Brandt on each failure (Brandt's technician responded to all three calls [m07.fact.023]) and sent a written warranty-claim demand on Jan 20, 2026 [m07.fact.029]. Not a live vulnerability, but the student should affirmatively check the box. {#b:b1d910c1}

### 1.8 Causation of the spoiled-product loss — genuinely mixed {#b:2ab7fef7}

The causation picture is the honest core of the matter and must be handled candidly [m07.fact.034]. Three strands: {#b:a7268699}
- **Equipment / installation defect (buyer-favorable):** improper refrigerant charge and expansion-valve setting at installation, plus marginal undersizing [m07.fact.032]; installer's pre-shipment edge-of-capacity flag [m07.fact.040]. The engineer's "no safety margin even if perfectly maintained" finding [m07.fact.032] makes the **defect the primary driver** and casts the other factors as aggravators. {#b:f340a1d1}
- **Electrical supply (buyer-adverse / contested responsibility):** a measured voltage imbalance on one leg of the co-op's three-phase panel [m07.fact.033]; the technician's "possible incoming power irregularity" note [m07.fact.025]; and the pre-purchase Ostrowski warning that the panel was undersized and aging, which the board deferred to save money [m07.fact.038]. The building panel is the **co-op's** side of the meter — a real problem for the buyer. Counter-nuance: Brandt does not actually know whether the imbalance **predated** the compressor faults or resulted from them [m07.fact.041], and a properly sized, properly configured unit tolerates more supply variation. {#b:648bd6b1}
- **Maintenance (buyer-adverse):** the co-op deferred a scheduled condenser cleaning in November while short-staffed, leaving coils fouled beyond the schedule [m07.fact.037]; the technician's reports repeatedly flagged fouled coils and lapsed maintenance [m07.fact.025]. This triggers § 9 of the agreement, under which failure to maintain "may void warranty coverage **as to defects caused by such failure**" [m07.fact.006] — note the causal limiter: it voids coverage only for failures the lapse caused, not globally. {#b:12e65268}

**Equipment vs. installation/electrical — the honest answer:** it is **both**, and an A-answer says so. The defect/undersizing is Brandt's; the panel and the deferred maintenance are the co-op's; and the engineer will not assign a clean percentage [m07.fact.032][m07.fact.034]. This mixed picture is exactly why the case does not settle for the full demand and why candor with the client beats advocacy theater. {#b:eb0eb0c0}

### 1.9 Damages measure {#b:5a159346}

- **Direct / warranty damages (§ 2-714 analog):** difference between the value of the unit as warranted and as accepted, or cost to repair/replace; on revocation, **return of the $96,000 price** or a conforming replacement [m07.fact.005]. {#b:6a2320f0}
- **Incidental damages (§ 2-715(1) analog):** ~$6,500 in emergency repairs and ~$9,000 to rent the portable cooling trailer [m07.fact.022] — the trailer doubles as **cover/mitigation** (substitute cooling to keep operating), which the buyer should foreground as reasonable mitigation. Caution: § 8 **expressly lists "the cost of substitute cooling"** among excluded items [m07.fact.006], so Brandt will argue even the trailer rental is barred — contested. {#b:896a88ba}
- **Consequential damages (§ 2-715(2) analog):** spoiled-milk value across ~19,000 lb over three loads [m07.fact.015][m07.fact.017][m07.fact.018][m07.fact.020] and lost quality premiums under the Larkspur supply contract [m07.fact.019][m07.fact.020], together with the incidentals totaling ~$28,000 on top of the unit [m07.fact.021]. **Foreseeable** (Brandt sold a milk cooler to a dairy co-op that ships to a creamery [m07.fact.019]) — but squarely within the § 8 exclusion and subject to the § 2-715(2)(a) cover/mitigation limit. {#b:93348388}
- **Proof:** spoilage quantities corroborated by Tuvang's contemporaneous handwritten log [m07.fact.035] and the Larkspur rejection tickets and credit memos graded by Callahan [m07.fact.020]; premium loss documented by the same credit memos. Evidentiary gap: whether the unit controller's internal temperature logs were overwritten or are recoverable is unknown [m07.fact.036] — a preservation/spoliation item the student should flag proactively. {#b:807717d3}

### 1.10 ADR posture {#b:ffa19dc2}

The agreement requires **non-binding mediation, then binding arbitration seated in Meridian** [m07.fact.009] (Exhibit m07.exh.001 § 11). No lawsuit is filed; the matter is pre-arbitration, in the demand-and-investigation phase as of June 30, 2026 [m07.fact.044]. There is **no District Court jury trial**. Consequences the student should internalize: narrow judicial review of any award, lower cost and faster timeline than litigation, confidentiality (which protects Brandt's reputation concerns and the co-op's Larkspur relationship [m07.fact.043]), and constrained discovery. Leverage sizing must be realistic given the § 8 exclusion risk and mixed causation. {#b:240b8722}

### 1.11 What an A-grade deliverable finds (bottom line) {#b:40a269c9}

- Correctly frames a **commercial UCC Article 2 warranty and remedies** problem; disposes of the SOL in a sentence (§ 305.20; in-period) [m07.fact.010]. {#b:8c1452b4}
- Identifies **express** (written warranty + performance spec) [m07.fact.006][m07.fact.007] and **implied** (merchantability; fitness) warranties, and **spots the missing § 2-316 disclaimer** — implied warranties survive. {#b:eef039cc}
- **Separates the two clauses**: argues § 7 limited remedy **likely failed of its essential purpose** [m07.fact.014][m07.fact.032], but candidly concludes the § 8 consequential-damages exclusion **may survive** in a commercial deal, so the ~$28,000 is contested, not banked [m07.fact.021]. {#b:5e74dce2}
- Recommends leading with **revocation/replacement** (the reliable path to the price/a working unit) and treating consequentials as leverage. {#b:a3fe1950}
- Confirms **notice** [m07.fact.029], handles **mixed causation** honestly [m07.fact.032][m07.fact.034][m07.fact.037][m07.fact.038], and flags the **controller-log preservation** gap [m07.fact.036]. {#b:e29a0173}
- Counsels a **realistic ADR strategy**: mediate first, size settlement to a compromise (conforming replacement or price credit plus partial cost-sharing) rather than the full demand, and preserve the Larkspur relationship [m07.fact.043]. {#b:44120113}
- Observes **Rule 4.2** — no direct contact with Brandt's people [m07.fact.042]; route through opposing counsel. {#b:3756348d}
- Keeps client-facing communications **facts-and-advice, no citations**; law lives in the separately assessed analysis. {#b:b3924f23}

### 2.3 Rule 4.2 — a represented adverse party DOES exist here {#b:9ce7ec55}

**Yes — this matter contains a live Rule 4.2 trap.** Sylvia Brandt, operations manager and co-owner of Brandt Refrigeration Systems, LLC, is the **opposing party representative** and is **represented by counsel** — Attorney Bernice Skoglund of Skoglund & Pratt LLP (`m07.per.brandt`, `represented_by_counsel: true`, `rule_4_2.applies: true`). She is interviewable **only by the seller role**, never by the student, who is buyer's counsel [m07.fact.042]. {#b:b346470f}

**The rule.** Under **Mer. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2** (ABA-model numbering per the Meridian canon), a lawyer representing a client may not communicate about the subject of the representation with a person the lawyer knows to be **represented by another lawyer** in the matter, absent that lawyer's consent. The bar reaches not only Sylvia Brandt personally but also Brandt's constituents whose acts/omissions bind the organization or who are involved in the matter — which includes **field technician Darius Pelletier** (a Brandt employee central to the events, even though he is a case-file witness, not a chat persona). Contact with any of them must **route through Attorney Skoglund**. {#b:0f7e7112}

**The in-app teaching moment.** If the student attempts to interview Sylvia Brandt (or otherwise reach Brandt's people directly), the interface fires a **no-contact teaching moment** — not a silent block — explaining that this is a professional-responsibility boundary, not a strategic choice, and directing contact through opposing counsel. The engagement letter reinforces this: "neither you nor we may contact Brandt's people directly about this dispute." Rubric criterion **m07.rub.c05** scores whether the student honors the limit; treat a knowing 4.2 violation as a serious professional-responsibility error (see § 4). This is a real 4.2 party — **not** the "no-trap" scenario. (There is no advocate-witness / Rule 3.7 problem in this matter; the only professional-responsibility hinge is 4.2.) {#b:48b45b21}

## (4) Grading Guidance — A/B/C discriminators, common errors, re-write loop {#b:36f68f2e}

### 4.1 Letter-grade bands (exact, per `letter_grade_map`) {#b:21b4f6e5}
- **A = 200; A- = 186; B+ = 176; B = 166; C = 146.** Read as thresholds: **≥ 186 → A-range**, **176–185 → B+**, **166–175 → B**, **146–165 → C**, **below 146 → failing / mandatory rewrite.** {#b:799b36c2}

### 4.2 What distinguishes the grades {#b:018b69d0}
- **A / A- (≥ 186).** Nails the **two-clause structure** and reaches the honest conclusion that the § 8 exclusion may survive even if the § 7 remedy fails [m07.fact.006][m07.fact.014]. Spots the **missing § 2-316 disclaimer**. Classifies posture correctly (accepted goods → revocation under § 2-608, not rejection) [m07.fact.010][m07.fact.029]. Handles **mixed causation** without spin [m07.fact.032][m07.fact.034][m07.fact.037][m07.fact.038]. In the interview, **earns both rapport-gated facts** [m07.fact.037][m07.fact.038] through non-judgmental rapport. Sizes ADR realistically and observes Rule 4.2 cleanly. Facts-only client communication. {#b:31c378a2}
- **B+ / B (166–185).** Solid warranty and failure-of-essential-purpose analysis but **treats the exclusion as automatically falling** with the remedy (misses or underplays the § 2-719(3) commercial-loss nuance), or over-promises consequential recovery. May surface only one of the two rapport-gated facts. Competent but generic ADR counseling. Rule 4.2 respected. Chronology accurate. {#b:933903e1}
- **C (146–165).** Identifies a warranty breach and the spoiled-milk losses but **conflates the limited remedy and the consequential-damages exclusion**, misses the revocation-vs-rejection distinction, and glosses the mixed causation (advocacy over candor). Thin or judgmental interview that never reaches the guarded facts. ADR treated as if it were a jury trial. {#b:a63d67a5}
- **Below 146 (rewrite).** Fundamental misframe (treats it as tort/negligence or a jury case), invents authority or cites law to the client, and/or **commits a Rule 4.2 violation** by contacting Brandt or its people directly. {#b:2e8a3df8}

### 4.3 Common errors to watch for (builds on instructor-notes) {#b:413084e7}
- **Conflating § 7 and § 8** — the signature error; the two provisions are analyzed under different rules. {#b:3337d03f}
- **Over-promising full consequential recovery** without grappling with the § 8 exclusion's likely survival or the mixed causation [m07.fact.021][m07.fact.034]. {#b:6d690163}
- **Rejection/revocation confusion** — calling this a rejection when the goods were accepted [m07.fact.010]; missing revocation as the route to the price [m07.fact.005]. {#b:769fc008}
- **Missing the § 2-316 disclaimer gap** — assuming the implied warranties were disclaimed when the contract never says so [m07.fact.006]. {#b:45ea055d}
- **Mis-sizing settlement** — treating this as a jury trial rather than binding arbitration [m07.fact.009]. {#b:55044751}
- **Contacting the represented opposing party** — a Rule 4.2 violation [m07.fact.042]. {#b:31e7d6b8}
- **Judgmental or badgering interview** — never earns [m07.fact.037] or [m07.fact.038]; a blind spot the student then carries into counseling. {#b:e401c06f}
- **Citing law to the client** — client-facing communications must be facts-and-advice only. {#b:e1c8d65f}

### 4.4 Re-write-loop demands {#b:64a3ea89}
Require a resubmission when the deliverable: (a) conflates the two clauses or concludes the exclusion falls without engaging § 2-719(3); (b) promises the client a recovery the mixed causation and exclusion do not support; (c) commits or fails to recognize the Rule 4.2 boundary; or (d) cites legal authority in the client memo. The rewrite must (i) separate and independently analyze § 7 and § 8; (ii) state the realistic exposure range and recommend the revocation/replacement-led strategy with consequentials as leverage; (iii) demonstrate the client learned the hard facts [m07.fact.037][m07.fact.038] were surfaced and counseled candidly; and (iv) restate the client communication in facts-and-advice form. {#b:8e3a51d0}
