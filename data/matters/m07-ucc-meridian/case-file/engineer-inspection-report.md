# Exhibit m07.exh.007 — Independent Refrigeration Engineer Inspection Report

**Prepared by:** Ingemar Rask, P.E., consulting refrigeration engineer
**Retained by:** Cloverdyke Dairy Cooperative
**Inspection date:** February 27, 2026
**Report date:** March 3, 2026

*Reproduced summary of the engineer's written inspection report. Observations and findings only.*

---

**Scope.** Inspect the installed bulk milk-cooling unit at the Cloverdyke receiving station; review the operator temperature log and the servicer's reports; form an independent opinion on the cause of the repeated temperature failures.

**Observations — refrigeration system.**
1. The condensing unit's refrigerant charge and expansion-valve setting were found outside the correct range for this equipment and application, indicating the system was not properly charged and adjusted at installation.
2. Sizing analysis against the co-op's peak incoming volume shows the unit as configured to be marginally undersized for peak load, with little capacity margin.

**Observations — electrical supply.**
3. A voltage imbalance was measured on one leg of the milk-house three-phase panel. Voltage imbalance stresses a compressor and can contribute to nuisance trips.

**Observations — maintenance.**
4. Condenser coils showed evidence of prior fouling. Fouled coils raise head pressure and degrade cooling; the servicer cleaned the coils on the December visit.

**Findings on causation.** Causation is mixed. The report identifies (a) an installation/configuration defect in the unit, (b) inadequate capacity margin for the operation, and (c) an electrical-supply imbalance on the building side, with (d) intermittent coil fouling as an aggravating factor. The report states that a unit charged, adjusted, and sized as this one was found would operate without a safety margin even under good maintenance, and that the observed pattern of repeated failures is consistent with such a system stressed by both configuration and power problems.

*This exhibit is offered as the co-op's independent engineering assessment. It supports both a defect theory and a mixed-causation picture that the opposing party will use.*
