# Witness Statement — Ingemar Rask, P.E.

**Witness:** Ingemar Rask, P.E., consulting refrigeration engineer (retained by Cloverdyke Dairy Cooperative)
**Taken:** May 12, 2026; signed statement summarizing the February 27, 2026 inspection
**Form:** Signed first-person expert statement (facts and observations)

---

My name is Ingemar Rask. I am a licensed professional engineer and I consult on commercial and agricultural refrigeration systems. Cloverdyke Dairy Cooperative retained me to inspect the bulk milk-cooling unit that Brandt Refrigeration installed at their receiving station and to give my independent opinion about why it repeatedly failed to hold temperature. I inspected the installed unit on site on February 27, 2026, and reviewed the operator's temperature logs and the technician's service reports.

I want to be careful to separate what I observed from what I infer. Here is what I observed.

First, on the refrigeration side, the condensing unit's refrigerant charge and the setting on its expansion valve were not where they should be for this equipment and this application. In plain terms, the system was not set up correctly at installation, and a system charged and adjusted that way will struggle to pull down and hold temperature under real load. Second, when I ran the numbers for the co-op's peak incoming volume, the unit as configured is marginally undersized for that peak load. It has little margin. On a mild day it might keep up; on a heavy day, or with any other stress on the system, it is at the edge.

Separately, on the electrical side, I measured a voltage imbalance on one leg of the milk-house's three-phase panel. A voltage imbalance is hard on a compressor and can contribute to nuisance trips. I did observe that.

Now the harder question is causation, and I will be honest that it is mixed. This is not a case where I can point to one single cause. I see an installation and configuration problem with the unit itself, I see a unit with very little capacity margin for this operation, and I see a real electrical-supply issue on the building side. Any one of those can hurt cooling performance, and here more than one was present at the same time. The repeated pattern of failures is consistent with a system that had too little margin and was then stressed by both a setup problem and a power problem.

I also cannot rule out that fouled condenser coils contributed on the specific days when maintenance had lapsed, because dirty coils raise head pressure and make everything worse. What I can say is that even a perfectly maintained unit, charged and adjusted the way I found this one, and sized the way this one is, would have been operating without a safety margin.

/s/ Ingemar Rask, P.E.
