# Witness Statement — Corinne Callahan

**Witness:** Corinne Callahan, receiving grader, Larkspur Creamery
**Taken:** May 19, 2026, at Larkspur Creamery
**Form:** Signed first-person statement

---

I'm Corinne Callahan. I work the receiving dock at Larkspur Creamery as a grader. When milk comes in, I check it — temperature, smell, the antibiotic and bacteria screens, the paperwork — and I decide whether we take the load, downgrade it, or reject it. I've been doing this work for eleven years. Cloverdyke is one of our supply co-ops and I handle their loads regularly.

There were three loads from Cloverdyke this past fall and winter that I could not accept. I don't have the exact dates memorized, but they're on my tickets, and they line up with when the co-op told us their cooler was having trouble.

Each of those three, the milk came in warm and off. Warm milk grows bacteria fast, and once it's out of temperature for long enough there's nothing to be done with it — it can't go into product. When that happens I write it up on a rejection ticket, note the temperature and the reason, and the load gets dumped. Then our office issues the paperwork on it, the credit memos and the quality deductions, so the accounting is clean on both sides.

I want to be fair to Cloverdyke here. In my experience they run a good, clean operation, and Reuben over there keeps careful records. These three loads weren't a case of sloppy handling on the farm side that I could see. The milk was warm when it reached me, and from what they told me their tank cooler had failed. My job is just to grade what shows up on my dock, and what showed up those three times could not be used. I documented each one the same way I document any rejection.

I can produce copies of the three rejection tickets and the related credit memos if they're needed. Beyond grading the milk and writing it up, I don't have any knowledge about the cooling equipment itself or whose fault the failures were.

/s/ Corinne Callahan
