# Witness Statement — Darius Pelletier {#b:4c49c7ec}

**Witness:** Darius Pelletier, field service technician, Brandt Refrigeration Systems, LLC
**Taken:** April 22, 2026, by telephone; memorialized statement reviewed and adopted by the witness
**Form:** Signed first-person statement {#b:7cdcec25}

---

I'm Darius Pelletier. I've been a field service technician with Brandt Refrigeration for about seven years. I'm the tech who went out to the Cloverdyke co-op on all three of their service calls this past fall and winter, so I'm the one who actually put hands on the unit each time. {#b:e76077ae}

I was part of the crew that installed the unit back in October, and I did the service calls after. On the first call, in early November, the compressor had locked out. I reset the controls and checked it over. It ran when I left. On the second call, later in November, I found a failed contactor and I replaced it. On the third call, in December, I cleaned the condenser coils, which were fouled, and I reset the control board again. {#b:e7bb07d9}

Every time I was out there, I wrote a service report. I try to be accurate in those. On at least one of them I noted that there was a possible incoming power irregularity, because when I metered the supply I saw the voltage on one leg wasn't sitting where I'd want it. I'm not the electrician of record for their building, so I flagged it as something for them to have checked. I also noted more than once that the condenser coils were fouled and that they needed to keep up the maintenance on the schedule that came with the unit. Dirty coils make a unit like this work harder and run hot, and in bad cases that can push a compressor to trip. {#b:8fe580c5}

I'll be straight about what I can and can't say. I can tell you what I found each time and what I did. I can't tell you for certain what the root cause of the whole pattern was — whether it was the power, the coils, something in how the unit was set up at install, or some combination. From my end, the two things I kept seeing were the power reading on that one leg and the coils being dirtier than they should have been. {#b:9d953c36}

On the install itself, I did the work I was assigned. Questions about how the unit was sized for their operation, or about the commissioning process and whether the full test run was done, are above my pay grade — that's the office and the sales side, not me. I did my service work and I documented it. {#b:03b6bcdb}

/s/ Darius Pelletier {#b:fa6574b8}
