# Witness Statement — Reuben Tuvang

**Witness:** Reuben Tuvang, milk-house operator, Cloverdyke Dairy Cooperative
**Taken:** April 8, 2026, at the co-op receiving station near Danforth, Voss County, Meridian
**Form:** Signed first-person statement

---

My name is Reuben Tuvang. I have worked the milk-house floor at Cloverdyke for a little over nine years. My job is to receive the loads coming in from our member farms, watch the bulk tank, run the wash cycles, and keep the logs. If the milk isn't cold, that's the first thing I'm supposed to catch, so I pay attention to the tank temperature all shift.

The new Brandt unit went in the middle of October. I wasn't the one who dealt with the sales side or the contract — that's Nils. But I was there when they installed it and I'm the one who ran it day to day after that.

The first failure was November 3rd. I came in early, and the high-temperature alarm was already sounding. The tank had climbed way up — it felt warm through the wall of the tank, which it never should. I checked my thermometer and it was up around fifty-five degrees. There was nothing to do at that point. That milk was already spoiled. We had better than six thousand pounds in there and it all had to go down the drain. I called Nils right away and he called Brandt.

The second time was November 24th. That one the compressor just shut off. I didn't hear an alarm right at first the way I did the first time, and by the time I saw the temperature moving the load was already past saving. That was another big loss, close to what we lost the first time.

The third one, December 12th, was over a weekend. Weekends we don't have somebody standing in the milk-house the whole time. The temperature drifted up slow, and when we got to it Monday the milk was downgraded. Larkspur wouldn't take it.

I want to be clear about the logs. The unit has its own controller that's supposed to record temperatures, but I also keep my own log by hand, every shift, on paper. I've done that for years, before this unit and after. My handwritten log shows the date and time of all three of these. I trust my own log because I write it down myself.

When Darius, the Brandt technician, came out, I heard him say the condenser coils were dirty and he said something about the power coming in maybe not being steady. I'm not an electrician so I can't tell you if he was right about the power. I clean the coils when I'm told to and when I have time. I know there was a stretch in November where we were short-handed and a cleaning got put off, but I don't set that schedule.

Whether the machine kept its own temperature history in its memory or wrote over it, I honestly don't know. That's not something I work with. I just know what I saw and what I wrote down.

/s/ Reuben Tuvang
