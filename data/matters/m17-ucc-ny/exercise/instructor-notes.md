## What this exercise is for {#b:f646a74a}

This is the second UCC Article 2 matter in the corpus (the first, m07, is a Meridian warranty/limited-remedy dispute). m17 deliberately shifts the doctrinal center of gravity from warranty-and-remedy to **conformity, rejection, acceptance, cure, and the seller's price-versus-damages remedy**, and it flips the posture: here the firm's client is the **buyer defending a filed suit for the price**, not the buyer bringing a claim. The teaching aim is to make students reason load-by-load rather than treating three deliveries as one event. {#b:2f77abf6}

## Governing law (instructor reference only) {#b:d3d6b174}

New York has enacted UCC Article 2 as N.Y. U.C.C. Law (McKinney). The provisions most in play: {#b:1d339e75}

- **Conformity / perfect-tender framing:** N.Y. U.C.C. Law § 2-601 (buyer's rights on improper delivery); § 2-106 (conforming goods). {#b:819e8294}
- **Rejection:** § 2-602 (manner and effect of rightful rejection — within a reasonable time, with seasonable notice); § 2-605 (waiver of unstated defects); § 2-607 (effect of acceptance; burden shifts to buyer). {#b:6152dfe6}
- **Acceptance:** § 2-606 (what constitutes acceptance — including an act inconsistent with the seller's ownership, i.e., resale); § 2-607(1) (buyer must pay at the contract rate for accepted goods). {#b:b639792e}
- **Commercial unit / partial acceptance:** § 2-105(6) and § 2-601(c). {#b:0bc9964b}
- **Cure:** § 2-508 (seller's right to cure). {#b:01ace62f}
- **Seller's remedies:** § 2-703 (seller's remedies generally); § 2-709 (action for the **price** — available only for accepted goods, or goods not reasonably resalable); § 2-706 (resale) and § 2-708 (damages) as the alternatives when a price action does not lie. {#b:d5aac66a}
- **Warranties:** § 2-313 (express warranty — the specifications and approved artwork); § 2-314 (implied warranty of merchantability, merchant seller). {#b:fd099aa3}

Procedure: filed in the Supreme Court, Commercial Division (see the naming caution in the jurisdiction file — the Supreme Court is the *trial* court in New York). N.Y. C.P.L.R. governs the pleadings and discovery. {#b:e65a7563}

## The load-by-load spine (ground truth in facts.md) {#b:2bbfb1b2}

- **Load A** — conforming, accepted, paid. Baseline that shows Empire is not a reflexive rejecter. {#b:01613871}
- **Load B** — genuine allergen-labeling nonconformity (missing tree-nut warning vs. approved artwork); rejected same day. Strongest rejection for Empire. Ostervold's own QA log ([m17.fact.040]) corroborates a label-template changeover. {#b:01d8a59c}
- **Load C** — genuine short-dating (~5 months vs. 9-month spec), but rejected only after ~8 days ([m17.fact.016], [m17.fact.036]). Timeliness is the live issue; carrier records ([m17.fact.035]) and the production backlog ([m17.fact.041]) undercut Ostervold's transit theory on causation, but the delay still threatens the rejection. {#b:fbe17ee0}
- **Load D** — mixed; the partial resale ([m17.fact.020], [m17.fact.037]) is the acceptance-by-resale trap. Even if the rest was rightfully rejected, the resold ~1/3 likely = acceptance → price owed for that portion. {#b:0de2110f}

## Interview design {#b:1be00c36}

Beltran readily gives the defects (they help her). The two rapport-gated facts are the ones that hurt: the eight-day delay ([m17.fact.016], gated on `nonjudgmental_response` + `follow_up_on_hint`) and the Load D resale ([m17.fact.020], gated on `confidentiality_reassurance`). The concealed motive — Empire's plan to switch to a cheaper packer ([m17.fact.038]) — should never unlock through pressure; strong students surface it by earning trust and by reading the file. Renfrew (cooperative) volunteers the delay openly and is a good corroboration interview. {#b:564daeb6}

## Professional-responsibility beat {#b:b320a2c8}

Ostervold is represented (Attorney Fessenden). A student who tries to interview Roland Ostervold should trip the Rule 4.2 (N.Y. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2) no-contact teaching moment. Keep client-facing memos facts-and-advice; legal authority belongs in the separate analysis. {#b:db768a8e}

## Discussion prompts {#b:f75f4966}

- Can Empire reject all of Load D, or must it separate conforming from nonconforming commercial units? What does the resale do to that answer? {#b:3c2c3b5d}
- Does the eight-day Load C delay convert rejection into acceptance? Does short-dating that worsens on the dock change the analysis? {#b:b79ddfd9}
- On which loads can Ostervold actually win the **price** under § 2-709, and on which is it limited to § 2-706/§ 2-708 damages? {#b:9c3af761}
- How does the refused cure offer cut for each side? {#b:0c0dcdb5}
