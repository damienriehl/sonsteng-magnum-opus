# Ground-Truth Facts — In re Petition for Disciplinary Action against Delphine A. Karsgaard

*Master narrative for the Practicum. This is the single source of truth; every persona, exhibit, and rubric anchor resolves to a numbered fact below. It is not a student-facing document.*

## The respondent and her practice

Delphine A. Karsgaard is a Minnesota attorney who has practiced for nineteen years and, until these events, had never been the subject of a disciplinary complaint [m12.fact.001]. She runs a two-lawyer general practice, Karsgaard Law Office, in a leased suite on the north side of the metro, handling personal-injury, small-business, and probate matters [m12.fact.002]. Her only associate is Jonas Emberly, admitted three years ago, and until the summer of 2024 her only support staff was a full-time paralegal, Marnie Delacroix, who ran the firm's calendar, intake, and filing [m12.fact.003].

The firm never adopted a shared, centralized docketing system; instead, Delacroix kept the firm's litigation deadlines in her own task-list application on her workstation, and the lawyers relied on her to remind them [m12.fact.004]. Karsgaard knew deadlines lived in Delacroix's personal task list rather than on the shared office calendar and had trusted that arrangement for years without incident [m12.fact.005].

## The underlying Dieterle matter

On January 20, 2024, Marguerite Dieterle slipped on an unmarked wet-floor area near the produce coolers at a Larkspur Market grocery store and fractured her wrist and elbow, requiring surgery and months of physical therapy [m12.fact.006]. Dieterle retained Karsgaard on February 8, 2024, to pursue a premises-liability claim against the store's operator, Larkspur Market Holdings, LLC [m12.fact.007]. Karsgaard evaluated the claim as meritorious and worth pursuing [m12.fact.008].

Karsgaard prepared a summons and complaint and had them filed on May 15, 2024 [m12.fact.009]. Under the applicable procedural timeline, the summons and complaint had to be served on the defendant within a fixed period, and the deadline to complete service and file proof of it fell on August 13, 2024 [m12.fact.010]. Delacroix calendared that August 13 service deadline in her personal task list [m12.fact.011].

## The paralegal's departure and the missed deadline

Delacroix gave notice in mid-June and left the firm on June 30, 2024, to move out of state for family reasons [m12.fact.012]. In the handoff, Delacroix exported her intake and billing files but did not migrate her personal deadline task list to anyone else, and no one at the firm re-created the Dieterle service deadline on the shared calendar [m12.fact.013]. Karsgaard did not hire a replacement paralegal for several months and covered the docket herself with Emberly during that stretch [m12.fact.014].

The August 13, 2024 service deadline passed with the summons and complaint never served on Larkspur Market Holdings [m12.fact.015]. Because the deadline was blown, and because the limitations period on Dieterle's claim expired soon after, the practical effect was that Dieterle's premises-liability claim was lost [m12.fact.016].

## How Karsgaard learned, and when

On September 18, 2024, opposing counsel for Larkspur Market Holdings, attorney Odalys Prentice, emailed Karsgaard stating that no service had been effected and that the time to serve had expired, and signaling she would seek dismissal [m12.fact.017]. Karsgaard opened and read that September 18 email at the time it arrived [m12.fact.018]. Overwhelmed and behind, Karsgaard told herself the email was merely adversarial posturing, did not act on it, and did not calendar or investigate the service status [m12.fact.019].

On October 2, 2024, the district court, on Prentice's motion, dismissed the Dieterle action, and the dismissal order was served on Karsgaard electronically that day [m12.fact.020]. In October 2024, before Dieterle understood anything was wrong, Karsgaard told Dieterle by phone that the case was "on track" and progressing, when Karsgaard already knew service had been blown and the action dismissed [m12.fact.021]. Karsgaard has since acknowledged privately that this reassurance to her client was false and made to buy time [m12.fact.022].

Dieterle first learned there was a serious problem on November 26, 2024, when she called the office to ask about a settlement and Karsgaard finally told her the claim had been dismissed [m12.fact.023].

## The motion to vacate and the candor allegation

On December 10, 2024, Karsgaard filed a motion to vacate the dismissal in the underlying case, seeking to reopen the Dieterle claim [m12.fact.024]. In a sworn affidavit supporting that motion, Karsgaard stated that she "first became aware of the failure of service on November 26, 2024" [m12.fact.025]. That statement was inaccurate: Karsgaard had in fact received actual notice of the service failure no later than September 18, 2024, and had received the dismissal order on October 2, 2024 [m12.fact.026].

Karsgaard maintains that the November 26 date in her affidavit was a good-faith reconstruction rather than an intentional misrepresentation — that she conflated the date she "fully grasped" the consequences (the November 26 client call) with the date she "first became aware," and that she drafted the affidavit hurriedly from memory without pulling her email [m12.fact.027]. The Director's position is that the affidavit misstated a material fact to the tribunal and that Karsgaard knew or should have known it was false when she signed it [m12.fact.028].

The district court denied the motion to vacate on February 5, 2025, and the Dieterle claim remained dismissed [m12.fact.029].

## The disciplinary proceeding

Dieterle filed an ethics complaint with the Minnesota Office of Lawyers Professional Responsibility on March 10, 2025 [m12.fact.030]. After investigation and district ethics committee review, the Director's office issued formal charges on August 20, 2025 [m12.fact.031]. The Director filed a Petition for Disciplinary Action, captioned in the Minnesota Supreme Court, on November 14, 2025 [m12.fact.032]. Senior Assistant Director Gideon Voss is prosecuting the petition for the Director's office [m12.fact.033].

The petition alleges three clusters of misconduct: neglect of the Dieterle matter and failure to act with diligence; a candor violation arising from the affidavit's inaccurate date; and failure to make reasonable efforts to ensure the firm's non-lawyer staff and systems adequately safeguarded client deadlines [m12.fact.034]. A referee, retired district judge Hon. Miriam Kessler, has been appointed to hold the hearing and make findings and a sanction recommendation for the Supreme Court [m12.fact.035].

## The defense and mitigation

Karsgaard retained Ellingboe & Ravndal LLP on December 1, 2025, to defend her and to argue for a proportional, lesser sanction [m12.fact.036]. The defense concedes the neglect but frames it as a systems-and-supervision failure triggered by an abrupt staffing loss rather than willful indifference, and it contests the candor charge as an honest reconstruction error rather than a knowing false statement [m12.fact.037]. In mitigation, the defense points to nineteen years of discipline-free practice, Karsgaard's cooperation with the investigation, and remedial steps: she has installed a centralized cloud docketing system with redundant deadline reminders and has had no further calendaring failures [m12.fact.038].

There are aggravating facts the defense must confront: Karsgaard read the September 18 warning and did nothing, and she falsely reassured her client in October that the case was on track [m12.fact.039]. During the relevant autumn of 2024, Karsgaard was also caring for her terminally ill father and running the practice short-staffed and exhausted — circumstances she is reluctant to raise, viewing them as an excuse rather than a defense [m12.fact.040]. Dieterle has since retained separate malpractice counsel, attorney Sunila Achterberg, to pursue a civil claim against Karsgaard, so Dieterle is a represented adverse party in a related matter [m12.fact.041]. Whether Dieterle's underlying premises claim can be revived by any means is unresolved and is not something Karsgaard can answer [m12.fact.042].

## The posture of the defense engagement

As of the simulation date, the disciplinary matter sits at the pre-hearing stage: the Answer to the Petition has been filed, the referee has held a scheduling conference, and the parties are preparing pre-hearing briefs and witnesses before the evidentiary hearing before Referee Kessler [m12.fact.043]. The central strategic questions for the defense are how to present the neglect as a supervisory-systems failure without appearing to disclaim responsibility, whether and how to contest the candor charge, and how much of Karsgaard's personal circumstances to put before the referee in mitigation [m12.fact.044]. Emberly, the associate, is expected to testify about the firm's reliance on Delacroix's personal task list and the gap that opened after her departure, and Delacroix has provided an affidavit describing the calendar handoff [m12.fact.045]. The Director is expected to argue that the neglect and the candor issue together warrant a suspension, while the defense will argue for a lesser sanction such as a public reprimand with probation and conditions [m12.fact.046].
