# Instructor Notes — m12 (Karsgaard Discipline) {#b:10fa7f4a}

*Teaching notes only. Not for student distribution. Citations below are instructor-verified at publish against the official Minnesota sources; verify currency before each term.* {#b:978a6a83}

## What this exercise is really testing {#b:a2fdbf4b}

The pedagogical core is the split between **neglect** (largely conceded, ordinarily lower on the sanction ladder) and **candor to the tribunal** (dishonesty, treated far more severely). Strong students will see that winning the candor charge — recharacterizing the affidavit date as a careless reconstruction rather than a knowing false statement — is where the case is actually won or lost, and will confront that the interview facts (client read the September email; client told the client the case was "on track" after dismissal) make the "good-faith" theory much harder. The best work does not hide from those facts; it counsels the client and frames the sanction argument honestly around them. {#b:67489f4f}

## Key authorities for instructor reference (verify before citing) {#b:0d837bba}

- **Diligence / neglect:** Minn. R. Prof. Conduct 1.3; communication duties under Minn. R. Prof. Conduct 1.4. {#b:898120e8}
- **Candor / dishonesty:** Minn. R. Prof. Conduct 3.3(a)(1) (false statement of fact to a tribunal); 8.4(c) (conduct involving dishonesty, fraud, deceit, or misrepresentation); 4.1. {#b:73f9c0d2}
- **Supervision of non-lawyers:** Minn. R. Prof. Conduct 5.3. {#b:d8f5aeea}
- **No-contact rule (the Dieterle teaching moment):** Minn. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2. {#b:201de5ca}
- **Procedure:** Rules on Lawyers Professional Responsibility (RLPR) — Director's petition, referee appointment and findings, Supreme Court review. {#b:e93ef8ef}
- **Sanction framework:** Minnesota's four-factor analysis (nature of misconduct, cumulative weight, harm to public and profession, aggravating/mitigating factors); referee findings are given deference where a transcript is ordered; the Court determines the appropriate discipline independently. Point students to current Minnesota Supreme Court discipline opinions for comparable neglect-plus-misrepresentation outcomes (ranging from public reprimand with probation to suspension). {#b:8e75e1d0}

## Teaching arc (suggested sequence) {#b:395fd81f}

- **Session 1 — Client interview (the distressed respondent).** Students interview Karsgaard. The objective is to earn the two rapport-gated facts (the read September email; the dying-father autumn) through confidentiality reassurance and non-judgment — not pressure. Debrief on how their reaction to the shameful facts either unlocked or shut down the truth. {#b:4672db15}
- **Session 2 — The candor reckoning.** Once students learn she read the September email, walk them into counsel's own Rule 3.3 duty: you cannot argue the affidavit was true. Reframe from innocence to mitigation. This is the intellectual and ethical peak. {#b:833cdf18}
- **Session 3 — Witness & the 4.2 boundary.** Emberly (free to interview) corroborates the systems failure and the exhaustion; Dieterle triggers the Rule 4.2 flag. Build the record-anchored timeline. {#b:2f25c3ec}
- **Session 4 — Brief & sanction.** Draft the pre-hearing brief with the correct standard/burden and a four-factor proportionality argument grounded in comparable outcomes. {#b:a27691a7}
- **Session 5 — Oral argument.** Argue before the referee; field hard candor and sanction questions without evasion. {#b:25057dfb}
