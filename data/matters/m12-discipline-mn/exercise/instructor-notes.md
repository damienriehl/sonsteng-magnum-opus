# Instructor Notes — m12 (Karsgaard Discipline)

*Teaching notes only. Not for student distribution. Citations below are instructor-verified at publish against the official Minnesota sources; verify currency before each term.*

## What this exercise is really testing

The pedagogical core is the split between **neglect** (largely conceded, ordinarily lower on the sanction ladder) and **candor to the tribunal** (dishonesty, treated far more severely). Strong students will see that winning the candor charge — recharacterizing the affidavit date as a careless reconstruction rather than a knowing false statement — is where the case is actually won or lost, and will confront that the interview facts (client read the September email; client told the client the case was "on track" after dismissal) make the "good-faith" theory much harder. The best work does not hide from those facts; it counsels the client and frames the sanction argument honestly around them.

## Key authorities for instructor reference (verify before citing)

- **Diligence / neglect:** Minn. R. Prof. Conduct 1.3; communication duties under Minn. R. Prof. Conduct 1.4.
- **Candor / dishonesty:** Minn. R. Prof. Conduct 3.3(a)(1) (false statement of fact to a tribunal); 8.4(c) (conduct involving dishonesty, fraud, deceit, or misrepresentation); 4.1.
- **Supervision of non-lawyers:** Minn. R. Prof. Conduct 5.3.
- **No-contact rule (the Dieterle teaching moment):** Minn. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2.
- **Procedure:** Rules on Lawyers Professional Responsibility (RLPR) — Director's petition, referee appointment and findings, Supreme Court review.
- **Sanction framework:** Minnesota's four-factor analysis (nature of misconduct, cumulative weight, harm to public and profession, aggravating/mitigating factors); referee findings are given deference where a transcript is ordered; the Court determines the appropriate discipline independently. Point students to current Minnesota Supreme Court discipline opinions for comparable neglect-plus-misrepresentation outcomes (ranging from public reprimand with probation to suspension).

## Discussion prompts

- Is a "reconstruction from memory" a defense to a 3.3/8.4(c) charge, or only mitigation of the sanction?
- How should counsel advise a client who wants to stand on the affidavit? What are counsel's own 3.3 obligations here?
- How much of the dying-parent mitigation is fair to put before the referee, and how?
