# Ground-Truth Facts — In re the Marriage of Camille Beaumont and Andre Lassiter

## The family

Camille Beaumont and Andre Lassiter were married in Oakland, California, on April 21, 2018 [m19.fact.001]. At the time of the engagement with the firm they had been married just under eight years, which both describe as "an eight-year marriage" [m19.fact.002]. They have one child: Noémie Lassiter, born August 14, 2019, age six [m19.fact.003]. The child's surname is Lassiter [m19.fact.004].

Camille Beaumont is thirty-nine [m19.fact.005]. She is the front-of-house owner and general manager of the couple's restaurant, where she runs the dining room, manages the staff, handles reservations and vendor accounts, and keeps the day-to-day books [m19.fact.006]. She takes an owner's draw of roughly sixty-two thousand dollars a year and has no employer-provided benefits outside the business [m19.fact.007]. Her hours are long but front-loaded into evenings and weekends, the restaurant's busiest service [m19.fact.008].

Andre Lassiter is forty-two [m19.fact.009]. He is the executive chef and co-owner; he designs the menu, runs the kitchen, and is the public face and reputation of the restaurant [m19.fact.010]. He works nights and is rarely home before eleven on a service day [m19.fact.011]. Andre trained in New Orleans before moving to California, and his cooking is the restaurant's identity [m19.fact.012].

## The restaurant

The restaurant, Petite Marigny, is a Creole bistro in the Temescal neighborhood of Oakland that the couple opened together in October 2019, a year and a half into the marriage [m19.fact.013]. It has thirty-eight seats, a full liquor license, and a loyal neighborhood following; it was written up favorably in local press in 2023 and 2024 [m19.fact.014]. On paper the business is a two-member LLC owned equally by Camille and Andre [m19.fact.015].

The seed money for the buildout is the central dispute. In 2016, before the marriage, Andre inherited about eighty thousand dollars from his grandmother, Odette Lassiter, and kept it in a separate credit-union account in his name alone [m19.fact.016]. When the couple built out the Temescal space in 2019, Andre transferred that inheritance money into the restaurant buildout — the kitchen equipment, the bar, and the leasehold improvements [m19.fact.017]. Andre insists the eighty thousand dollars is his separate property and that he can trace it, and he wants that contribution credited back to him before the business is divided [m19.fact.018]. Camille responds that the money went into a jointly owned, jointly built business years ago, that it was commingled with their shared labor and community funds, and that Andre has never actually produced clean records tracing the separate account into specific improvements [m19.fact.019].

The two also disagree about what the restaurant is worth. Camille believes Petite Marigny is a genuine going concern with real value — the license, the equipment, the lease, the name, the reviews, and a steady book of business — and that it should be valued and divided as a community asset [m19.fact.020]. Andre maintains the restaurant "is my hands and my name," that its reputation walks out the door with him, and that without him in the kitchen it is a used stove and a lease nobody wants [m19.fact.021]. The restaurant's tax returns show net income between roughly forty thousand and ninety thousand dollars over the last three years, with the strongest year in 2024 [m19.fact.022].

Marguerite Tessier has managed the floor and kept the supplementary books at Petite Marigny since 2021 and knows the operation intimately [m19.fact.023]. The couple's outside accountant prepared the LLC returns, and the parties have jointly retained a valuation analyst, Daniel Ferrante, to appraise the business [m19.fact.024].

## The home and other property

During the marriage the couple bought a two-bedroom bungalow on Chabot Terrace in Oakland in 2021, which they still own; the current mortgage balance is about three hundred forty thousand dollars against an estimated value near six hundred twenty thousand dollars [m19.fact.025]. The down payment came from their joint savings during the marriage, and both agree the house itself is a community asset; the fight there is only over who keeps it [m19.fact.026]. Andre had a modest retirement account from a restaurant job he held before the marriage, the premarital balance of which is disputed and undocumented in the file [m19.fact.027].

## The child's routine

For most of Noémie's life Camille has been the more available parent in the daytime and morning hours, doing the school drop-offs, the pediatrician visits, and the bedtime routine, because Andre's kitchen hours keep him out most evenings [m19.fact.028]. Noémie is in first grade at a public elementary school near the Chabot Terrace house and attends an after-care program until Camille finishes the dinner-service prep and hands the floor to Marguerite [m19.fact.029]. Noémie takes a Saturday-morning dance class and is close to her godmother, Yvette Larue, who often helps with childcare [m19.fact.030].

Andre is a loving and involved father who adores Noémie, but his availability is genuinely constrained by the restaurant's night-and-weekend hours [m19.fact.031]. Both parents agree Noémie loves them both; the dispute is about a workable schedule, not either parent's basic fitness [m19.fact.032]. Camille seeks primary physical custody with a predictable schedule and joint legal custody; Andre seeks substantial and roughly equal parenting time and argues he can restructure his hours to be present [m19.fact.033].

## How the marriage broke down

By 2024 the marriage was strained by money pressure, exhaustion, and the fact that the couple worked opposite ends of the same restaurant and rarely saw each other off the clock [m19.fact.034]. They attended five sessions of couples counseling in the fall of 2024, which did not resolve their conflicts [m19.fact.035]. Over the same period Andre's drinking increased; the demands of running a kitchen and a nightly late finish had, in Camille's account, tipped into a pattern of drinking heavily after service [m19.fact.036].

On a Saturday in June 2025, Andre was supposed to pick Noémie up from a classmate's birthday party while Camille worked the dinner rush [m19.fact.037]. He arrived nearly an hour late, and the host parent told Camille afterward that Andre had smelled of alcohol and seemed unsteady; Camille arranged for Yvette to collect Noémie instead and confronted Andre that night [m19.fact.038]. No one was hurt, no police were called, and Andre says he had "a couple of drinks at a colleague's thing" and lost track of time; he disputes that he was unfit to drive [m19.fact.039]. The incident frightened Camille and is a major reason she now worries about unsupervised overnight time [m19.fact.040].

Camille told Andre she wanted a divorce in December 2025 [m19.fact.041]. Andre moved out of the Chabot Terrace house in January 2026 and rented a one-bedroom apartment about fifteen minutes away; the parties have since run an informal, improvised parenting schedule that neither finds satisfactory, with frequent friction over restaurant coverage and pickups [m19.fact.042].

## What each parent now seeks

Camille wants joint legal custody but primary physical custody, a predictable schedule built around the restaurant's service hours, and the ability to keep the Chabot Terrace house so Noémie does not change schools [m19.fact.043]. She wants the restaurant valued fairly and divided as a community asset, and she resists Andre's separate-property reimbursement claim on the eighty thousand dollars [m19.fact.044]. Andre wants joint legal custody, roughly equal parenting time, credit for his separate contribution before any division, and either a buyout that lets him keep the restaurant or a sale in which his contribution is repaid first [m19.fact.045].

## Two sensitive matters Camille holds close

There are two things Camille is reluctant to discuss and tends to minimize. The first is the June 2025 late-pickup incident and the broader pattern of Andre's drinking after service [m19.fact.046]. She is torn: she believes it bears directly on overnight custody, but she does not want to be seen as weaponizing it against a father Noémie loves, and she fears looking vindictive [m19.fact.047].

The second is money. Beginning around the time Andre moved out, and worried she would be cut off from the accounts if things turned adversarial, Camille quietly moved about eighteen thousand dollars out of the couple's joint account and the restaurant's cash deposits into a personal checking account in her name that Andre does not know about [m19.fact.048]. She thinks of it as a safety cushion for herself and Noémie, but she is deeply anxious that it will look like she was hiding marital assets, and she has not disclosed it to anyone [m19.fact.049].
