## What the exercise is really testing

This is a community-property dissolution built around a rapport-gated client interview, a separate-property tracing fight, a chef-driven business valuation, and a professional-responsibility double: Rule 4.2 no-contact plus the client's own fiduciary duty of disclosure. The strongest students will (a) earn the two withheld facts (Andre's drinking / the June pickup, and the funds Camille moved) through empathy rather than pressure, (b) frame the estate correctly under the community-property presumption, and (c) spot that the client's moved funds are a disclosure problem to be cured, not a secret to be kept. The pedagogical center of gravity is the interview: a student who races to the property spreadsheet drafts on an incomplete record and misses the disclosure problem sitting in the client's own conduct.

## Teaching points and where to send research

- **Community property and equal division.** California is a community-property state; property and debt acquired during marriage are presumptively community, divided equally (Fam. Code §§ 760, 2550; separate property § 770). Start students in the California Family Code, Divisions 6 and 7 (see `ca.json`, "Dissolution and community property"). The Chabot Terrace home (agreed community — only who-keeps-it is disputed, fact.026) and the Petite Marigny LLC (opened during the marriage, fact.013/fact.015) are the core community estate.
- **Separate-property characterization, tracing, and reimbursement.** Andre's $80,000 inheritance (received 2016, pre-marriage, fact.016) is separate in origin, but the party asserting a separate interest bears the burden of tracing (See v. See, 64 Cal.2d 778 (1966)), and commingling can defeat it. The file's gaps are deliberate: a single 2016 statement, a 2019 wire of only $52,000, no continuous trail. Send students to research separate-property tracing and the statutory reimbursement right for separate contributions to community property (the Fam. Code § 2640 concept — reimbursement without interest or appreciation, limited to what is documented — instructor-verify current text against leginfo.legislature.ca.gov). The teaching payoff: even a successful trace yields **reimbursement of the traceable amount (~$52k on this record), not a pre-division ownership carve-out**, and the business stays community.
- **Business valuation / goodwill.** The enterprise-vs-personal goodwill split is the crux (In re Marriage of Golden and the California goodwill line). Enterprise goodwill — the license, lease, name, systems, reviews, and clientele Camille and Tessier describe (fact.020, fact.014) — is divisible community value; goodwill personal to Andre is not freely transferable. Research appropriate valuation dates and normalization of owner compensation. The analyst's report (Ferrante) is intentionally preliminary and flags a cash-deposit irregularity (fact.024/fact.054); students should treat the number as unsettled and identify the discovery/expert work still needed.
- **Custody / best interests.** Legal vs. physical custody under the best-interests standard (Fam. Code §§ 3011, 3020, 3040), Family Court Services mediation, and how a parent's alcohol use is addressed **proportionately** — as a targeted overnight-safety concern (structured or stepped-up time, sobriety conditions), not an automatic disqualification. The June 2025 late-pickup (fact.037–fact.040) is the concrete hook. Reward students who prioritize a durable, service-hours-aware schedule over a fight about custody labels, and who keep Noémie central.
- **Spousal support.** Roughly comparable owner draws (~$62k, fact.007) and an eight-year marriage frame the temporary and long-term support questions (Fam. Code §§ 3600, 4320 — instructor-verify).
- **Professional responsibility (two rules).** (1) Andre is represented by Corinne Vasquez; direct contact implicates Cal. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2 — route through counsel. (2) The funds Camille moved implicate each spouse's fiduciary duty of full disclosure of community assets (Fam. Code §§ 721, 1100(e), and the § 2100-series declaration-of-disclosure requirements — instructor-verify). Counsel the client toward disclosure; do not help conceal.

## Facilitation notes

- Andre is a represented adverse party; if petitioner-side students interview him directly, the Rule 4.2 teaching moment fires — use it, do not skip it. His two rapport-gated facts (the June pickup, fact.039; the worsened drinking, fact.036) and his concealed tracing weakness (fact.019) are what a properly noticed deposition and document demand would target — good material for a discovery-plan discussion, not a live interview.
- Marguerite Tessier (over-talker, interviewable by both sides) is the enterprise-goodwill corroboration interview and gives measured, non-weaponized drinking corroboration (fact.036) only on confidentiality reassurance. Yvette Larue is the friend/childcare witness. Point out that a neutral witness available to both sides invites a race — and that Tessier flags impression-vs-record cleanly.

Note the natural bridge from the trust-accounting lesson to the client's own fiduciary-disclosure duty on the moved funds.

## Discussion prompts

- Does Andre's $80,000 claim survive the tracing gaps, and if partly traced, is the remedy ownership or reimbursement? What discovery closes the gap — and who bears the burden?
- Is Petite Marigny "worth nothing without Andre," or does enterprise goodwill (license, lease, name, systems, clientele) carry real divisible value? What does the valuation analyst still need, and what does the cash-deposit irregularity signal?
- How should counsel advise Camille about the ~$18,000 she moved — cure by disclosure in the declarations of disclosure, or something else? What are the consequences of non-disclosure for property, credibility, and custody?
- How do you raise Andre's drinking proportionately, protecting Noémie's overnights without looking like you are weaponizing it against a father she loves?
- Why is prioritizing a durable parenting schedule (and treating the property questions as tradeable) often the better service to this client and this child than winning a custody-label fight?

## Common student misses to probe in debrief

- Racing to draft on an incomplete record without earning the two withheld facts.
- Counseling concealment (or never confronting the duty) on the moved funds — the ethical core of the matter.
- Treating a traced separate contribution as an ownership carve-out rather than a § 2640 reimbursement right.
- Accepting Andre's "worth nothing without me" or Ferrante's preliminary number at face value.
- Contacting Andre directly in violation of Rule 4.2.
- Framing the drinking as an automatic disqualifier (or ignoring it entirely) rather than handling it proportionately.
